Business

The retail & money world

Mostly words you already live every day — here are their exact names so everyone means the same thing.

Commercial model & money

Buy & Sell (Outright)

KDPS buys the stock outright and owns it from day one. No returns; profit is price minus cost (a margin).

In KDPS: the system watches sell-through & dead stock, not a return deadline.

Correction (25-18-10)

KDPS owns the stock, but the brand grants a capped return allowance (≈10% of goods, stretchable). Returns are manual, within a window and within the budget.

In KDPS: the system tracks the allowance budget ("used ₹40k of ₹1L") and the 30/15/7-day window.

SOR (Sale or Return)

The brand owns the stock; KDPS sells on its behalf for a commission. All unsold goes back, no cap. To get more, raise a fresh booking.

In KDPS: nothing is paid at inward — the vendor is paid as each piece sells.

Consignment

Same as SOR — brand-owned, all unsold returns, no cap — but the brand keeps topping up, so no fresh booking each time.

In KDPS: near-identical to SOR downstream; the only difference is the rolling top-up.

Margin

Your profit slice on a sale — selling price minus cost — usually shown as a %.

In KDPS: drops from ~25% to ~18% during EOSS.

Commission

The % KDPS earns on a brand-owned sale (SOR or Consignment), instead of a margin.

Markdown

Deliberately lowering a price (a discount) to clear stock.

Profitability

Whether something makes money after costs. In KDPS it's calculated, never typed in.

Product & identity

SKU (Stock Keeping Unit)

One uniquely sellable, countable item.

In KDPS: SKU = Style × Size × Color. "Air Max 90 · White · Size 8" is its own SKU.

Style

The design itself, before size and colour — e.g. "Nike Air Max 90".

MRP (Maximum Retail Price)

The legal top price printed on the tag in India. You may sell at or below it, never above.

Barcode

The scannable code that links a physical piece to its SKU in the system.

Season

Which selling period an item belongs to. SS26 = Spring/Summer 2026, AW26 = Autumn/Winter.

Collection

A grouping of stock — Core, Fresh, or Clearance.

Dead stock

Items that aren't selling — e.g. 45+ days with zero sales.

Sell-through

How fast stock is selling — the % sold over a period.

Documents & compliance

PT file

Your master list of every item with its price and tax details — the main document a shipment travels with.

In KDPS: every brand sends a different format; the system normalises them all.

Invoice

The vendor's bill listing what they sent and what it cost.

GRN (Goods Received Note)

The record of what physically arrived, checked against the invoice, noting shortages or damage.

In KDPS: becomes a real system object — nothing moves downstream without it.

Reconciliation (3-way match)

Comparing Invoice ↔ GRN ↔ PT to catch anything that doesn't agree.

Credit note

A "the brand owes us" document raised when goods go back, reducing what KDPS must pay.

GST / GSTIN

India's sales tax (GST), and the tax registration number each vendor has (GSTIN).

HSN code

A standard product-category code that decides the GST rate for an item.

Tally

The accounting software that is the official "book of record" for the business.

TIR

Your internal name for the clean, accounting-ready dataset the books expect — not a standard industry term.

Places, people & selling

SBU (Strategic Business Unit)

Any single location — a store, a warehouse, or a franchise.

MBO (Multi-Brand Outlet)

A store selling many brands. These shift stock between each other.

EBO (Exclusive Brand Outlet)

A single-brand store. Its stock is handled brand-to-store directly.

POS (Point of Sale)

The billing system at the counter where a sale is rung up.

EOSS (End of Season Sale)

The big seasonal clearance — Summer ~August, Winter ~February.

SOH (Stock on Hand)

How many units a location currently has.

In KDPS: can briefly go negative when staff sell before inwarding — fixes on next sync.

Inward

Receiving stock into a store by scanning each piece in.

Transfer voucher

The document that moves stock from one SBU to another.

Exchange

A customer swap, valued against the original bill (MRP-to-MRP).

Account manager

The KDPS person who manages the relationship with a brand.

🟧 The one business choice that controls everything

The commercial model — one of four (Buy & Sell, Correction, SOR, Consignment). It decides who owns the stock, whether & how much can be returned, margin vs commission, and when the vendor gets paid. Set it right on the brand and most other rules fall out of it automatically.

Technical

The software world

The words engineers use. For each, the plain idea — and the moment in your workflow where it already shows up.

The data core

Database

The organised store that holds all the system's data.

PostgreSQL (Postgres)

A powerful, dependable open-source database — comfortable with millions of rows.

In KDPS: 20K SKUs × 50 stores is a lot of rows; this is built for it.

Table / row

Like a spreadsheet: a table has columns; each record is a row.

Ledger

An add-only list of movements, like a bank statement. You never edit it; the balance is the sum of entries.

In KDPS: the heart of stock — every +in / −out recorded forever.

Append-only / immutable

You can only add new records; existing ones are never changed. That's what gives a perfect audit trail.

Event sourcing

The grown-up name for the ledger idea: store every event, rebuild the current state by replaying them.

Source of truth

The one place that's authoritative; everything else is derived from it.

Running balance

A pre-saved current total so the system doesn't re-add the whole ledger every time you ask "how many?"

Reliability

ACID

A database's promise to keep data correct even if it crashes mid-save — saves are all-or-nothing.

In KDPS: a sale can't deduct stock but fail to record the bill, or vice-versa.

Transaction

A bundle of changes that all succeed together or all fail together — never half-done.

Audit trail

A tamper-evident record of who did what, when, and why.

Idempotent

Safe to repeat: running the same action twice has the same result as once.

In KDPS: if the POS sync runs twice, stock isn't deducted twice.

Architecture & flow

Spine / core

The robust centre — the ledgers and masters — that must never be wrong.

Edge

The outer, helpful-but-fallible parts — the AI features.

Rule engine

Plain "if-this-then-that" logic in config, not AI — predictable and versionable.

In KDPS: "if SOR and 30 days to deadline → alert." Always the same answer.

Decoupling

Letting parts run independently so one waiting doesn't block another.

In KDPS: receive goods (GRN) without waiting for the PT file to arrive.

Asynchronous (async)

Things happen on their own timeline and reconcile later, not in lockstep.

Event-driven

Parts react to events ("invoice uploaded") instead of being called in a fixed order.

Queue / job

A background to-do list the system works through — e.g. "read this invoice".

Confidence score

How sure the AI is (0–100%). Used to decide: auto-accept, or ask a human.

Draft / pending state

AI output parked for approval before it's allowed to become real.

AI & interfaces

LLM

A large language model (like Claude) that understands and writes text.

Vision

AI that reads images and PDFs into structured data.

In KDPS: turns a photo of an invoice or tag into clean rows.

Forecasting

Predicting future demand from past sales.

In KDPS: at the size level — which sizes to send each store.

Model

A trained engine that makes a prediction or classification.

Schema

The defined shape of data — which columns exist and what type each is.

Normalization

Turning many messy formats into one standard shape.

In KDPS: every brand's PT file → one KDPS format.

Taxonomy

The agreed category tree everything is filed under.

API

The doorway one system uses to talk to another.

Sync

Bringing two systems' data into agreement.

In KDPS: reading each store's POS export every 15 minutes.

PWA (Progressive Web App)

A website that behaves like a phone app — camera, barcode, no app-store install.

In KDPS: runs on cheap store Androids straight from the browser.

Dashboard

Visual screens of numbers you can drill into.

Webhook

An automatic "ping" one system sends another the moment something happens.

🟦 The one technical word that makes it robust

Ledger, not count. Never store "size 8 = 3". Store the list of movements and add them up. You get a full audit trail, self-healing stock, and something that scales to millions of rows — for free.
