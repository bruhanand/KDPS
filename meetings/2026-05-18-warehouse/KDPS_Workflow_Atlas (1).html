§ 01

The Two Realities of Inbound Stock

Before any workflow can be understood, one fact must be held in mind: KDPS operates two entirely different inbound supply chains at the same time, with different documents, different vendors, different routes, and different headaches.

Why this matters
Every other workflow on this page branches off this distinction. The same operations team, the same office, the same WhatsApp groups handle both paths — but the work required is fundamentally different. Branded is pattern-matching against a known master. Non-branded is interpretation from incomplete data.
§ 02 · Priority #1 (stated by client)

What the human actually does

The operations lead opens the master PT file. They search for the invoice reference number — for example, 3219 — which appears on the invoice. That same reference appears as a row identifier in the master. They copy the relevant data into the KDPS internal format. This is pattern matching, not creative work. It could be done by software that has been shown the two files.

The other three people in the chain perform verification, status-update, and approval steps. None of them re-interpret the invoice. They check that the previous person did their job correctly.

Pain · stated by operations lead
"PT file ek funnel ki tarah ho gaya hai." The PT file process has become a funnel. Four people working heavily. If any one of them is missing, the entire work stops. Stores are sending requests on a daily basis throughout the day.
Pain · WhatsApp as primary tool
The operations team works on PCs but the tool is WhatsApp. WhatsApp Web frequently shows "could not find message" for older files. To match a barcode against an older invoice, someone has to scroll backwards through weeks of conversation. The folder structure on disk maintains separate folders per season per store, just so pending files can be located.
The invoice reference number → master PT row lookup is one of the cleanest cases of automation-ready work observed during the visit. The brand has already done the structuring; KDPS is doing the lookup by hand.

The specific friction of this path

The invoice arrives and tells KDPS almost nothing. For Radha Krishna Silk, a saree vendor, the invoice contained only HSN, GST, quantity, and rate. Everything else — what the item actually is, what colour, what size, what it should be sold for — comes from elsewhere.

Speaker 1 described the workflow plainly: he calls the vendor. He asks what the style codes represent. He takes notes. Then he constructs the PT file from those notes plus the invoice.

Sarees in particular resist differentiation. Free size. Many colours, no practical way to encode every variation in the SKU. The compromise: one barcode per item type, sized variants where applicable, colour treated as a non-modeled attribute.

Pain · same barcode, different MRPs
A subtle constraint that the system must honour: a barcode generated for a previous lot of a non-branded item may be reused for a new lot at a different MRP. Software that assumes barcode → price is a one-to-one mapping will break here.
Insight · margins are not gut-based
KDPS applies a flat 1.1% transport cost to base rate. On top of that, branded items earn approximately 25% margin and non-branded items earn 30-35%. These are documented internal rates. Variations on these rates are driven by demand history on repeat purchases — when a style sold well last cycle, it earns slightly less margin headroom because it must be procured competitively.

The fix the client articulated
Quality enforcement should happen at capture time, not at processing time. The handheld device used at the store should refuse to upload an unreadable photo. The phrase the client used: "Data is not clear, redo it." The capture device should also auto-convert to PDF, auto-rename the file using a consistent scheme (Invoice + Brand), and end the practice of maintaining one folder per store per season just to find pending files.

The agent as institution

Each major sourcing region — Mumbai, Kolkata, Indore, Raipur, Bangalore — has one dedicated agent. The agent is not a KDPS employee. He is a guarantor on both sides. He guarantees to the vendor that KDPS will pay within the 60-day cycle. He guarantees to KDPS that the vendor will dispatch on schedule. His compensation is a 1-2% commission negotiated with the vendor side.

The agent persists across vendor changes. If KDPS stops buying from one factory in Mumbai and starts buying from another, the same Mumbai agent typically handles the new relationship. This is institutional memory that lives entirely in the agent's head.

Constraint · upstream is unstructured
Any future system that tries to reconcile vendor performance, predict supply, or audit payment cycles is reaching into territory that today has no digital footprint. The agent's commitments, the verbal bookings, the schedule expectations — none of it is captured in any tool. The arrival of an invoice is the first structured event in this entire chain.

The retroactive offer application

One detail buried in the workflow deserves attention. When a salesman at a store fails to apply an eligible brand discount during a sale, KDPS still claims the brand-funded portion of that discount in the commission report. They apply the offer retroactively on paper and report it as if it had been applied at billing time. This is a known practice on the KDPS side. Whether the brand audits this is a separate question.

Constraint · the deadline is non-negotiable
The submission deadline of 4th-5th is determined by the brand, not by KDPS preference. If KDPS misses it, the brand delays the commission disbursement to the 7th or beyond. If commission is delayed, KDPS payroll is delayed. The reporting cycle is therefore the most rigid and least flexible workflow in the entire operation.

Pain · no consolidated view
To compile a network-wide report, the operations lead logs into multiple separate POS store accounts one by one, retrieves the export from each, and stitches the results together by hand. There is no global dashboard. Adding a store means adding another login to the rotation.
Pain · the barcode price collision
A consequence of reusing barcodes for new lots of non-branded items at different MRPs. The POS frequently encounters a scanned barcode for which it cannot determine which MRP applies. The displayed error is "Stock not available." Staff perform a manual billing workaround to serve the waiting customer, and the back office reconciles the entry afterwards.

EBO vs MBO clarification

Exclusive Brand Outlets (EBOs) — single-brand stores — were explicitly placed outside the scope of any KDPS system intervention. The brand provides its own software for those locations. KDPS's pain, and therefore the system's responsibility, lies entirely with Multi-Brand Outlets (MBOs) and direct stores. The Deoghar store, branded as KDPS Lifestyle, falls inside scope.

§ 08 · Leakage

The Discount Application Workflow

The fix as articulated by the client
Hardcode the discount rules at the barcode × brand × period level. The POS should refuse to ring up a Van Heusen jacket at any discount other than the brand-funded one currently in force. The salesman should lose the ability to enter a custom percentage. The quote from the client: "There are many such small loopholes in the system where there is a source of earning. We have to choke all of that, close it."

The Roles in the Operation

A flat list of the roles involved in the workflows above, what they do today, and where they appear. Names omitted intentionally — the document describes structure, not individuals.

Role Context What they actually do
Operations Lead Head office · Ranchi Generates PT files from master + invoice. Owns the master PT file. First point of contact for incoming store requests on WhatsApp. Compiles network-wide reports during the 1st-7th cycle.
Verifier Head office · WFH during crunch Reviews PT files generated by the operations lead for parameter correctness. Checks GST, margin application, MRP. Approves before inwarding.
Inwarder Head office Marks approved PT files as inwarded in the system. Status-update role within the chain.
Approver Head office · billing trigger Final OK on PT files. Triggers the billing event to stores. Also handles manual data entry of multi-item Tally invoices that arrive without standardised data.

Store Managers Per location Scan barcodes to officially receive goods. Post daily WhatsApp reports per store. Forget to take reports approximately 2-3 times per month, requiring ad-hoc generation by head office.
§ 10 · Reference

Structural Constraints

Facts about the operation that any future intervention must accommodate. Not opinions, not pain points — constraints that exist independent of which solution is built.

  • The same barcode can carry different MRPs across lots, especially for non-branded items. A system cannot assume barcode is the unique price key.
  • Bookings are verbal upstream. There is no paper trail before the invoice arrives. The invoice is the first structured event in the supply chain as far as KDPS digital systems are concerned.
  • Regional agents are external parties holding institutional memory that does not exist in any system. They are the only enforcement layer on payment cycles.
  • Branded item invoices follow the Madura group format more or less consistently. About 70% of stock arrives this way.
  • Non-branded vendors provide minimal data on their invoices. About 30% of stock arrives this way, almost all of it routed through the warehouse rather than direct to stores.
  • The brand-side master PT file arrives every 15 days. Special-case single-shipment PT files can be requested but consolidating to the 15-day rhythm is the operational preference.
  • Exclusive Brand Outlets are outside KDPS system scope. The brand provides software there.
  • The POS system in use is 5-7 years old. It exposes per-store login portals only, with no consolidated view across the network.
  • Brand commission disbursement is gated on report submission by the 4th-5th of each month. Employee payroll is gated on commission disbursement. The cycle is non-negotiable from KDPS's side.
  • EBO format and MBO format differ. Sanskar (Ratu Road) is an example MBO with floor-level segregation by gender. Deoghar operates under the KDPS Lifestyle identity.
  • Style codes embed sub-brand identity (e.g., AL/AS = Allen Solly male, AW = female, AK = kids). Human operators currently parse these.

§ 12 · Gaps

What This Visit Did Not Cover

An honest enumeration of operational territory still unmapped. These items should drive the agenda for the upcoming store visit and any subsequent head-office conversations.

  • The point of sale itself — customer interactions, salesman billing behaviour, queue dynamics at the counter, return processing at stores.
  • The store-side handling of incoming PT files. What happens after the approver bills the store? How does the store inward goods? Who scans what?
  • Tally configuration. The version in use, how vouchers are currently exported or entered, what fields are populated.
  • The brand-specific report templates. Each brand expects a particular format for the monthly commission submission. None of these were collected.
  • Agent-side workflow. Whether agents currently interact with any KDPS digital system, or operate entirely off-system through phone and WhatsApp.
  • The full vendor list. The 70%-branded / 30%-non-branded split was stated; the underlying vendor population was not enumerated.
  • Brand-specific discount rule complexity. The number of currently active rules per brand × category × season is unknown.
  • Attendance recording today. How attendance feeds into the incentive sheets generated during the 1st-7th crunch is unmapped.
  • Defect handling. Speaker 2 mentioned the case of 100 ordered / 96 usable arriving, with returns handled by the regional agent — the workflow itself was not walked through.
  • Store closure and opening events. The Definitive Plan mentions restructuring; the visit did not cover how store transitions are handled today.
  • The relationship between WhatsApp groups and structured data. Daily reports flow as WhatsApp messages, but the storage and retrieval of those messages for monthly aggregation was not detailed.
  • The 0.1% case — when non-branded items mistakenly route directly to stores instead of the warehouse. The recovery workflow when this happens.
