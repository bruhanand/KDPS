Chapter 4 · The two ledgers

The Stock Ledger — a second, parallel ledger

Perpetual inventory as its own voucher-driven stock ledger with a balance cache (Bin), valuation (FIFO / moving average), and conditional projection into the GL when perpetual inventory is enabled. The proof that an ERP is a set of sub-ledgers feeding a general ledger.

Why this subsystem decides whether your ERP is real

In the accounting ledger we saw the General Ledger: a single controlled book where every business event lands as a balanced set of debit/credit rows and cancellation is traceable. The stock subsystem is the moment you discover that the GL is not enough. The GL tells you that inventory worth ₹4,80,000 sits on the balance sheet. It cannot tell you that the ₹4,80,000 is 118 of SKU ABC-M-Blue in the Patna store and 40 in the Deoghar warehouse, nor what each unit cost, nor what you can promise a customer who walks in tomorrow.

So ERPNext builds a second, parallel stock ledger dedicated to stock — the Stock Ledger Entry (SLE) — running alongside the GL with strict voucher-driven creation/cancellation, but not strict row immutability: reposting can update carried valuation fields on existing future rows. The SLE is keyed by (item_code, warehouse, posting_datetime, voucher) and records the physical truth: how many units moved, what they were worth, and what the running balance is after the move. Then — in companies using perpetual inventory — stock movements also project balanced pairs into the GL, with the money figure derived from the stock ledger's own valuation. Operations and books stay aligned when that perpetual-inventory projection is enabled.

Key idea An ERP is not "an accounting system with some inventory bolted on." It is a federation of sub-ledgers — stock, accounts receivable, accounts payable, payroll, cash — each an independent per-key event log, all of which project into the one General Ledger. Inventory is simply the most demanding sub-ledger, because unlike money it has to track quantity and value separately, and value is path-dependent. Get this one right and the pattern repeats everywhere else.

The rest of this chapter walks the six reusable patterns that make this work, with the real ERPNext code for each, and a generalization you can lift onto a Django/Postgres stack. The single source file you will return to most is erpnext/stock/stock_ledger.py — the valuation brain — and the controller base erpnext/controllers/stock_controller.py, which is where "one event, two ledgers" is enforced.

Pattern 1 · Stock as a second voucher-driven ledger

The first and most foundational decision: inventory is not a mutable quantity column on the item or the warehouse. You never run UPDATE item SET qty = qty - 1. You create voucher-backed movement rows, and the balance is something the ledger carries, with future rows reposted when a past movement changes.

PATTERN

Event-source the inventory

The idea

Inventory is a ledger of movements. Each row records the delta and the balance-after, so the ledger is simultaneously the audit trail and the source of truth.

You never UPDATE an item or warehouse balance directly. A correction goes through the source voucher; ERPNext marks cancelled rows and writes reversing rows, then reposts future carried balances.

How ERPNext does it

The SLE is is_submittable + in_create with read-only fields in normal document use. actual_qty is the delta; qty_after_transaction / valuation_rate / stock_value are the carried balance and can be rewritten by the repost engine.

on_cancel() in stock/doctype/stock_ledger_entry/stock_ledger_entry.py hard-throws — you cannot cancel one SLE. is_cancelled is a soft flag, never a delete. A composite index on (item_code, warehouse, posting_datetime, creation) is added in on_doctype_update().

Apply to any ERP

On Django/Postgres: a StockLedgerEntry table with item_id, warehouse_id, qty_delta, posting_datetime, voucher_type, voucher_id, plus carried qty_after / value_after columns.

Expose changes only through voucher/repost services. For a stricter greenfield design, make the row insert-only and write replacement projections separately; if you keep ERPNext-style carried balances on each row, accept that reposting must update future rows.

The refusal to cancel an individual row is the load-bearing rule — it forces every correction through the voucher, which keeps the audit trail intact:

When a voucher is cancelled, make_sl_entries() (in erpnext/stock/stock_ledger.py) flips is_cancelled = 1 on the original rows and writes reversing entries with negated actual_qty and swapped incoming/outgoing rates, then reposts forward. Gotcha — the running balance is on the row The SLE stores qty_after_transaction, valuation_rate and stock_value on each row. This is the source of all back-dating pain (Pattern 3): any insert/edit/cancel in the past makes every later row's balance and valuation wrong, forcing a forward repost. If you instead computed the balance on read you'd avoid reposts entirely but pay on every query. ERPNext chose write-time and built a repost engine to compensate. Know which trade-off you are making — it is the single biggest architectural fork in this subsystem.
Applying to KDPS This SLE shape is the literal spine of KDPS's "documents-write-ledgers" rule. item_code = the SKU at style × size × colour granularity; warehouse = the store or warehouse. An SLE keyed by (item_code, warehouse) is KDPS's per-store, per-SKU stock ledger — and because qty survives at SKU level on every row, "stock at style level only" can never sneak in. Corrections to a PT or GRN become reversing inserts, never edits, so the statutory audit trail Tally expects stays intact.

Pattern 2 · Valuation is the hard part — isolate it, store its state

Quantity is trivial: it is just the sum of deltas. Money is not. The cost of what you ship depends on the method you chose (Moving Average vs FIFO vs LIFO vs serial/batch), and it is path-dependent — the same three receipts and one issue produce a different COGS under FIFO than under moving average. ERPNext's answer is to push all of this into one file, erpnext/stock/stock_ledger.py, behind a small set of methods, and to serialize the method's full state onto every SLE row so the next entry can resume without replaying history.

PATTERN

Encapsulate valuation behind a strategy; persist its state per row

The idea

Cost is method-specific and path-dependent. Hide each method behind one interface (add_stock, remove_stock, total), and persist enough state on each ledger row to resume valuation deterministically without re-reading the whole history.

How ERPNext does it

update_entries_after.process_sle() dispatches on self.valuation_method: get_moving_average_values() recomputes a weighted-average rate; update_queue_values() delegates to FIFOValuation/LIFOValuation in erpnext/stock/valuation.py.

FIFO/LIFO model stock as a list of [qty, rate] bins. The entire queue is serialized into the SLE column stock_queue (Long Text) so the next entry resumes without replay.

Apply to any ERP

Define a Valuation strategy interface with FIFO/LIFO/MovingAverage implementations. Persist the strategy's state (the bin list, as JSON) on each ledger row.

On any stack this is a small pure module with zero DB dependency — unit-test it in isolation, which is exactly what ERPNext does in test_stock_ledger_entry.py.

Moving average — a weighted mean, recomputed on each receipt

The whole moving-average method is a few lines of arithmetic with no database in sight:

def get_moving_average_values(self, sle):
    actual_qty = flt(sle.actual_qty)
    new_stock_qty = flt(self.wh_data.qty_after_transaction) + actual_qty
    if new_stock_qty >= 0:
        if actual_qty > 0:
            if flt(self.wh_data.qty_after_transaction) <= 0:
                self.wh_data.valuation_rate = sle.incoming_rate
            else:
                new_stock_value = (
                    self.wh_data.qty_after_transaction * self.wh_data.valuation_rate
                ) + (actual_qty * sle.incoming_rate)
                self.wh_data.valuation_rate = new_stock_value / new_stock_qty

FIFO — stock is a queue of [qty, rate] bins

FIFO/LIFO live in erpnext/stock/valuation.py as a pure data structure. A receipt appends a bin; an issue pops bins first-in-first-out; and — critically — a depleted queue keeps a negative bin rather than erroring:

def add_stock(self, qty: float, rate: float) -> None:
    if not len(self.queue):
        self.queue.append([0, 0])
    if self.queue[-1][RATE] == rate:        # merge same-rate bin
        self.queue[-1][QTY] += qty
    else:
        if self.queue[-1][QTY] > 0:
            self.queue.append([qty, rate])  # positive balance, new bin
        else:                               # negative balance qty
            qty = self.queue[-1][QTY] + qty
            if qty > 0:
                self.queue[-1] = [qty, rate]
            else:
                self.queue[-1][QTY] = qty

BinWiseValuation.get_total_stock_and_value() collapses the bin list back to a single (qty, value) pair when the row needs a flat number. Dispatch between the three methods happens in process_sle(), and the dividing line between "add" and "remove" is just the sign of actual_qty.

Gotcha — you cannot "just store a cost" Valuation is path-dependent and method-specific. The stock_queue (FIFO/LIFO bins) is serialized as JSON on every SLE so the next entry resumes correctly. Moving Average vs FIFO give different COGS for identical movements. And the method is per-item/company (get_valuation_method), so one warehouse can legitimately mix methods across items. A schema that has a single unit_cost column and no per-row strategy state cannot reproduce correct numbers.
Why the strategy module is the easiest thing to test Because valuation is pure (in → out, no IO), it is the part of the whole ERP you can cover most cheaply and most completely. Feed a sequence of receipts and issues, assert the rate and the queue. ERPNext does exactly this; you should too, before you wire valuation into the controller. A bug here corrupts the GL silently.

Pattern 3 · Back-dated entries invalidate the future → a repost engine

This is the pattern naive reimplementations miss, and the one that will bite you in production. Because each SLE carries a running balance and a path-dependent valuation, inserting or editing an entry in the past makes every later row wrong — both its quantity-after and its rate. You cannot leave them stale. You must recompute forward. And for a hot SKU with tens of thousands of movements, that recomputation cannot be synchronous, so it becomes a checkpointed, resumable background job.

PATTERN

A back-dated mutation triggers a resumable forward repost

The idea

Carrying running balances means the past edits the future. Any back-dated insert/edit/cancel must recompute every later row forward. For long chains this must be async and resumable — checkpoint progress so it survives timeouts and restarts.

And: restrict who may back-date at all, behind a role and a freeze date.

How ERPNext does it

update_entries_after walks SLEs forward re-deriving balances. update_qty_in_future_sle() does a single bulk SQL UPDATE to shift the qty tail fast.

The full valuation repost is offloaded to Repost Item Valuation: repost() → repost_future_sle() loops items, persisting current_index + a gzipped data file after each, catching timeouts to resume, then repost_gl_entries() re-posts the GL. validate_with_last_transaction_posting_time() + Stock Settings gate back-dating.

Apply to any ERP

Treat any back-dated mutation as a trigger to enqueue a repost job scoped to (item, warehouse). The job recomputes forward, writes a fast bulk qty-shift, and checkpoints progress so it survives restarts.

Gate back-dating by role + freeze date from day one. This is the single biggest thing naive reimplementations leave out, then retrofit painfully.

The quantity tail is shifted in one bulk statement — fast, because qty is just additive — while the (expensive, path-dependent) valuation repost is split off into the async job:

Gotcha — ordering key is posting_datetime + creation, never just the date Multiple movements can share a posting_date. The real ordering key is posting_datetime (date + time combined) plus creation as a tie-break — get_actual_qty orders by posting_datetime desc, creation desc. This matters for FIFO (which bin pops first) and for "which row is last." Storing only a date will scramble valuation the moment two entries land on the same day.
Applying to KDPS KDPS will routinely correct a PT or GRN after the fact — a wrong rate, a miscount, a late freight line. The Repost Item Valuation engine is the answer: a back-dated PT correction enqueues a scoped repost, the qty tail shifts in bulk, valuation re-runs forward, and the corrected COGS re-posts to the GL (and from there to Tally). Pair this with the freeze-date + role gate so that once a month is closed and filed, nobody silently rewrites it. This is also where four of the five "CA-ruling" items live — they are all about when the GL leg may legitimately move after the fact.

Pattern 4 · The Bin — a derivable balance cache for O(1) availability

Summing the ledger to answer "how much do I have?" is fine once. Doing it on every sales-order line, for every item, while a salesperson waits, is not. So ERPNext keeps a one-row-per-key cache of the current balance — the Bin — but treats it as a rebuildable projection of the ledger, never the source of truth.

PATTERN

A denormalized balance cache, always rebuildable from the ledger

The idea

Reading "how much do I have / will I have" by scanning the ledger is too slow for every line. Keep a one-row-per-key cache of the current and projected balance — but treat it as a rebuildable projection, never authoritative. The ledger is truth; the cache is speed.

How ERPNext does it

Bin holds one row per (item_code, warehouse) (unique index via on_doctype_update's add_unique). It carries actual_qty, valuation_rate, stock_value and a forward-looking projected_qty = actual + ordered + indented + planned − sales reservations − production/subcontract reservations.

update_bin()/update_qty() write it as a side effect of posting; get_actual_qty() reads the true value from the last SLE; recalculate_qty() fully rebuilds it.

Apply to any ERP

A materialized stock_balance table keyed (item, warehouse), updated in the same transaction as the ledger insert, holding current and projected quantities.

Always provide a rebuild-from-ledger function and use it as the correctness backstop. Add the unique key so concurrent posts cannot fork the balance.

The projected quantity is the genuinely useful part — it folds in the supply/demand pipeline, not just on-hand:

actual_qty
What is physically on hand right now — mirrors the last SLE's qty_after_transaction.
ordered_qty
On open Purchase Orders, inbound.
indented_qty
On Material Requests (internal demand raised).
planned_qty
To be produced (Work Orders).
reserved_qty
Committed to open Sales Orders / reservations — subtracted, because it is spoken for.
reserved_qty_for_production / reserved_qty_for_sub_contract / reserved_qty_for_production_plan
Additional committed stock buckets for manufacturing and subcontracting — also subtracted.
projected_qty
actual + ordered + indented + planned − reserved − production/subcontract reservations — "what I will have when the dust settles" (set_projected_qty, erpnext/stock/doctype/bin/bin.py:66).

The unique index that prevents two concurrent posts from forking the balance is declared in on_doctype_update (erpnext/stock/doctype/bin/bin.py:229). Gotcha — the Bin can go stale; never blindly balance += delta Bin is a cache, not truth. It can drift on back-dated transactions and cancellations. update_qty() explicitly re-reads from the SLE when future_sle_exists or is_cancelled, rather than trusting its own increment (erpnext/stock/doctype/bin/bin.py:251). Applying to KDPS The Bin's projected_qty is exactly what a KDPS store needs at the counter: "do I have this SKU, and is more on the way?" answered in O(1) without scanning a per-SKU ledger that may run to tens of thousands of rows across 50+ stores. The reserved_qty column is where a hold for an online order or an inter-store request lives, so the floor cannot oversell stock that is already promised.

Pattern 5 · Every physical movement also hits the GL

Here is where the two ledgers become one discipline. Inventory is an asset; moving it changes the balance sheet. So a single business event must post to two ledgers atomically: the stock sub-ledger (quantity + valuation) and the financial GL (a balanced debit/credit pair). And the GL amount is not entered by anyone — it is read straight off the stock ledger's computed value. This is what makes "profitability is derived, never hand-entered" structurally true rather than a policy you hope people follow.

PATTERN

One shared controller posts both ledgers; the GL value is derived from the SLE

The idea

A single physical event must post to two ledgers atomically — the stock sub-ledger and the financial GL — inside one DB transaction. The GL figure is derived from the sub-ledger's computed value, never typed in.

Generalize: stock, receivables, payables, payroll are each sub-ledgers, and each entry projects into the one GL.

How ERPNext does it

StockController(AccountsController) in erpnext/controllers/stock_controller.py is the shared base for Purchase Receipt, Delivery Note, Stock Entry, Sales/Purchase Invoice.

make_sl_entries() writes the SLEs; make_gl_entries() runs only if erpnext.is_perpetual_inventory_enabled(company), delegating to BaseStockGLComposer.compose(), which debits the warehouse inventory account by flt(sle.stock_value_difference) and credits the expense account by its negative.

Apply to any ERP

Make a shared posting base class for any inventory-affecting document. On submit it (1) appends stock ledger rows, (2) reads back each row's value delta, (3) emits a balanced GL pair (debit inventory asset, credit COGS / clearing) in one DB transaction, gated by a company "perpetual inventory" flag.

Generalize the principle to every sub-ledger.

Gotcha — the GL leg is conditional on perpetual inventory The financial pair only fires when is_perpetual_inventory_enabled(company) is true. Under periodic / non-perpetual inventory, stock moves but no GL entry is made until a manual period-end adjustment. So "stock always hits the GL" is conditional on company configuration — do not assume the inventory asset account moves in real time everywhere. For most modern builds you want perpetual on, but know which mode you are in.
Gotcha — inter-company / cross-state transfers need a transfer rate Internal transfers between warehouses of different companies or states need a transfer valuation rate (get_incoming_rate_for_inter_company_transfer) and can produce GL rounding gain/loss entries. This is not an edge case for KDPS — it is the Bihar ↔ Jharkhand reality (see below).
Applying to KDPS Two consequences land directly. (1) Cross-state transfers are taxable supplies. Bihar and Jharkhand are two GSTINs / distinct persons, so a stock move between them is not a free internal transfer — it needs a transfer valuation rate and a real IGST GL leg, exactly the inter-company nuance above. (2) SOR / consignment is about when the GL leg fires. KDPS counts SOR stock by quantity (the SLE actual_qty / Bin actual_qty) while keeping its value off-book until sale — in ERPNext terms, deferring when stock_value_difference hits the GL and the vendor liability posts. The five CA-ruling items (single-recognition GST, the 6-month deemed-supply clock, late-freight-after-PT, sold-before-PT, no-reposting) are precisely decisions about when that GL leg may move.

Pattern 6 · Locations as a nested-set tree

Warehouses are not a flat list. They form a hierarchy — region → store → bin — and you need two things from it: fast subtree rollups ("total stock under Bihar") and a hard rule that you cannot post stock against an interior "group" node, only against leaves.

PATTERN

Hierarchical locations with subtree rollup and leaf-only posting

The idea

Locations form a tree (region → store → bin). You want O(subtree) rollups (total stock under a region) and a rule that forbids transactions against non-leaf "group" nodes — you post to a shelf, not to "all of Bihar."

How ERPNext does it

Warehouse(NestedSet) in erpnext/stock/doctype/warehouse/warehouse.py uses Frappe's nested-set mixin (lft/rgt/parent_warehouse, is_group, nsm_parent_field). SLE validation blocks posting to group warehouses (block_transactions_against_group_warehouse → is_group_warehouse).

The nested-set bookkeeping itself lives in frappe.utils.nestedset Frappe core — ERPNext only subclasses it.

Apply to any ERP

Model location as an adjacency-list tree with materialized lft/rgt (nested set), or use Postgres ltree / recursive CTEs. Mark interior nodes is_group and reject leaf-only operations (stock postings) against them.

Use lft BETWEEN parent.lft AND parent.rgt for subtree aggregation.

The leaf-only rule is enforced at SLE validation time, not just in the UI:

Applying to KDPS The tree fits KDPS's 50+ stores rolling up by state and region: India → Bihar / Jharkhand → district → store → (optional) bin. "Total stock under Bihar" becomes a single lft BETWEEN … AND … range scan, and the state node naturally anchors the GSTIN that every leaf store maps to — which is exactly what makes the cross-state transfer in Pattern 5 a taxable supply.

Building it on Django / Postgres — a concrete shape

If you are reimplementing this (which is the KDPS plan), here is the minimal table set that preserves every property above. Treat it as the contract, not the styling.

stock_ledger_entry (insert-only)
id, item_id, warehouse_id, posting_datetime, creation, voucher_type, voucher_id, actual_qty, incoming_rate, outgoing_rate, qty_after_transaction, valuation_rate, stock_value, stock_value_difference, stock_queue (jsonb), is_cancelled. Composite index (item_id, warehouse_id, posting_datetime, creation). No UPDATE/DELETE grants in app code — corrections are reversing inserts.
stock_balance (the Bin)
item_id, warehouse_id UNIQUE, plus actual_qty, reserved_qty, ordered_qty, indented_qty, planned_qty, projected_qty, valuation_rate, stock_value. Written in the same transaction as the SLE insert. A recalculate(item, warehouse) function rebuilds it from the ledger — your correctness backstop.
valuation strategy module
Pure Python/TS, no DB: FIFO / LIFO / MovingAverage classes with add_stock, remove_stock, total. Serialize the bin list to the SLE's stock_queue jsonb. Unit-tested in isolation.
repost job
Celery/RQ task scoped to (item, warehouse), triggered by any back-dated mutation: bulk UPDATE qty_after_transaction for the tail, then forward valuation recompute with a current_index checkpoint, then re-post GL. Gated by a freeze date + role.
posting base class
A StockDocument mixin whose on_submit calls make_sl_entries() then, if company.perpetual_inventory, make_gl_entries() — debit inventory asset = stock_value_difference, credit COGS/clearing = its negative — all inside one DB transaction.
warehouse tree
Postgres ltree or recursive CTE; an is_group flag; a check that blocks postings to group nodes. (Simpler than reimplementing nested-set bookkeeping.)
Key idea The discipline that keeps an ERP's books and its operations in agreement is one sentence: every physical movement writes its stock sub-ledger row, and in perpetual-inventory mode atomically posts a balanced GL pair derived from that row; money is computed from the ledger and never hand-entered; corrections reverse and trigger a forward repost. Build stock this way and the same skeleton — movement ledger + balance cache + projection into the GL — drops straight onto AR, AP, cash and payroll.

Takeaways

  1. Stock is its own movement ledger, not a column. The Stock Ledger Entry is submittable and refuses direct row cancellation (on_cancel throws); you cancel the source voucher, which marks/counters rows and reposts carried valuation forward. Reuse the voucher-driven ledger shape for every sub-ledger, but be explicit about whether your rows are strict event log rows or ERPNext-style rows with reposted carried balances.
  2. The SLE carries the running balance and a valuation snapshot on each row (qty_after_transaction, valuation_rate, stock_value, stock_queue). That choice buys O(1)-ish reads but means the past edits the future — a deliberate write-time vs read-time trade-off you must make consciously.
  3. Valuation is the hard, path-dependent part — isolate it. Moving-average, FIFO/LIFO (a queue of [qty, rate] bins) and serial/batch live behind one interface in pure code with the strategy's state serialized per row. Unit-test it without a database before wiring it in.
  4. Back-dated entries demand a resumable repost engine. Bulk-shift the qty tail in one UPDATE; offload the valuation + GL recompute to a checkpointed (current_index + gzip file) background job; gate back-dating behind a role and a freeze date from day one.
  5. The Bin is a derivable cache, never truth. One unique row per (item, warehouse) with current and projected quantities for O(1) availability — but always re-read from the SLE on back-date/cancel and keep a rebuild-from-ledger path. Never blindly balance += delta.
  6. Every perpetual-inventory movement posts the GL too, with the money derived from the SLE. A shared StockController debits the warehouse inventory asset by stock_value_difference and credits COGS / Stock-Received-But-Not-Billed by its negative — gated by the perpetual-inventory flag. This is what makes "profitability is derived, never hand-entered" structurally true when that mode is enabled.
  7. For KDPS: SLE keyed by (SKU, store) at style×size×colour is the per-store stock ledger; the repost engine answers back-dated PT/GRN corrections; the nested-set warehouse tree anchors the two-GSTIN cross-state transfer as a taxable supply; and SOR/consignment is precisely the decision of when the stock_value_difference GL leg fires.
