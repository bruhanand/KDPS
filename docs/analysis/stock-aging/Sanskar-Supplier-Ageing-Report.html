The single most important number: ₹22.4L of these bills are already more than 180 days old. Under GST Rule 37, input tax credit taken on a purchase must be reversed (with interest) if the supplier is not paid within 180 days of the invoice. Another ₹25.8L crosses that line within the next 30 days — paying those 27 bills first protects the credit before it is lost.
  • This is not an old, forgotten book — it is a recent bulge. 61% of the money (₹64.2L) sits in the 91–180-day band, almost all of it from heavy buying in January and March 2026 (₹24.5L + ₹23.4L of those two months' bills are still unpaid).
  • Ten suppliers hold half the money. The top 10 account for 50% of the outstanding — ten settlement conversations cover ~₹53.0L.
  • 150 of 163 bills have had no payment at all (₹95.1L); only 13 bills are part-paid.
  • ₹2.7L of Sanskar's money is lying with 33 suppliers as excess payment / credit balances — recover it or adjust it against fresh purchases.
  • The register needs housekeeping: ~₹8.8L of part-payments are not mapped to bills (the ageing sheet overstates those parties), and 6 suppliers with ₹8.5L of ledger dues have no bills in the ageing sheet at all — led by Shree Garments (₹5.2L).

FIRST Protect the ITC — clear the 151–180-day window

27 bills worth ₹25,84,448 cross the 180-day GST line within the next 30 days. Paying these first prevents fresh ITC reversal. Then work through the ₹22.4L already past 180 days (20 suppliers above) — each payment allows the reversed credit to be re-availed.
