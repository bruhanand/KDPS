ERP Requirements Register

The master list of everything the system must do — gathered from the requirements-confirmation document, the current-workflow record, the client's demands, the real report & bank files, and the requirements session of 11 June 2026. This is the WHAT, never the HOW.

MustShouldLater priority (proposed — review & adjust) · AI reads/writes with a language model · Forecast prediction from numbers · Client still unconfirmed by the client
Fixed scope rulings (do not reopen): the Ten Software POS owns everything customer-facing at the counter — billing, customer data, the selling moment. Our scope at a store is stock in, stock out, and reporting what happened. No customer module, no loyalty; sales channel is in-store only. Non-branded invoice → PT (the PT Mapper) is core scope. Tally stays the book of record. The POS stays as-is; we assume its API and documentation come from the client.

1 · The system at a glance

  • 1.1One central system covering stock from brand to store, PT-file making, stock aging, daily cash & payment audit, and brand reports.Must
  • 1.2The POS stays untouched at the counter. Our system connects to it — sends it new items and prices, reads back sales, stock and collection.Must
  • 1.3Tally stays the statutory book of record. The system prepares import-ready vouchers for Tally; it never replaces it.Must
  • 1.4The PT Mapper, both kinds: convert any brand's PT file to KDPS format, and build a PT from a plain invoice — with barcodes.MustAI
  • 1.5Owners get key numbers on WhatsApp daily — the figures plus a short written summary of anything unusual.MustAI
  • 1.6A central sales dashboard — every store in one place, one login.Must
  • 1.7Everything runs in the browser — no app installs. Store staff use the store computer; phones serve the warehouse and people on the move.Must

2 · Buying terms — brands & vendors

  • 2.1A brand book: every brand's buying model (SOR / Outright / Consignment), margin percentages, return window days, payment terms — set once, applied everywhere.Must
  • 2.2The buying model is recorded per order, not only per brand — one order can occasionally run on a different model.Must
  • 2.3Fixed brand vendors are always branded. A non-branded vendor's shipment counts as branded only when all three are present: vendor barcode & tag, a proper detailed invoice, and a vendor PT file — checked per shipment.Must
  • 2.4Non-branded intake: read the vendor's invoice from a photo or PDF, ask the warehouse person for whatever is missing, produce the KDPS PT and barcode stickers.MustAI
  • 2.5No size on the invoice → the item is recorded as Free Size.Must
  • 2.6Price-setting for untagged goods: cost + ~1.1% transport + GST + margin by category. Decided in the inbound design: default 30–35% per category (the old plan's 20–25% is superseded). The per-category margin list is still needed from the client.MustClient
  • 2.7The calculated MRP can be overridden — decided in the inbound design: the operator may override within the category band.Must
  • 2.8Premium / Medium / Economy is used for non-branded stock when the invoice doesn't give a colour for every item — checking colour piece by piece is too painful. The warehouse operator picks the tier by judgment; no fixed price bands.Should
  • 2.9City sourcing agents are real records — who introduced the vendor, answerable until delivery.Must
  • 2.10Vendors without a GSTIN must still be representable. Ask the client: do all non-branded vendors have a GSTIN? (Buying and paying work differently without one.)MustClient
  • 2.11Brand incentives (target bonus, display money, advertisement support) tracked per brand.ShouldClient

3 · Booking orders

  • 3.1Brand orders: photograph/upload the brand's printed receiving paper → the order is drafted automatically → KDPS's person checks and confirms.MustAI
  • 3.2Non-branded verbal orders: the account manager types the order, speaks it as a voice note, or uploads a WhatsApp screenshot / photo — or simply forwards the vendor chat to the system's WhatsApp number. The system drafts the order from any of these.MustAI
  • 3.3Every capture makes a draft first; a person confirms before the order exists. Nothing auto-finalises.Must
  • 3.4The order lives in the system from booking day — branded 6–8 months ahead, non-branded 3–4 months ahead.Must
  • 3.5An order line is style + size + quantity. No colour, no prices at booking — prices come with the goods (PT).Must
  • 3.6One order = one brand, one season; goods may arrive in several lots against it.Must
  • 3.7Order statuses stay simple: booked → part-received / received → cancelled. If the vendor says an order can't be delivered, the person marks it cancelled.Must
  • 3.8Ordered vs received is always visible; pending balances stay open until goods come or a person cancels the line. The system never auto-closes anything.Must

4 · Goods arrival & PT making

  • 4.1Branded goods land at the store; non-branded at the warehouse (almost always). At a store, staff open the order, mark what actually came, press Done; the warehouse completes the entry.Must
  • 4.2The warehouse does counting only — quantity against the invoice. Damage is checked at the store; a damaged piece is set aside there, not sellable, and goes back to the brand.Must
  • 4.3Vendors sometimes send more than ordered. Over-received items are received on the same screen — an added line, flagged with a required remark; the original order lines stay, flagged (per the inbound design). Asking the vendor to take extras back is an outcome recorded on the flag, not a reason to skip the entry.Must
  • 4.4Short supply: the order shows part-received; the pending quantity stays open and visible until the goods come or a person cancels it.Must
  • 4.5Patna head office works with data only — it checks the PT (GST, margin), inwards it, and confirms the store split. Receiving never waits for Patna; nothing physical is ever blocked.Must
  • 4.6The warehouse proposes which store gets how much; Patna confirms; each store sees only its own share. The proposal starts from each store's past-sales share.MustForecast
  • 4.7PT making, all four ways: convert the vendor's PT · build the PT from the invoice (call the vendor for gaps) · keep the vendor's barcode and MRP tag · KDPS sets the MRP and prints barcodes when nothing is tagged.MustAI
  • 4.8Incoming invoice rows are matched against the brand's master PT file automatically.MustAI

5 · Stock in the store

  • 5.1The receiving store sees a pending inward the moment the sender saves the voucher — quantity visible before the goods arrive. Pieces are scanned in; all scanned = sellable. (This flow runs inside the POS today and stays there; our system tracks it.)Must
  • 5.2For brands whose PT carries no barcodes (e.g. Jockey), entry is by style code.Must
  • 5.3Every item carries season, collection and age — tagged automatically from its code. Aging drives markdowns and dead-stock handling.Must
  • 5.4Each store sees only its own stock; every item always shows a clear status — in transit, in store, sold, returned, set aside as defective.Must
  • 5.5Selling before the inward is keyed in happens and is not policed. The system absorbs it; owners can simply see what's happening in each store when they look.Must

6 · Stock going out — sale & exchange

  • 6.1The POS bills the sale; our system reads it and the stock comes off in our records. All bills, in every store, are made on the POS — confirmed. Every store runs its own Ten Software, used only as the billing counter.Must
  • 6.2Exchange, stock side: the returned piece comes back in — good condition → sellable stock; defective → the defective flow. Exchange is an MRP-to-MRP swap valued at the original final bill.Must
  • 6.3Exchange, money side: the customer pays the gap upward or adds items downward; the difference lands in that day's collection so the cash audit always adds up.Must
  • 6.4Whether the POS links an exchange to the original bill — and what happens on a no-bill exchange — are client questions. The bill-link half is already on the prepared Ten Software question list (returns/exchanges with reference to the original bill).Client

7 · Transfers between stores

  • 7.1Transfers are MBO stores only; EBO stock moves are handled between the brand and the EBO, outside our system.Must
  • 7.2The transfer voucher, pending inward and scanning all happen inside the POS today and stay there. Our system reads and tracks the movement.Must
  • 7.3Transport details recorded against the transfer — bus, driver, courier, who carried it. (Today this lives in a WhatsApp group and is lost.)Must
  • 7.4A reason on each transfer — customer waiting, slow mover, seasonal swap, freeing floor space.Should
  • 7.5Sent ≠ received: the receiving store closes its inward at what came; the gap sits visibly as "in transit — unresolved" against that voucher until head office settles it. Nothing disappears silently.Must
  • 7.6The e-way bill number is recorded on the transfer (made on the portal as today). Preparing e-way bill data inside the system comes later.Must (preparation: Later)

8 · Returns to brands & disposal

  • 8.1Defective returns run on two routes: Madura brands (Allen Solly, Louis Philippe, Van Heusen, Peter England) collect directly from the store; every other brand and non-branded — defectives gather at the warehouse and go back from there.Must
  • 8.2Seasonal SOR returns: unsold pieces within the agreed allowance go back at season end. The system tracks each brand's return window and alerts before it expires (30 / 15 / 7 days).Must
  • 8.3The system works out the return value KDPS is entitled to — designed already: one pool per vendor per season, growing with each priced stock-in at stock-in value (ex-GST), shrinking with each return; the 30/15/7-day alerts show pool remaining. Still for the brands/client: do brands compute their 10% on the same basis, and which date anchors the window.MustClient
  • 8.4Credit notes: every return to a brand should end in a credit note; the system tracks which came and which are pending.Must
  • 8.5Consignment vendors pick up their unsold goods; what they took is recorded.Must
  • 8.6Return lists ready-grouped by the brand's warehouse.Should
  • 8.7Stock nobody takes back (dead outright stock, refused defectives, abandoned consignment) gets a proper exit — proposed: owner approves, piece leaves the books as a written-off loss with the matching Tally entry. What happens physically today is a client question.ShouldClient

9 · Counting stock

  • 9.1Count sessions, store by store, section by section — scan the racks, the system shows book vs counted.Must
  • 9.2Every difference becomes a listed item — found / missing — never a silent correction. Adjustments post only after approval; count history stays per store.Must
  • 9.3How counting runs today, who answers for differences, and the counting rhythm are client questions.Client

10 · Prices, offers & EOSS

MRPs don't move — discounts do all the moving. Branded (~70% of buying): the brand decides the discount. Outright / non-branded: KDPS decides.

  • 10.1An offer book holding every offer type seen in practice: GWP (buy above a value, get a gift item at a token price — gift's worth recorded), ATV slabs (spend or quantity thresholds, stacked in families), item-scope conditions (specific fits/categories, MRP floors), buy-2-get-1 cheapest free, percent-off.Must
  • 10.2Each offer carries the stores it applies to, start date, end date — and supports "end not disclosed yet": the offer stays running until the brand closes it.Must
  • 10.3A fallback offer can take over when one ends.Must
  • 10.4Read the offer from the brand's circular or image and draft it; a person confirms. Manual entry always works.MustAI
  • 10.5Two truths per bill, kept side by side: what the counter actually billed (from the POS, never altered) and what the brand's offer says should have applied (from the offer book). The brand report is built 100% on the brand's terms.Must
  • 10.6Every bill where billed ≠ offer lands on a difference list — store, bill, gap. Owners see the total gap per store and brand. This also catches the pocketed-discount fraud.Must
  • 10.7EOSS records: who funds the discount (brand or KDPS), margin drop (25 → 18), by season/brand/category/store/dates.Must
  • 10.8A markdown ladder suggestion as stock ages (10 → 20 → 30%…).ShouldForecast
  • 10.9Who creates/approves an offer, what wins when two offers fit one bill, and whether gift items sit in stock records are client questions.Client

11 · Money — cash audit, vendor payments, brand paperwork

The daily cash audit

  • 11.1Per store, per day: cash + UPI + card/swipe + dues from the POS matched against what actually reached the bank.Must
  • 11.2Bank statements come into the system (upload or feed, per account) — without them the audit has nothing to match against.Must
  • 11.3Deposits matched to stores even from messy bank narrations (NEFT/INFT codes).MustAI
  • 11.4UPI and card settlements matched with their delay and bank charge, per store terminal (MID).Must
  • 11.5Petty cash spent from the till (salary advances, tea, alteration, courier, rent, electricity, repairs…) recorded before deposit.Must
  • 11.6Credit (udhaar) sales and dues recovery tracked in the daily cash book — the store files prove they exist.Must
  • 11.7The store's daily workbook (DSR) — sales lines, cash book with opening/closing balance, inward, reconciliation, petty cash — is produced by the system, not typed by hand.Must

Paying vendors & brand paperwork

  • 11.8Vendor payments need approval; above a limit, someone senior approves — routed by amount, approvable inside WhatsApp. Who and what limits are the client's call.MustClient
  • 11.9SOR payments are for sold goods — the system prepares what's owed from actual sales. How the brand demands it today is a client question.MustClient
  • 11.10No payment to any vendor unless their bank details and GSTIN are on file. (Proposed rule — client to accept.)MustClient
  • 11.11One lump payment split across many part-paid invoices automatically.Must
  • 11.12Cash discount calculated on the pre-tax amount only; the GST line stays whole.Must
  • 11.13Brand statements reconciled against our records — sales, returns, credit notes, incentive claims — so what the brand says matches what we have.Must

12 · Tax & Tally

  • 12.1GSTIN on every entity and vendor; HSN code and tax breakup on every item line; every document filing-ready.Must
  • 12.2Vouchers prepared for Tally import — purchases, sales, returns, credit notes, write-offs. Nightly auto-sync can come later.Must (auto-sync: Later)
  • 12.3E-way bill number recorded on inter-store transfers (Bihar↔Jharkhand crossings especially).Must
  • 12.4For the client / CA: how many entities and GSTINs · does e-invoicing apply · who makes e-way bills today · how GSTIN-less purchases are booked · Tally company & ledger structure for import.Client

13 · Reports & daily numbers

  • 13.1Monthly brand reports (Sale vouchers + SOH) generated by the system in the exact format sent today — discounts filled per the brand's offer book, returns cancelling the right lines.Must
  • 13.2The daily WhatsApp to owners: yesterday's sales, collection vs bank, and anything unusual — numbers plus a short written summary.MustAI
  • 13.3The central dashboard: every store's sales in one place, one login.Must
  • 13.4Stock aging on every item, so old stock is caught before it dies. The "old after how many days" thresholds are the client's to set.MustClient
  • 13.5Trading profit per store and brand — cost from the PT at stock-in, revenue from the POS at sale. Never hand-entered.Must
  • 13.6The full store P&L reads expense totals from Tally (rent, salaries) — nobody enters expenses twice. Petty cash is the one expense stream captured by us.Should
  • 13.7Staff performance, reporting only: actuals per salesperson from POS data — sales value, pieces, per store, per month, ranked. Whether owners set targets to compare against is a client question.MustClient
  • 13.8The POS data is dirty and the system survives it: brand names written many ways, tier words in the colour field, mangled barcodes, manual + online bill numbers, returns as minus quantities. Name variants are mapped, nothing crashes on a weird row.MustAI

14 · People & access

  • 14.1Three kinds of users to start — Store (own store only), Warehouse, Patna/Head Office (everything) — plus an admin.Must
  • 14.2Every action records who did it and when — automatic, for everyone equally, kept seven years.Must
  • 14.3Profit and margin visibility is a setting; who sees it is the client's call.MustClient
  • 14.4Per-store vs per-person logins, Hindi/English screens, and who adds/removes users — client questions.Client

15 · Old data & going live

  • 15.1Past sales history loaded, so reports, aging and forecasting have something to learn from. How many years exist in the POS and whether they can be exported is a client question.MustClient
  • 15.2Opening stock per store = one physical count at go-live. Unavoidable.Must
  • 15.3Go-live store by store: 2–3 pilots, then waves; the system runs in parallel with today's routine until proven.Must
  • 15.4Training simple, in Hindi, mostly short videos and voice notes.Must
  • 15.5Pilot store choice and the festival/audit blackout calendar — client questions.Client

16 · Success & ownership

  • 16.1One named super-user at KDPS who owns the system day-to-day.MustClient
  • 16.2A written six-month success definition from the owner — the numbers that make him say "this worked."MustClient
  • 16.3Running cost stays within the client's stated cap (₹25–40K/month) — cap to be re-confirmed.MustClient
