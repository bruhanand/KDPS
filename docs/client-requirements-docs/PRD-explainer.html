Product Requirements Document · Explained

KDPS — an operating system for a multi‑brand fashion retailer

This PRD describes four connected control systems that replace today's manual, Excel-and-WhatsApp way of running stores. Each one turns a fuzzy, human-dependent process into a structured, auditable digital workflow — from the moment goods arrive, through paying the brand, selling in-store, and paying the staff.

1 — Overview

What problem is KDPS actually solving?

Today the business runs on parallel, loosely-connected manual processes. Goods are matched to invoices by eye, PT files are interpreted by hand, payments are approved by memory, store stock is tracked in heads and handwritten memos, and attendance is a WhatsApp photo. Every one of these is slow, easy to game, and impossible to audit later.

❌ The "as-is" reality
  • Inventory matched to invoices manually, by experience
  • PT files in every format — cleaned & mapped by hand
  • Data reshaped in Excel before it reaches accounting
  • Payments approved informally, no record of who/why
  • Store stock moves on handwritten memos
  • Attendance is a photo in front of the store
✅ The "to-be" target
  • Every input normalized into one KDPS schema
  • Accounting-ready (TIR) data with zero manual rework
  • No rupee paid without reconciliation + approval + audit trail
  • Store stock as a state-driven ledger, not one number
  • EOSS "billed-but-retained" stock tracked separately
  • Verified attendance via face check-in, feeding payroll
One sentence: KDPS converts real-world, inconsistent, delayed inputs (invoices, PT files, physical goods, staff presence) into clean, structured, auditable truth that operations, accounting, and banking can all trust — without anyone reshaping data in Excel.

3 — Deep dive

①

Inventory Ingestion & Normalization Engine

IINE · "the entry point for all inventory and financial truth"
What the client wants

A system that takes any vendor input — a photographed invoice, a PT file in any brand's format, physical goods on the dock — and converts it into one clean, standardized, accounting-ready dataset. The output must always be 100% compatible with the TIR format (the structure Tally / the accounting software needs) without any manual reshaping.

Every received item must be…
  • Physically validated — it actually arrived
  • Financially traceable — cost is known
  • Semantically standardized — mapped to KDPS taxonomy
Success looks like
  • Zero Excel-based transformations
  • Minimal manual classification work
  • Real-time view: ordered vs received vs payable
  • Consistent taxonomy across branded & non-branded
Why today is painful (As-Is)

Three parallel processes, all human-heavy:

FlowWhat happens nowPain
A. Physical receiptItems matched against invoice by hand; shortages/damages noted on paper, told to brand via account managerInformal "received as expected / discrepancy" — nothing structured
B. PT file (branded)PT opened manually; columns differ per brand; mapped by experience to category/subcategory/type/fitNo standard format, no automated mapping, heavy reliance on one person
C. Non-brandedNo PT file; human interprets product from invoice + inspection, creates attributes, pricing, tax, margin, barcodeSubjective classification, duplicate SKUs, high cognitive load
D. Transform to accountingData manually reshaped into TIR format, uploaded to accountingSlow, error-prone, blocks payment
The structural gaps: no unified data model · no decoupling (physical receipt, PT, and accounting are tightly coupled and block each other) · no rule engine (mappings are implicit & unversioned) · no SKU identity control (same product → many SKUs) · GRN isn't a real system object · no audit trail of "who mapped what, and why".
Proposed design — the to-be flow

IINE becomes a central layer between physical inventory and the accounting system, decoupling the three flows so they run independently and asynchronously.

Step 1 · Invoice Upload mandatory — no downstream processing without it
↓
Step 2 · GRN Creation confirm received items, mark shortages/damages — independent of PT
↓
Step 3a · Branded upload PT → auto-map → normalize
Step 3b · Non-branded create product entries → generate PT internally
↓
Step 4 · Reconciliation system flags mismatches & missing data: Invoice ↔ GRN ↔ PT
↓
Step 5 · Accounting Export TIR-ready file generated — no manual reshaping
↓
Step 6 · Approval & Payment (handed to Module ②) enabled by clean data

Store Inventory Movement, Adjustment & Sell-Through Control

SIMAS · "a state-driven inventory control system, not a stock counter"
What the client wants

This module governs everything that happens after inventory becomes valid store stock. The key insight: in an MBO, store stock is not a single number — the naïve formula opening + inward − sales = closing is too simplistic. Stock also changes through transfers, defects, brand returns, and end-of-season commercial billing where stock is billed but physically stays in the store.

The core formula it must respect:
Store Available Stock = Opening Accepted Stock + New Accepted Inward − All Valid Reductions − All Pending Outbound Not Yet Accepted.
…but reductions must be classified by type, because each has different commercial meaning.
The five reduction / movement types
#TypeWhat it means
1Customer sale (POS)Normal sale — stock physically leaves the store
2Internal transferStock moves to another store (must be authorized & acknowledged)
3Defective stockFound defective after inward
4Brand-approved / seasonal returnReturned to the brand under contract
5EOSS billed-but-retainedCommercially billed (sold for settlement) but physically stays in store
Why this is hard — three different "stocks"
The dual/triple reality

The business must always distinguish:

  • Physical stock — what's bodily in the store
  • System stock — what records say is available for sale
  • Commercially billed stock — billed to brand but still on the shelf
Questions it must answer instantly
  • What's available in Store A right now?
  • What's in the store but not available for sale?
  • What's on the way from Store A → B?
  • How much was billed under EOSS but still lying in store?
  • How much of that has since been physically sold?
Key improvement — model each SKU as a state machine

Instead of flat stock-in/stock-out, every SKU at a store exists in one of several states:

Available for Sale Pending Acceptance Pending Transfer Out In Transit Accepted at Destination Defective / Quarantined Return Approved Returned to Brand Sold to Customer EOSS Billed & Retained Liquidated / Disposed

Workforce Attendance, Presence & Payroll Control System

WAPCS · "verified staff presence without legacy punch machines"
What the client wants

Answer one question reliably: who came to work, when did they arrive, when did they leave — and on that basis calculate salary and incentives. Today's "send a photo in front of the store" proves nothing about start time, end time, or continuous presence, and is easy to fake. The goal is a trusted presence layer that feeds payroll, plus an employee profile/document store (PAN, Aadhaar).

The central tension: attendance must be easy enough to use daily, but strong enough that people can't casually game it. The design must balance ease of use, fraud resistance, low hardware dependency, and simple rollout.
Two options considered → the recommendation
✅ Option 1 — Store-owned Android device + face check-in (RECOMMENDED)

One dedicated phone/tablet stays in the store; employee opens the app, face is verified against their stored profile to clock in/out.

  • Standardized hardware, easy to control & supervise
  • Simple to train: come in → check in → leave → check out
  • No dependence on Wi-Fi strength or personal phones
  • Implementation complexity: Moderate — the practical, stable choice
Option 2 — Employee phone + Wi-Fi / IP presence

App on personal phones infers presence from store Wi-Fi / network.

  • No store hardware needed; potentially richer data
  • But: Wi-Fi off, battery issues, fluctuating signal, false exits
  • Android background-process restrictions, privacy complexity
  • Implementation complexity: High — harder to build, trust & support
Proposed design — system layers

Store Attendance App

Dedicated Android device: check-in/out, face verify, offline queue

Employee Self-Service

View history, upload documents, raise corrections

HR / Admin Dashboard

Create profiles, approve docs, late/absent reports, payroll export

Face Verification Engine

Stores templates at onboarding; liveness detection vs spoofing

Attendance Event Engine

Check-in, check-out, missed event, manual correction, override

Device & Store Binding

Each device mapped to a store; events auto-tagged to that store

Employee Master

Details, documents, salary structure, incentive plan, assigned store

Rules + Payroll Layer

Shift timing, grace, half-day → days present, absences, payable days

Core workflow (end-to-end)
Onboarding HR creates profile · PAN + Aadhaar uploaded · face enrolled · store assigned · salary & incentive rules linked

4 — Reference

Glossary of terms in this PRD

TermMeaning
PT file"Packing/Product Transfer" file the brand sends listing what's shipped. Format differs per brand — the thing IINE must normalize.
GRNGoods Receipt Note — the structured record of what was physically received (incl. shortages/damages). IINE makes it a first-class system object.
TIRThe structured, accounting-ready transaction format the accounting software (Tally) needs. Not a vendor file or industry standard — KDPS's internal "books-ready" dataset.
SOR vs OutrightSale-or-Return (brand owns stock until sold, with a return window) vs Outright (KDPS owns from day one). Drives margin, returns & EOSS rules.
EOSSEnd Of Season Sale. Can involve "billed but retained" stock — commercially billed for brand settlement while physically staying in the store.
MBOMulti-Brand Outlet — a store carrying many brands, which is why stock states & commercial models get complex.
Billed but RetainedA distinct inventory bucket: stock that's been billed (e.g. under EOSS) but is still physically on the shelf, sold later without re-counting revenue.
SKUA specific sellable item. In fashion = Style × Size × Color, so stock must always be tracked at that granularity.
