The one design that emerged

Seven independent research passes pointed at the same shape: not a flashy "AI agent" — a disciplined, explainable, read-only brain with one trustworthy spine of numbers underneath.

The principleWhat it means, in plain words
1 · One spine of numbersDefine every metric once, computed by plain code. Makes the digest, the reports and any "ask-anything" agree — instead of quietly contradicting each other.
2 · Clean the data on read, nowMap LP / LY / LR → one brand, fix season labels, drop cost > MRP rows — without waiting for the D8 master clean-up.
3 · Code does the maths, AI only talksDeterministic code computes the number; the LLM only narrates, explains and translates it (to Hindi, on WhatsApp).
4 · Copilot, never autopilotAI suggests, a human approves. This is how the mature market runs — it is best practice, not timidity.
5 · Crawl → walk → runSimple rules now (they stay permanent); ML later wraps them. Promise the client honesty, not magic.
6 · Meet owners on WhatsAppThe digest is an exception feed, not a report. The craft is what you leave out.
The pitch to the client
  • We're not automating KDPS's spreadsheets — we're giving the business a single source of truth + a brain on top.
  • It watches every store every day, explains itself in plain Hindi on WhatsApp, and never moves a rupee on its own.
  • That's how Aditya Birla, Levi's and PUMA run retail intelligence (via tools like Increff) — we build the right-sized version for KDPS.

1 · The foundation — one spine of numbers BUILD FIRST

Everything else depends on this, so it comes first. Three read-only parts, all buildable by one developer.

PartWhat we buildWhy it matters
(a) Canonicalization crosswalk Map raw brand codes → one canonical brand; normalise season labels to one cohort key; quarantine impossible rows (cost > MRP). Source data untouched — analytics reads a clean view over it. Dirty data poisons margin & forecasting ("garbage in, garbage out"). Not throwaway — it becomes D8's head start.
(b) Semantic / metrics layer Define each KPI once (SQL views + a small metric dictionary in the app — no heavy tool needed). Derived-profit logic lives here, once. Kills "your sell-through ≠ my sell-through". dbt 2026 test: NL-query accuracy 84% → 100% when routed through a semantic layer; failures become honest error messages, not wrong numbers.
(c) Data-trust score Show coverage on every figure — "92% of LP units mapped to a clean season cohort; 8% unclassified — tap to see". A shown confidence beats a false-precise total. Turns dirty data into a visible, improving KPI ahead of D8.

Sources: dbt — semantic vs text-to-SQL · RudderStack — entity resolution · Profisee — survivorship · lakeFS — coverage as a metric.

Sequencing flag for the architecture
  • Master-data consolidation is D8 — after this layer.
  • But analytics can't wait for clean masters — the crosswalk copes now and seeds D8.
  • So the foundation slice is likely one of the earliest things built, even though D7 is "last" in the discussion order.

2 · The KPI canon — the numbers to standardise

Define these once in the spine. For a multi-brand retailer the centre of gravity is the brand × store matrix; the master inventory metric is GMROI — but sliced by commercial model (see §4).

Two things to remember
  • Vanity to avoid: all-time totals, raw footfall, and the dangerous one — gross sales without margin (SOR looks huge but earns only commission). Test any metric: can it lead to an action?
  • Cold-start: sell-through, weeks-of-cover, ageing, dead-stock %, realisation, gross margin all compute day one. LFL / GMROI-trend / full-price sell-through need ~one clean season. Conversion needs footfall hardware — later.

3 · The daily owner digest — an exception feed, not a report

For owners who live on WhatsApp, the digest is the product. The key lesson comes from alerting practice: ~97% of alerts are noise, and noise trains people to ignore the 3% that matter. The craft is subtraction.

The discipline

  • The actionability gate: if the owner can't take a specific action, the alert shouldn't exist. (Target 30–50% alert-to-action.)
  • Thresholds vs comparison: static thresholds for hard states (₹0, terminal down); relative-to-same-weekday/last-year for sales — retail is too seasonal (EOSS, Diwali) for a flat "down 20%" rule.
  • Volume floor: so tiny stores don't false-trigger on noise.
Severity tierKDPS examplesWhere it goes
SEV1 — ping nowStore at ₹0 by mid-afternoon · payment terminal down · cash/shrink spikeReal-time; escalates if unacknowledged
SEV2 — digestStore well below target · fast-mover stockout · offer mis-applied · return-window deadline nearBundled into the 9 PM digest, ranked, capped ~5
SEV3 — weeklyDead-stock ageing · slow margin driftMonday deep-dive (keeps the daily short)

The 9 PM digest — what's in it (fits one phone screen)

  • 1 narrative line — today's revenue vs target/yesterday/last-year + the one driver (LLM phrases it, in Hindi/English).
  • ≤5 headline numbers — in Lakh format.
  • Store leaderboard — top 3 / bottom 3.
  • "One unusual thing today" — a single anomaly line.
  • ≤5 SEV2 exceptions — each reply-to-drill.
  • Footer — "reply [store] for detail".

Delivery & roles

  • Channel: WhatsApp Business API via a Meta-authorised India provider (AiSensy / Wati / Interakt); scheduled sends use approved templates (~₹0.85/conversation). INDIA
  • Primary beat: the 9 PM close the client asked for. Later: a one-line 9 AM nudge + the Monday deep-dive.
  • Role scope from day one: owner sees the network; a store manager sees only their store.
The trap to design against
  • The urge will be to stuff the 9 PM message with everything the system can compute.
  • Every source says the opposite — the value is in what you leave out. Hold the line: ≤5 numbers, ≤5 exceptions, 1 anomaly line.
  • Free-form "ask anything" is deferred until the semantic layer can ground it (an ungrounded bot returns confident wrong numbers).

Sources: incident.io — SRE alerting · OneUptime — severity tiers · WA.Expert — live India digest bot · AiSensy — WhatsApp API providers.

4 · Profitability & margin — the same number means different things per model

KDPS manages 40 brands under four ownership models — that's the analytical heart. Profit is derived (frozen ex-GST cost × POS revenue). Stop at gross / contribution margin; net profit stays in Tally (net needs store opex that lives there).

The key insight: GMROI is meaningless for SOR / Consignment

  • GMROI's denominator is your inventory cost. For SOR/Consignment that's ~zero → GMROI undefined/infinite, which flatters non-owned goods.
  • So report margin per model, on its own terms (table below).
  • Hard dashboard rule: never rank an SOR brand on GMROI beside an owned brand — segment by ownership first.
ModelOwns stockHeadline metricWhy
OutrightKDPSGMROI + full margin ₹KDPS cash at risk → return-on-inventory
Correction (25-18-10)KDPSGMROI + state-tagged realisationOwned, but margin% depends on rate-state — tag normal vs EOSS vs return
SORBrandMargin% · sell-through · margin-₹-per-shelf-slot (not GMROI)No capital at risk → "worth the space?"
ConsignmentBrandSame as SOR + settle-on-sale margin ₹Commission vs space/staff occupied

The margin-leakage waterfall — turn "low realisation" into an answer

  • Start at MRP value, subtract each step: MRP → −markdown → −free-gift (GWP) → −returns → net pocket.
  • So instead of "Brand X has low realisation", the owner sees: "lost 14 pts — 9 markdown, 3 gifts, 2 returns".
  • Real waterfalls hit 30–40% erosion; making it attributable is how mature teams stop hidden leakage.
  • KDPS's GWP gifts (valued at MRP) and returns each get their own step — never buried in COGS.

The scorecard — keep KDPS's two-axis instinct, upgrade it

  • The Vaishnavi analysis already scored on Realisation % × Sales÷Stock — that's the merch-native Margin × Velocity quadrant the literature endorses.
  • Upgrade 1: velocity axis = GMROI for owned / velocity for SOR.
  • Upgrade 2: render owned vs SOR as separate boards (the Y-axis means different things).
  • Upgrade 3: add margin-₹-per-shelf-slot — the one metric fair across all models.
  • Upgrade 4: score stores on peer-percentile, not absolutes (a small store isn't judged against a flagship).
  • Quadrants: Lean-in · Defend · Churn · Pull-back. One click to the leakage waterfall.

Sources: NetSuite — consignment economics · ConsignCloud · Zilliant — margin leakage · Retalon — peer-percentile scorecards.

5 · Dead-stock & markdown — mostly already designed

The most-decided piece — full sourced brief in markdown-ai-research.html, direction set in Offers §7. The short version, in one place:

The markdown recommender

  • Signals in: age · sell-through-vs-target · weeks-of-cover-vs-remaining-window · broken size runs.
  • Out (a package, not a number): a single % snapped to an allowed ladder · expected sell-through/margin · a 2–3 step step-down plan · a priority list of which lots first · a plain-Hindi reason.
  • Hard cap: a margin floor from the PT cost a suggestion can never breach.
  • Path: crawl (rules now) → walk (add forecasting) → run (ML elasticity, 2–3 seasons away). Rules never disappear — ML wraps them.
  • Always: AI suggests → a human authors the offer → it goes live (the Oracle pattern).

The dead-stock hunter — KDPS already has the worked template (Vaishnavi)

  • Classify by season-cohort age: truly dead = 2024-&-older never-sold · aging = last-season unsold · fresh = current (normal — don't condemn it).
  • Match dead at exact size×colour barcode; value at frozen cost.
  • Action per commercial model: SOR/Consignment → return before the window closes · owned & selling elsewhere → inter-store transfer · not selling anywhere → markdown/clearance.
  • Past the window the brand won't take back: the V-flip settles it as "sold under EOSS" and flips ownership to KDPS.
  • Every output is a suggestion a human confirms — the client's "auto-list to return / Create Return" wording must stay propose-not-execute.

Full sources (Increff, Oracle, Umbrex, Eightx, Filene's Basement) in markdown-ai-research.html.

6 · Forecasting & replenishment — build the boring thing well

At KDPS's data maturity the winning move is rate-of-sale + comparison + analogy + rule-based reorder/rebalancing — not ML. Benchmarks confirm simple methods beat deep learning on sparse, intermittent retail data.

Cold-start forecasting (mostly-new products)

  • Forecast by analogy: borrow the demand pattern of similar past styles (cluster by brand × category × price-band × colour-family × season — rule-based, not ML).
  • Blend in real sales: analogy at launch → shift to the item's own rate-of-sale by ~2–3 weeks → trust its own trajectory by ~8 weeks.
  • Honest accuracy (put in writing for the client): new products ~40–55%; established basics climb to ~75–85% only after clean seasons. Measure in WAPE, not MAPE.
  • Year 1 goal: "narrow the decision", not "predict the number".

Size curves — the highest-ROI, most under-rated piece

  • Forecast total style demand, then split to size with a curve (each size's share), built from "clean weeks" and rebuilt every order cycle.
  • Build it per region (Bihar vs Jharkhand) — curves vary by geography.
  • Broken size runs cost apparel retailers up to ~20% of profit: core sizes stock out, fringe sizes become markdown fodder.
  • Make it a first-class trigger: flag a lot once core sizes are gone, even if overall sell-through still looks fine.

Replenishment & inter-store rebalancing — automate the gut call

  • Reorder on weeks-of-cover-driven min/max, per-store, recomputed on live sell-through (static thresholds fail in fashion).
  • Rebalance stock trapped in a slow store to where demand is live — prioritised by recent sell-through, assortment gap, excess cover. (A documented case: +28% bestseller availability, −15% surplus.)
  • New-store / new-arrival allocation falls back to a comparable store's pattern — the right feed for Inbound's "AI store split".
Defer honestly
  • Weather feeds (+~2% topline, real but v2 — season/calendar already captures most).
  • Promo-uplift modelling (needs control data you lack — start with a descriptive "sales during offer vs baseline", no causal claim).
  • SKU-level intermittent-demand tuning & true elasticity (wait for 2–3 clean seasons).
  • All forecasting accuracy is conditional on the canonicalization clean-up — without it, forecasts "do more harm than good".

Sources: OnePint — new-product forecasting · Eightx — size curves · Onebeat — rebalancing · arXiv — simple beats deep.

7 · The anomaly, fraud & leakage watchdog — flag & rank, never block

The field is "Exception-Based Reporting" — always flag-for-a-human, never block; rules first, statistics later, all maths in deterministic code. India tilt: UPI is self-logging, so leakage concentrates in hand-keyed discounts and the cash-to-bank-deposit gap.

Day-one rules (zero history needed)

  • Discount leakage — the flagship: every bill where billed total ≠ offer-rulebook total → list the gap, rolled up per store / brand / cashier. No statistics — just expected price vs keyed price. Plugs the placard hole directly.
  • Cash deposit: deposit < system-expected cash beyond a small tolerance → count breaches per store/month → "Store X under-deposited 3×". (Signal = persistence, not a single miss.)
  • Returns / voids / overrides: over-threshold refunds · returns with no bill reference · voids after partial completion · manual discounts beyond any active offer · repeated round-number cash bills · after-hours sales.
  • Data errors (first-class): cost > MRP · duplicate bills (POS-hang double-record) · brand/season drift · in-transit transfers open > N days · negative stock still negative after the sync window (transient self-healing negatives are known noise).

Month-3+ statistical layer, then human judgement

  • Once ~8–13 weeks of clean data exist: z-score / std-dev bands on per-cashier & per-store rates.
  • Peer-cluster ranking (3–6 comparable stores; cashier-vs-store, store-vs-region).
  • Seasonal / day-of-week baselines, refreshed quarterly, so EOSS/festival spikes don't false-trigger.
  • Isolation forest — optional later catch-all; supplements rules, never replaces them; every flag carries a reason code.
  • LLM may narrate a flag ("billed ₹X, offer ₹Y, gap ₹Z") — never computes the score.
A flag is a suspicion, not evidence
  • "12 voids on a shift is a red flag — but maybe 12 customers changed their minds."
  • KDPS has no CCTV → the human review step is the verification. The flag → review → confirm/dismiss loop is non-negotiable, and outcomes feed back to tune thresholds.

Sources: Agilence — EBR catalogue · apg — cash/deposit recon · Spot AI — suspicion not evidence · SmartBench — maths to deterministic tools.

8 · Conversational AI & narration — the LLM talks, it never calculates

Where the client's "wow" meets reality. Near-unanimous consensus: deterministic code computes; the LLM only translates the question in and explains the validated answer out. A peer study: hybrid 87% vs LLM-only 63%.

Ship now (safe)Defer
  • LLM writes the digest's plain-Hindi/English line from computed numbers.
  • "Why did this move?" — narrates the rows the code pulled.
  • Translates reports to Hindi (frontier models keep Hindi+English ~67–79% correct — fine to narrate).
  • Free-form "ask anything" NL query — 10–20% accuracy on a dirty schema with no semantic layer; a wrong number kills trust on contact.
  • When added: route semantic-layer-first, text-to-SQL only as a labelled "unverified" fallback — never for owner numbers.

Governance (matches "AI never posts to a ledger")

  • Confidence thresholds → an honest "not enough data" instead of a guess.
  • Tamper-evident audit trail of every AI suggestion + the human's accept/reject.
  • LLM kept physically unable to compute or write (the Power BI "can't cross semantic boundaries" design).

Sources: TDS — hybrid AI · BlazeSQL — NL-to-SQL cliff · MS — Copilot boundaries · arXiv — Hindi/English accuracy.
