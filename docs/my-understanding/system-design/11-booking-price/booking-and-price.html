KDPS · D11 · Booking & Price · The Commitment-to-Ticket Module

Booking & Price — Consultation & System Design

Two halves of one decision. Booking is where KDPS commits money it has not yet spent — a brand's collection ordered six to eight months before it can be sold. Price is where KDPS decides what that money comes back as — the ticket on the piece, the discount that comes off it, and the net the till actually collects. Everything in between (cost, GST, margin, season) is already built and is not restated here; this document is about the four moments where a human decides a number, who that human is, and who has to countersign. Companion to Vendor & Booking, Offers and Outbound.

§0 · Standing on

What was ruled on 3 August 2026

Five questions went to Anand before a line of this was designed. The answers are load-bearing — everything below is built on them, so they are quoted first and never re-litigated silently.

#QuestionRuling
R1Which "Booking"? The word is overloaded in our own glossary.Vendor/brand booking — the order placed with the brand six to eight months ahead. The customer-side set-aside is a different animal and is out of scope here.
R2Who is an "Advertisement user"? No such role exists in the nine-role table.A marketing / promo person who authors offers and publishes them to stores — the digital replacement for the placard that goes up on the shutter. A tenth persona, ratified today.
R3Is a brand-funded discount reclaimed from the brand, or is it lost margin?Neither framing is wanted. Stock owned by the brand → the brand decides the discount (the combinations are already written down). Stock owned by KDPS → KDPS administers the discount. Margin is simply different on-sale and off-sale. Nothing is "lost"; stop carrying that word.
R4Month-end: build the date/bill-number reshuffle that report-offer.md describes?No. The report states sales exactly as billed. KDPS optimises before the sale, never after. The one thing to build is the honest finder: eligible discount that the counter failed to apply, surfaced on the true bill, on the true date.
R5Scope of this round?Design and build. "I actually want to complete the module" — with the role-by-role process map for every persona.
Consultant's note on R3. The ruling is the right one and it simplifies the build considerably, so it is worth saying plainly what it settles: ownership decides authority. The system already knows who owns a piece — cost_book on every sale line says own or vendor, and a V-flip is the document that moves a piece from one book to the other. That single fact is now also the answer to "who is allowed to set this discount", which means the rule does not need a second, hand-maintained flag that can disagree with the books. One fact, two uses, no drift.
§1 · The idea

The price of a piece is decided five times, by five different people

Every price complaint in a retail chain — "why did the shop sell it at that?", "why is our margin down this month?", "who allowed this?" — is really a question about which of these five moments went wrong. The module's whole job is to make each of the five a document with an owner, a date and a countersignature, so the question has a one-screen answer instead of a phone call.

#MomentWho decidesWhat is fixed there, and what it costs to be wrong
1Commit — the bookingBrand Manager proposes · Owner approvesHow many pieces of what, at what expected ticket, for which stores, six to eight months out. Wrong here and no discount policy on earth saves it: the season's markdown budget is decided the day the booking is signed, not the day EOSS starts.
2Cost — the PTWarehouse makes · Accounts postsLanded cost per barcode, frozen. Already built and immutable after posting (a reversal is its own document). This is the floor every later number must respect.
3Ticket — the MRPBrand's tag (branded) · KDPS formula + HO (own goods)The number printed on the piece and the legal ceiling for the sale. This is the half of the module that does not exist yet. Today it is a column that gets overwritten silently by the next PT.
4Discount — the offerPromo/Brand Manager authors · Owner or Brand Manager countersignsWhat comes off the ticket, for whom, where, for how long. The engine is built and deterministic; the authoring and the countersignature are what this round adds.
5Net — the billCashier, inside a cap · Store Manager above itWhat was actually collected. The only one of the five that is allowed to be a judgement call at the moment of sale — and therefore the only one that needs a hard ceiling and a named override.
Why five and not one big "pricing screen". Every ERP that tries to hold price in one place ends up with a screen only two people in the company are allowed to open, so the other forty ring those two. Splitting by moment of decision means each persona gets a small screen that answers exactly their question, and the audit trail assembles itself: the booking explains the buy, the PT explains the cost, the price history explains the ticket, the offer explains the discount, the bill explains the net. Five documents, one story per piece.

§3 · Booking

The commitment — six months of margin decided in one afternoon

A booking is the only document in this system that spends money on stock that does not exist yet. Everything downstream — how much EOSS the chain will need, how deep, on whose goods — is largely settled the moment it is signed. It therefore gets the shape every commitment document gets: a maker, a value, and a checker.

StepWhoScreenWhat happens
1Brand Manager (or Warehouse)/booking/newVendor, brand, season, destination store, lines (style · size · qty · expected MRP). A photographed or PDF order sheet can be dropped in and the agent drafts the lines — the human still owns every one.
2System—The booking's committed value is computed from its own lines (qty × expected MRP) as it is written — that part is already built. What was missing is anybody being shown it before saying yes, which is what the approval line below carries.
3Brand Manager/booking/:id → Send for approvalThe draft leaves the maker's hands and appears in the approval inbox with its value, brand, season and store spread on it.
4Owner (or HO Ops inside the band)/approvalsApprove → the booking becomes Booked and starts expecting goods. Reject with a reason → back to the maker. Maker may not be checker, enforced by the shared approval spine, not by the screen.
5WarehouseInbound / PTGoods arrive against the booking. Under-supply is normal: the booking is short-closed with a reason, which is already built.
6Store/booking (view)A store sees what is coming for it and when. Read-only, on purpose: a store does not commit the chain's money.
The gap this closes. The ratified access table already promises booking: approve to the Owner and operate to the Brand Manager — i.e. it already says "one places, another signs". The code has no such transition: a booking goes from draft straight to booked on its maker's own say-so. Either the table is wrong or the code is. The table is right; the code gets the gate.
What I am deliberately not building yet. Open-to-buy — a per-brand, per-season budget the booking screen checks against — is how the industry stops over-buying, and it is the single highest-value thing to add after this round. It needs last year's sell-through by brand and season to be trustworthy first. Building the budget before the history is trustworthy produces a number people learn to override. Parked, named, and sequenced: after this round
§4 · Price

The half that does not exist: a ticket with a history

Price is not a column. It is a master with a history, and almost every hard question about it is a question about a past value, not the current one.

The question that gets askedWhat it needs
"What is this piece priced at?"Current ticket per barcode, with its cost, its margin at full price, and whether it is allowed to be discounted at all. A column can answer this. Only this.
"What was it priced at on 14 March, when this bill was made?"Effective-dated history. Without it, last month's bill cannot be explained, a brand's monthly statement cannot be reconstructed, and a customer's return three weeks later is priced by whatever the column happens to say today.
"Who changed it, from what, to what, and why?"An append-only price-change trail: actor, timestamp, old, new, reason, and the document that caused it (a PT, or a deliberate re-ticket).
"May this person change it?"A rung, not a habit. Authoring a ticket is operate on Offers & Price; re-ticketing an already-selling piece is a named act with a reason, and below cost it is refused outright.

Two kinds of ticket, two different authorities

Kind of stockWho sets the ticketRules that follow
Branded (brand's tag on the piece)The brand. KDPS transcribes it from the PT.The tag is the ceiling and it is not KDPS's to move. A re-ticket is an exception, always deliberate, always with a reason. Selling above the printed figure is never possible in this system — the engine cannot produce a net above MRP.
KDPS-owned / non-brandedKDPS: landed cost → +1.1% → + category margin → rounded.The formula proposes; a human accepts. The floor is the landed cost, enforced in the database. A change is a document, so a season's re-ticketing can be read back later.
Legal edge, flagged not decided. In India the printed MRP is a ceiling under the packaged-commodities rules, and a revised retail price has to be declared without hiding the original. Our system already cannot exceed MRP, and the price-change trail is exactly the evidence a revision needs. Whether a physical re-tag needs both figures shown on the label is a question for the CA/legal, in the same way the corpus already gates other statutory choices. CA to confirm
§5 · Discount

Ownership decides authority

Per R3, there are exactly two families of discount and the difference is whose stock it is — a fact the books already hold.

FamilyWho decides itHow it enters the system, and what it may do
Brand-decided
stock owned by the brand		The brand's scheme, transcribed by the Brand Manager or the Promo userArrives as a placard, a WhatsApp image or a PDF. Transcribed into the rulebook in the brand's own words (the one-liner), scoped to that brand's barcodes, with the brand named as funder. KDPS does not deepen a brand's scheme on its own; it may decline to run it.
KDPS-decided
stock owned by KDPS		KDPS administration — Promo authors, Owner countersignsAnything the engine can express: flat %, slabs, buy-x-get-y, spend-and-save, gift, bundle. Bounded below by landed cost and by the category margin floor the Owner sets.

What is already built and must not be redesigned

The rulebook and its engine are done, deterministic, and covered by shared golden vectors so the till and the server agree to the paisa. Restating any of it in a new screen would be the one way to break it:

PieceBehaviour
Eight patternsflat %, flat ₹, slab %, slab ₹, buy-x-get-y-free, spend-and-save, gift-with-purchase, bundle price.
Three layersBrand scheme → chain scheme → counter discretion, applied in that order, never silently stacked: stacking is a property of the offer, stated by whoever authored it.
No-discount stockThe NOD/AMM flag on a SKU wins over every offer, always. This is a brand contract, not a preference.
EOSS modeA season moves Open → EOSS → Closed on a calendar; the markdown ladder steps down over the months of the run and the recommendation engine proposes each step from actual sell-through and cover.
Frozen once liveA live offer's commercial dials cannot be edited — it is stopped and a new one authored. The bill from yesterday must stay explicable by the rule that priced it.
The season sale is a run, not a day. "EOSS and discounts that run for months" is the reason the ladder exists: one offer object with a step-down schedule, rather than forty offers authored on forty mornings. The promo user schedules the run once; the engine picks today's rung from the calendar. This also means an approved future-dated run is safe to push to the tills early — the dates gate the pricing, so an offline till already has tomorrow's offer before the shutter goes up.

A defect found while reading the running system, not a design opinion. The offer write endpoint is gated at manage on Offers & Price. Exactly one persona holds manage there — IT Admin. So today, in a system whose access table hands offers to the Owner, the Brand Manager and HO Ops, the only seat that can write an offer is IT, and every rule in the running database was in fact seeded by code rather than authored by a person. That is why no authoring screen exists: nobody who would use one is allowed to. Authoring drops to operate, countersigning sits at approve, and manage keeps what it should have kept — configuration.
§7 · The two gates

Where a second person is required, and why exactly there

GateTrips onWho may clear itWhy here
The author gateEvery offer, before it may price a single billOwner or Brand Manager — exactly the two seats the ratified table puts at offers_price: approve. HO Ops plans the markdown and does not sign it; IT Admin holds manage and is deliberately not an approver, as it is not on any other family.An offer is a standing instruction to give money away at every till, for weeks. It is the highest-leverage document in the company per keystroke. One person authoring it alone is the definition of an unchecked commitment — and the spine that enforces maker ≠ checker already exists and is used by six other families.
The counter gateA keyed-in discount above the capStore Manager, by PIN, at the tillThis one must resolve in seconds with a customer watching, so it is a PIN at the counter rather than an inbox — but it is still recorded: who allowed it, how much, on which bill. Beyond a second, wider band it stops being a counter decision at all and goes to HO.
The booking gateEvery booking leaving draftOwner — the one business seat the table puts at booking: approveSee §3. The access table already promised it.
The re-ticket gateChanging the MRP of a piece already on the shelfoperate with a written reason; below landed cost, refused outrightA silent re-ticket is how a chain loses the ability to explain last month. A value-banded countersignature on top of the reason is the natural next rung. next round
§8 · Month-end

The honest engine (R4)

The corpus records an intention to move sale dates and bill numbers between months so that a brand's discount claim lands where it is most useful, kept under a threshold so it does not attract attention. Anand's ruling is that we do not build it. As the consultant asked to say what the industry does, here is the reasoning in full, once, so it never has to be re-argued.

Why notConsequence
It is a false commercial record.A statement to a counterparty, restated on a date the sale did not happen, under a bill number that belonged to something else, is not aggressive optimisation — it is a fabricated document used to draw money. It also makes the GST return and the brand statement disagree on the same sale.
It breaks the invariants everything else rests on.Gap-free bill numbering per store per year, an append-only ledger, and a stock movement that matches a bill. Reshuffling dates and numbers invalidates all three at once, and every reconciliation the system performs would then be lying with confidence.
It converts a one-off favour into a permanent liability.Once the mechanism exists in software, it is used by whoever holds the screen, for reasons nobody will remember, for years. There is no version of this that stays small.

What captures most of the same money, honestly

LeverHow
1Apply it at the tillThe engine already prices every eligible line automatically. The largest single leak is a scheme that existed on paper and never reached the counter — and that is an authoring problem, which §6 fixes by letting the business author rules at all.
2Find the misses this roundThe nightly check already flags every bill where the till's discount differs from the rulebook. Turned into a screen, that becomes "eligible discount not applied", by brand, by store, by month — claimed on the true bill, on the true date. This is the legitimate version of the same intent, and it is faster than a phone call.
3Design earlierDiscount is decided when the booking is signed and when the run is scheduled, not at month-end. The season calendar and the step-down ladder are where the months-long run is shaped.
4Move the offer window, never the saleIf a month needs more depth, the honest instrument is to schedule the next rung of the run a week earlier — a decision, countersigned, before the sales happen. Same effect on the month; nothing restated.

§A · Consultation

Questions I would still ask, with my recommendation attached

Each is a real fork where the corpus does not decide, and where guessing wrong costs a rebuild. My recommendation is stated so silence still produces a defensible system.

Q1 · Who owns the ticket for KDPS-owned goods — the warehouse making the PT, or head office?

Today it is whoever types the MRP column while making a PT. Recommendation: the formula proposes it at PT time and it becomes real there (the warehouse cannot invent a number, only accept one), but any later change is head office with a reason. Keeps the goods moving; stops the ticket drifting. awaiting your ruling

Q2 · Should a store manager be able to run a local offer?

Recommendation: no. A cap on keyed-in discount is enough local latitude; a local rule is how two branches of one chain end up with different prices on the same barcode on the same day, which is the fastest way to lose a customer's trust and a brand's. Local exceptions belong in the counter gate, where they are visible one bill at a time. awaiting your ruling

Q3 · Is the counter cap one number for the chain, or per role and per store?

Today: one chain-wide percentage. Recommendation: keep one number until there is evidence a store needs its own; make it per-role now (cashier tighter than manager) because that distinction is already in the access table and people expect it. Per-store is a policy row when someone asks. awaiting your ruling

Q4 · How wide is the Promo user's writ — every brand and every store, or a slice?

Recommendation: chain-wide authoring, because a promo calendar is a chain-level artefact, but every offer still needs the countersignature of someone answerable for that brand or that chain. Scope discipline through the approver, not through hiding brands from the author. awaiting your ruling

Q5 · Does the store have to acknowledge a published offer?

The placard it replaces was physically put up by a person. Recommendation: not yet — the engine applies the discount whether or not anyone read the board, so an acknowledgement is a management signal, not a control. Revisit if shops start telling customers something different from what the bill does. parked, cheap to add

Q6 · Gift-with-purchase: is the gift stock, and whose?

The engine can already promise a gift; the books must then lose a piece. Recommendation: a gift is always a real barcode leaving real stock at zero net, never a note on the bill — otherwise the shelf and the ledger part company at exactly the moment a brand's scheme is running. depends on the gift's own stock treatment

Q7 · A booking's value: at expected MRP, or at cost?

Recommendation: show both, approve on cost. Cost is the money committed; MRP is the sales potential. An approver signing a number should be signing the one that leaves the bank. awaiting your ruling

Q8 · What is the approval band for a booking?

Recommendation: start with "every booking needs a second person", because there are tens per season, not thousands, and the value of each is large. Introduce a band only if the inbox becomes a rubber stamp — that is a real signal, and it is measurable from the data by then. awaiting your ruling

Q9 · Can the EOSS ladder step itself down automatically?

Recommendation: the engine recommends and a human accepts, exactly as it does today. Automatic markdown is the one place where a wrong dial compounds daily across every store before anyone notices. Recommendation plus one click is nearly as fast and cannot run away. already the built behaviour

Q10 · Re-ticketing: does a physical label need both the old and new figure?

Statutory, not architectural — the system keeps the evidence either way. CA/legal

Q11 · Payments — fold in now, or reference?

Recommendation: reference now, build next. Bank/tender offers ("10% on HDFC credit cards") are a genuinely different animal: the trigger is the tender, so it resolves after the basket is priced, at the payment step, and it usually involves a settlement with the bank rather than with a brand. Bolting it onto the basket-time engine would blur a boundary the engine currently keeps clean. It is a well-understood next slice, not a gap in this one. next round

Q12 · Do we need a "no offers today" state a shop can trust?

Recommendation: yes, and it is free — the offers board should say "nothing running for this store today" in words, rather than showing an empty table that reads like a loading failure. Small thing; it is what stops the phone call. building it that way

What the industry does, and where we deliberately differ

§C · Findings

Defects found by reading the running system

FindingEvidence
F1Only IT can write an offerThe write gate is offers_price: manage; IT Admin is the sole holder of that rung. §6.
F2Three of the four demo offers discount nothingSeeded shapes bypass the strict serializer: a quantity offer with min_qty but no slabs, two spend offers with min_spend_paise but no slabs, and a buy-x-get-y whose buy/get sit in the reward instead of the trigger — which is why the screen reads "buy 0 get 0 free".
F3An approved future-dated offer could never startApproval left the rule at approved while the engine only reads live, and nothing promoted it on its start date. Publishing on approval fixes it: the dates gate the pricing, so live-with-future-dates is both correct and what an offline till needs.
F4A booking approves itselfThe access table promises booking: approve to the Owner; the model has no such transition. §3.
F5A ticket has no historyMRP is overwritten in place by each PT posting; yesterday's bill cannot be re-explained. §4.
