The billing screen, rebuilt to fit one screen Agreed — grilled 1-2 Aug 2026

The Billing tab works, but it reads like a form that grew: the page scrolls, the money card is a stack of boxes, and the two things a cashier needs every minute — the amount due and Save & Print — are the first to slide off the bottom. This document is the research and the proposed new structure. Functionality does not change; the frame it sits in does. When agreed, it supersedes the region placement in store-front-design.html §4 and enters the normal dev process.

1 August 2026 · research: 10+ commercial systems (Oracle Xstore, Dynamics 365, Shopify, Square, Lightspeed, Toast) + Indian POS (Ginesys, GoFrugal, Marg, Zoho) + open-source (Odoo, ERPNext) · full source notes in the change log entry

1 · What is wrong today

  • The page scrolls. The scan box and bill area sit at the top; the payment card, customer card, totals and every action button live below the fold. On a normal laptop the cashier bills on one half of the screen and pays on the other, with a scroll in between.
  • The money section is a form, not a till. Four equal input rows, a small "Still to pay", a separate "Cash received" and "Change" — nothing is visually the amount due, nothing helps the cashier avoid arithmetic.
  • Seven buttons of equal weight sit in one bottom row — the button that closes money (Save & Print) looks like the button that clears the screen (New bill).
  • Banners stack. Each alert pushes the bill further down.
  • The customer card can't help. Mobile is a free-text box; the system never says "this is Sunita, she was here in June."

2 · What the research says

Every serious POS, Western or Indian, commercial or open-source, converges on the same few rules. These are the ones that decide our structure:

The screen fills the viewport exactly once; only the item list scrolls. Only the bill's line list scrolls, under a sticky column header.

UPI on charge = a QR with a tiny state machine. Do not copy the product grid. Odoo/ERPNext give ~60% of the screen to a product picker because cafés pick from menus. An apparel till scans.The width goes to the bill grid. The existing type-to-search "did you mean" list stays as the failed-scan fallback.

3 · The proposed structure — one fixed frame, three bands

The whole tab, mid-bill. The outer frame never moves; only the line list (centre-left) scrolls. Proportions: bill grid ≈ 2/3, right rail ≈ 340px, footer ≈ 56px.

Band by band

BandWhat lives there, and why
Top strip (fixed)Bill number + sync light folded into one chip · the scan box, still the biggest input on the screen, still always focused · then the bill's lifecycle: Find a bill · Held bills (n) · Hold bill · New bill. These are "which bill am I on" actions, so they sit with the bill's identity, not with the money. Alerts (printer off, offers stale, second-window block) collapse into one line under this strip instead of stacking.
Work area (only the line list scrolls)Left ≈ 2/3: the bill grid. Sticky column header; the line list is the single scrolling region of the whole tab. Offer chips stay on the line. The "did you mean" suggestions and the not-in-system prompt render at the top of this area, never pushing the rail. Right ≈ 340px: the rail, fixed — payment card above, customer card below, both always visible (today's 264px rail is too narrow for tender rows plus chips; ~340px is the floor).
Bottom bar (fixed)The totals read left to right — Pieces · Gross · You saved · Tax included · Net (largest figure) — then Reprint and Save & Print (the one visually primary button on the whole screen) pinned at the right. Visible from the first scan to the last, exactly as asked.

The payment card, rebuilt

  1. "To pay ₹X" — the hero number, updating live as lines enter.
  2. Tender rows: Cash · Card · UPI — and these rows are split payment. Any combination, no separate "split" button or mode: put an amount on one row, the balance line shows what's left, put the rest on another. Tapping into an empty amount box pre-fills the remaining balance; typing over it splits further. Example: ₹4,848 bill → 2,000 on Card → tap UPI, it offers 2,848, overtype 2,000 → tap Cash, it offers 848 → customer hands ₹1,000 → green "Change to give ₹152". This is the Oracle/Zoho/ERPNext pattern; the research found the explicit "Split payment" button (Shopify, Square) is the weaker one — an extra step on every mixed payment, and Square's version voids all legs if the split takes over five minutes. Counter credit notes are retired by POS counter redesign grill Q3/Q3b.
  3. Quick chips under Cash: Exact, and two round-ups (next ₹100 / ₹500) — one tap records what the customer handed over. Ruled yes — §4 D-2
  4. One balance line, colour-coded: red "Still to pay ₹X" until covered, flipping to green "Change to give ₹X" the moment cash exceeds the bill. "Cash received" folds into the cash row rather than being a separate block.
  5. UPI row carries "show QR." Entering an amount and tapping it opens the charge card: the amount huge, a QR panel (mock today, hardware later), and the states Generating → Awaiting (Cancel · Check status) → Success (ref captured, bill proceeds) / Failed (retry or another tender) / Unknown ("can't reach the bank — check again"). Built behind a payment adapter with a mock, the same shape as the print adapter, so the hardware slice later touches no screen.
  6. Manager authorisation no longer covers discounts or credit notes. Its surviving counter role is the late-exchange window override, recorded on the bill.
  7. An exchange may never net in the customer's favour: the outgoing pieces must be equal-or-up, and Save & Print refuses a short exchange with a plain explanation (POS counter redesign grill Q3/Q3b).

The customer card, rebuilt

  • Mobile first. From the third digit, matching customers list directly under the field — name and mobile only. Enter (or tap) attaches. No visit count, last-visit date, spend, or other purchase history is shipped to the till.
  • Unknown number → "bill to a new customer" inline: name, nothing else. The bill still works with no customer at all.
  • GSTIN moves behind a "Business bill?" disclosure. It matters on a minority of bills; when opened it behaves exactly as today (state-code check, tax-kind banner).
  • The Customer master is now built: every till syncs all-KDPS mobile/name/GSTIN rows for offline lookup, while bills keep their text snapshots. Past bills are fetched only through the online, store-scoped bill search in counter return mode. Ruled: customer master, all-KDPS, synced — §4 D-3

What explicitly does not change

Everything under the skin: scan-first entry with local lookup, offers applying themselves, per-line salesman with last-picked default, the ambiguous-barcode season question, not-in-system manual lines, hold semantics, commit-then-print, offline queue and sync light, the second-window guard, the late-exchange-window manager PIN gate, paper re-entry mode, exchange legs riding the bill. This is a re-framing, not a re-build of behaviour.

4 · Decisions — ruled in the Phase 1 grill (1-2 Aug 2026)

The full record with Anand's reasoning: docs/features/billing-screen-revamp/grill-decisions.md. The substance:

5 · How this lands

  • This chat is exploration only. Once the decisions above are made and the structure is agreed, the revamp enters the normal dev process (feature-analyst onward) as a UI slice; the payment-card behaviours (pre-fill, chips, UPI states) and the customer lookup are its only functional additions.
  • On agreement, store-front-design.html §4's region table is updated to this frame (the change log there records the supersession) — the behaviour rules in §4 stand untouched.
  • The build has a house pattern to stand on: the app shell already pins the viewport, and the scan screens (GRN, Stock Count) already do fixed-header / scrolling-middle / fixed-footer. The billing frame is the same idea applied to a route.
