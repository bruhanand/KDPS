Design discussion D10 · Store front · Running record

The store front — running design record

The screen-by-screen design of what a store login sees, decided in conversation and recorded here as we go. Companion to pos-store-requirements.html (the requirements) in this folder. Store users are KDPS users — same login, same role matrix; there is no separate store app or separate user store.

Started 30 July 2026 · grows section by section as each screen is discussed

1 · The sidebar — ten sections, no subsections Decided 30 Jul

The store login's sidebar is a flat list of ten sections. No subsections in the sidebar, ever — anything that needs dividing divides inside the page, as tabs. Receive and Transfer stand as their own sections (revised 30 Jul, same day): they are the actions a store performs daily, and Receive is where the data gets true — the moment stock and its paper enter the system. (How the data gets true at Receive is a discussion of its own, parked for when we design that screen.)

SectionWhat lives inside (as in-page tabs where needed)
1 · DashboardThe store's day at a glance, plus the quick actions. Approvals and alerts appear here as cards (the bell stays in the top bar). Design in section 2 below.
2 · SellBilling. Return & exchange folds onto the counter: scan or type a bill number, or search the customer master by mobile/name and choose one of that customer's past bills. The final region/table placement waits for POS redesign slice 8 (#273).
3 · InventoryOne page, four tabs: Stock on Hand (grouped by item type, filtered by brand and season) · Damage & Quarantine · Count & Adjust (count sessions, adjustments) · Return to Brand.
4 · ReceiveOne page, tabs: GRN · Upload Bill · PT Files — the stock that came, the paper it came with. The daily action where the data gets true.
5 · TransferOne page, tabs: Send · Requests (incl. cross-store stock requests, section 3) · In-Transit · Movement History.
6 · BookingBookings list + detail; create only where the role allows.
7 · MoneySingle section. For a store: expenses and today's collections.
8 · Offers & PricingSingle section. Price list and the offers live in this store — read-only at the counter.
9 · ReportsEverything report-shaped: sales, stock, item-wise, member-wise, date-range, daily summary.
10 · HRMSAttendance; Member Details visible to the manager only.

Mapping from the current build: today's 13-section sidebar folds down — Home→Dashboard; Stock, Stock Count and Return to Brand fold into Inventory's tabs; Receive Goods→Receive and Transfer stay as they are; Setup disappears for store logins (RBAC already hides it). The nav already renders a one-link section as just the link, so each section is one URL.

3 · Search across stores → request the stock Decided 30 Jul

A store can search stock across all stores — a customer wants a size this store doesn't have; staff find it at a sister store and ask for it on the spot. Reachable from the dashboard's quick actions (and the top-bar search); the request lands in Transfer → Requests.

The flow

  1. Search. Staff searches the item across stores; results show which store holds it, size by size.
  2. Ask. From the search result, the store raises an approval request for a transfer.
  3. Own manager first. The request goes to the requesting store's store manager.
  4. Up to Operations Head. The store manager sends it on to the Operations Head — the standing rule that the Operations Head fronts every transfer holds.
  5. The conversation. The Operations Head talks to the holding store's manager (and to the brand manager where the call needs one). Whatever they conclude decides the request — the holding store's manager approves out of that discussion, or the Operations Head approves directly. There is no hard system rule that blocks a request; "possible or not" is this human judgment.
  6. Then the normal transfer. The holding store makes a transfer for the piece, it goes in-transit to the requesting store, and is received there by the same scan-based receive flow already built. Both stores watch it in their Transfer tabs.

The waiting customer gets a time, not a hold. No reservation is placed on the piece. The counter tells the customer how long the stock will take and asks them to come back — this evening, tomorrow, another day. The request should therefore carry an expected-arrival time the counter can quote; how that time is worked out (route history vs staff-entered) is settled when the Transfer screen is designed.

The approval chain is stored as data (an approval route), not hard-coded — Rule 12, variation is data not code. That keeps two doors open that Anand has already flagged: automating the chain later (steps 3–5 collapsing where trust allows), and the Operations Head's direct-approve short-cut today.

4 · Sell — the POS screen Decided 30 Jul

The most important screen in the store. Design brief: the skeleton is the current RetailJI screen (the four screenshots in current-pos/) so staff adapt in minutes, and the research below confirms that skeleton is the right one — the leading POS systems (Microsoft Dynamics 365 Commerce, Lightspeed, Oracle Xstore) all use the same anatomy: a scan/search input, a line grid, a glanceable total, a payment panel, a customer card, and an action row. We keep the shape and spend our effort where research says it pays: fewer interruptions, instant feedback, and everything one scan or one key away.

What the research says (and how it lands here)

FindingWhat it means for our screen
Cashiers work at roughly double normal speed, read the screen from ~80 cm, and split attention between screen, customer and queue.Big fonts on the numbers that matter; the bill total must be readable at a glance; no small click targets in the hot path.
Every extra step, screen or confirmation slows the line; validate only where it is critical.The hot path is scan → scan → payment key → done. No dialog boxes inside it. Confirmations only where money or stock could go wrong.
An error costs up to ten times the transaction time.Prevent instead of punish: auto-applied discounts, auto GST, auto salesman default — the cashier types almost nothing.
POS design must assume misconduct exists (documented cashier theft patterns).Voids, over-cap discounts and reprints are gated and logged with who/when — silently, without slowing the honest path.
Park / hold a sale is a standard feature of every major POS.We add Hold Bill — the current POS lacks it, and its absence is felt every time a customer runs for one more item.
Returns and exchange handled inside the same transaction is the industry norm.Confirms the current Return-tick-in-the-same-bill gesture we already decided to keep.
Layouts are assigned per register/user and kept configurable (Dynamics 365 model).Our screen definition lives as data per store/role where cheap to do — Rule 12 again.

The screen, region by region (the skeleton, kept)

RegionAs today, plus the deliberate changes
Top barDate · store · next bill number · a sync light (green = synced, amber = billing offline, with a count of bills waiting to sync). The one new indicator on the screen.
Scan box (top right, always focused)As today — plus it doubles as type-to-search: type a name/design no. and it auto-suggests, for the tagless or unreadable-barcode piece. Cursor always returns here after every action. Lookup is local: instant, offline, never a spinner.
Line grid (centre)Same columns staff read today: item, brand, barcode, design no., size, qty, rate (MRP), disc %, disc ₹, GST, salesman, amount, net. Discount and GST fill themselves (rulebook + slab master); an offer chip on the line names the rule that fired. Nothing to type on a clean sale.
SalesmanStays per line — but the popup defaults to the last-picked salesman; one keystroke to change, zero to accept. (Research: kill per-scan interruptions; the current every-scan popup is the one part of the skeleton that fights speed.)
Payment panel (right)Bill amount, large. Tender rows: Cash · Card · UPI, amounts splittable across them, must sum to the bill. Tendered cash + change at the bottom. The counter neither issues nor redeems credit notes (POS redesign grill Q3/Q3b).
Customer strip (bottom)Mobile number first, then name and optional GSTIN; the bill works without any of them. The till reads the synced, all-KDPS Customer master for its mobile/name typeahead. After Save & Print accepts a bill, that bill feeds the master; the bill itself keeps its text snapshot. In return mode, the same mobile/name search finds the customer's past bills.
Totals stripQty, gross, "you saved ₹X" (the offers' visible sum — staff quote it to the customer), net. Return-voucher no. + amount when an exchange is in the bill.
Action rowSave & Print (the one big button, locks + spins while working) · Print/Reprint · Hold Bill · Search / Find bill · New Bill. F-keys are accelerators, never the only door (supersedes the 31 Jul no-keyboard ruling; POS redesign grill Q4): F2 Hold · F3 Look up / Find bill · F4 New bill · F9 Pay & Print · Esc back to scan/dismiss error · Enter for the next bill on the finish overlay. Buttons carry no printed key label; hover shows the shortcut, and handlers use preventDefault.
Return & exchangeThe counter's mode toggle owns the flow. It finds the original bill by receipt barcode, typed number, or Customer-master mobile/name search; picked lines carry reason and condition, and new pieces scan into the same exchange. The equal-or-up gate refuses a short exchange. The final shipped-frame region table lands after slice 8 (#273).

Behaviour rules (the invisible part)

  • Scan is instant and offline — local item/price/offer/stock data; stock under the barcode shows at scan, exactly as today's "Total Stock" readout.
  • Offers apply themselves as lines enter; the Offer Detail area lists what fired, per brand. Manual discount within the role's cap is a keystroke; over the cap, the bill will not close without a manager's OK, recorded on the bill.
  • Ambiguous barcode (several live seasons): one-tap "which season?" — the only question the screen is ever allowed to ask mid-sale, and it never blocks billing.
  • Printer checked before Save & Print; if it is off, the bill still saves and the screen says so, with Reprint one tap away.
  • Saved means saved: append-only, reprint only, corrections by reversal — the store's own rule.
  • Hold Bill parks the cart locally (name/phone optional), retrieve from a one-tap list; held bills are till-local, never touch stock or money until saved, and show on the Dashboard. At day close they are flagged and the store chooses: keep (the bill carries over and reprices at retrieval) or expire. Nothing expires silently. (Refined 30 Jul, grill Q13; a multi-day set-aside is a Booking, not a hold.)
  • Nothing on this screen shows cost or margin, and it sees only this store's data (the cross-store search is its own, read-only flow).

Research sources: Microsoft Dynamics 365 Commerce POS screen-layout documentation (transaction-screen anatomy: receipt panel, totals panel, customer card, number pad, button grids, per-register/user layout assignment); Creative Navy's POS UX guide (the 16 UX factors: time pressure, 80 cm viewing distance, attention splitting, error cost, misconduct, training under stress); Bright Inventions and Hashmato on payment-flow speed and validation economy; Shopify POS UI principles; Lightspeed and Deal POS on park/retrieve; Dynamics 365 and StoreConnect on same-transaction returns/exchange.

5 · The grill — engineering & policy decisions Decided 30 Jul

The feature entered the dev process (docs/features/pos-store-front/). The Phase-1 grill closed every open question in fourteen decisions, validated by four research passes (B2B/GST at the counter, goods-on-approval, offer tie-breaking, offline-first POS architecture). The full record with sources is docs/features/pos-store-front/grill-decisions.md; the substance:

DecisionRuling
Bill numbering offlineThe till owns its store's gap-free counter in persistent local storage; the server verifies continuity at sync. A dead till = a register handover: resume from the last synced number, re-enter unsynced bills from their printed copies (the paper-bill F3 muscle).
Commit pointSave & Print. Bill final locally (number, stock, print), synced immediately or queued with minute-level retry; server accepts any order; every bill idempotency-keyed and stamped origin: offline/online.
Reference-data syncOffers, items, prices, slabs and the Customer master auto-pull whenever online; the "offers live" notification informs, never gates. Offer start/end dates ride inside the data, so a stale till can miss a new offer but never applies a dead one.
Credit notesRetired at the counter by POS redesign grill Q3/Q3b (2 Aug 2026). Nothing issues or redeems one; historical rows remain append-only history.
Sold-before-inwardBill now, post when priced: an unknown scan bills as a manual line (MRP from the tag); its postings wait in the exception queue until GRN/PT prices the piece. Sale never blocks, nothing posts at zero. GST side stays CA-gated.
Customer return (D-4)Superseded by POS redesign grill Q3/Q3b: no standalone/plain return and no refund. A return exists only inside an exchange tied to the original bill, with reason + condition per line; outgoing value must be equal or higher, or Save & Print refuses to close.
B2B corner (D-5)GSTIN + name at the till, nothing more; the till derives CGST/SGST-vs-IGST from the GSTIN's state code. B2B bills feed an HO queue with a hard 30-day IRN clock (mandatory at KDPS's turnover). Party A/C, Cess tab, dynamic QR: dropped.
F10 Approval/Order (D-6)Almost certainly goods-on-approval (challan out, invoice on acceptance, 6-month deemed-supply clock). V1 ships without it; the stores are asked now; a ready design (non-financial Approval document, Convert-to-Sale / Return-to-shelf) follows fast if confirmed.
Offer tie-break (D-7 / O3)Three layers: one brand offer per item by best-deal-wins (B2G1: dearest-first, cheapest free); storewide then bank offers stack only by explicit flag; new offers default non-combining; same cart always prices the same, and every line records which rule won.
Sidebar & accessThe ten-section sidebar is a presentation fold — the 13 permission keys underneath don't change; tabs stay gated by their original codes. The role matrix becomes admin-editable data (Setup screen, it_admin/superadmin) with the four money floors hard-coded and every change audit-logged.
Store targetsA small HO master, one monthly target per store, set by the Operations Head; the edit right is itself a matrix cell.
Build orderOffline-first from day one (research overturned online-first-then-retrofit): sidebar+Dashboard → cross-store request → the till as one supervised money slice → the offer engine (pilot waits for it) → customer search + equal-or-up exchange on the counter → Cash Summary + the daily reconciliation gate. Riders: till standardised on Chrome (installed PWA — Safari evicts local data after 7 idle days), and an early receipt-printer hardware spike.
