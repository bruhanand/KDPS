Offers — System Design

One tidy place where every brand's offer is written the same way — so the shop can apply the right discount on its own, managers can control big discounts, and at month-end we know exactly how much margin each brand's discounting cost. A discount just lowers the selling price (KDPS earns less margin); nothing is reclaimed from the brand. Feeds the counter sale in Outbound.

Stage: offer grill complete — 13 questions locked (18 Jun 2026). The model (Q1–Q13) is settled: one common offer form (trigger + reward + dials), item-level in a multi-brand bill, brand + KDPS offers, AI-suggested clearance for owned stock, auto-apply via a swappable POS adapter, two approval gates, daily reconcile + month-end brand pack. A discount only lowers the selling price → KDPS earns less margin; nothing is claimed back from the brand (corrected 18 Jun). Client confirmation doc: 04-client-docs/Offers-Module-Client.html.

1 · The one idea

Every offer — no matter the brand or how messily it's written — is the same simple shape:

What the customer must do  →  what they get

"Spend ₹6,999" → "get ₹600 off". "Buy 3 shirts" → "cheapest one free". "Spend ₹23,000" → "free trolley bag". Once every offer is written in this one shape and kept in one place, the shop till can apply it automatically, and we can check and report it later. The patterns below are just the different ways the two halves get filled in.

Locked (Q1, 18 Jun): one common form for every offer. An offer is stored as a trigger (what must happen) + a reward (what they get) + its dials (§3). The eight patterns are just trigger+reward combinations, so a new pattern is a new choice — never a rebuild. In plain terms:
Trigger — "when…"Reward — "…give"
nothing (just applies) · spend ≥ ₹X · buy N items · buy from a chosen group / category · later a coupon, a card, a member% off · ₹ off · an item free (cheapest) · a fixed price · a gift · later points, cashback
Offers attach to items, not the bill (multi-brand store). KDPS is a Shoppers-Stop-style multi-brand store, so one bill can carry many offers at once — each item gets its own offer (a Mufti item → Mufti's offer; a Blackberry item → Blackberry's) and a store-wide KDPS discount can sit across everything. Discounts apply per item as it's scanned at the till and total up on the bill. A "spend ≥ ₹X" offer measures the spend over its own scope's items (that brand's lines), then splits its reward back across them. Locked (Q6, 18 Jun — Anand)

4 · EOSS is a mode, not a new pattern

End-of-season sale (cleared around August and February) uses the same patterns above — usually a straight percent off or buy-more-bigger-percent — but with three things switched on: it's deep, it's tied to a season, and it's often KDPS-decided (clearing our own old stock) rather than the brand's. So we treat "EOSS" as a label/mode on an offer, not a ninth pattern. The brand sheets confirm this — Libas literally tags rows "Promo" vs "EOSS".

The design rule this sets. Because the pattern list will grow, the rulebook can't be a fixed menu of eight hard-coded types. It's built as an open model — a new pattern is added as a new rule type (config), not a code rewrite. Start live with the eight; the rest are pre-cleared to join.

6 · Two sources of offers: brand vs KDPS

The eight patterns are the same for everyone. What differs is only who decides the discount — the money effect is the same either way: KDPS earns less margin, nothing is reclaimed.

Brand offerKDPS offer
On what stockBrand-controlled goods (SOR / Consignment, and brand-run schemes on Correction / Outright).KDPS-owned goods — Outright & Correction stock, and dead stock flipped to KDPS.
Who decidesThe brand — they send the offer (photo / sheet / PDF).KDPS decides, freely and whenever it wants.
Money effectKDPS earns less margin on the sale. Nothing is reclaimed from the brand.KDPS earns less margin on the sale (its own choice).
How it's createdRead the brand's offer into the rulebook (seamless ingest), then apply.KDPS authors it directly — often from an AI suggestion (§7).
At month-endNo claim. The discount-by-brand total feeds the brand performance report (margin attribution).Same — tracked as KDPS's own margin hit in the report.

The same goods can see both over time: a brand runs its in-season offer; later, once the stock is old (or flipped to KDPS), KDPS runs its own clearance offer. So the split isn't about the item — it's about who decides this particular discount (the "who decides" dial in §3). The money effect is the same either way: less margin, no reclaim.

Where offers ends and payments begins (Q7 — corrected 18 Jun). A discount is not a claim against the brand. It simply lowers the selling price, so KDPS earns less margin — that's the whole money effect (profit is derived: cost from the PT/settlement, revenue from the discounted sale). Nothing is reclaimed. The split is only about who decides the discount: brand-controlled stock → the brand sets its discount scheme (KDPS applies it, earns less margin); KDPS-controlled stock → KDPS decides its own. Each line still records a funder tag, but only to attribute the margin in the monthly brand report — never to bill the brand. Payment to the vendor is separate and on the vendor's terms (the four commercial models). The real brand-money flows live in Payments (D4) and are unchanged: SOR/Consignment payable on sale, RTV defective credit, EOSS/V-flip settlement, goods-return DN.

7 · The AI helper for KDPS-owned stock read-only · suggests, never sets

On the brand side, offers arrive ready-made. On the KDPS side, nobody should have to spot old stock by hand. An AI helper watches every lot of KDPS-owned stock and proposes a discount — with reasons — for a person to approve. It only ever suggests; a human reviews, adjusts, and creates the actual offer. Nothing changes price on its own (architecture Rule 8).

What it watches

How old the lot is · how fast it's selling (sell-through) · how many weeks of stock are left vs how much season is left · broken size runs (only odd sizes left) · the cost floor (never suggest a price below an agreed margin floor).

What it proposes — a package, not just a number

Which lots to act on first · a suggested discount as a min–max band snapped to sensible steps (not a random %) · the best pattern to use · the expected result (roughly how much will clear, and the margin hit) · a step-down plan ("20% now; 30% in two weeks if it's still slow").

Example: "FM jackets, SS-25, 120 days old, selling slow — suggest 30–40% off, expect to clear ~60% in 3 weeks, floor ₹X (cost). [Create offer]".

Honest on the "AI agent" word. The dependable core is classic maths (optimisation) plus a human approving — that's what the mature tools (Increff is the most India-relevant) actually do. The "agent / LLM" part is the language layer: plain-English explanations, "what if we did 25%?" questions, and summaries — useful and worth having, but it never sets prices by itself. An AI that prices fully on its own is mostly hype today. Full research + sources: markdown-ai-research.html.
Where it lives. The suggestion engine sits in the intelligence layer (the D7 analytics discussion) and feeds this rulebook: AI suggests → a human authors the offer here → it goes live like any other → the result feeds back so the suggestions sharpen.

8 · Getting offers to the till, and approval

Auto-apply (Q8): the offer engine is the single brain. It sits behind one swappable POS adapter with two modes:

  • Until our counter exists: the manual bill is the Sale of record; basket offers (spend ≥ ₹X, buy-2-get-1) are reconciled against the rulebook at end of day.
  • Target (KDPS-built POS): push the full rules; the till applies them live as each item is scanned, and enforces approval at the counter.

Approval (Q9) — two gates:

  • Creating an offer: beyond a set depth, an offer needs a named approver before it goes live — so every offer, including the AI's suggestions, has a human sign-off (Rules 8 & 10).
  • At the counter: a manual discount or override beyond a cashier's limit needs a manager's OK before the bill closes.

The exact limits and the who-approves-what role grid are set in the roles / payments work (D4); the offer module just places the two gates.

What the research confirmed

  • The pattern menu is finite and known. Every KDPS offer maps to one of 8 standard families; nothing in their sheets falls outside it.
  • "Buy X get Y, cheapest free" is the universal default. Logic ERP and GoFrugal both ship it as "lowest rate free"; SAP/Oracle/Square offer "cheapest free". "Most-expensive free" is offered by essentially no one.
  • Two kinds of "slab" are first-class everywhere: by bill value (spend tiers) and by quantity (buy-more tiers). KDPS uses both.
  • A bill-level discount must be split back across the items on the bill — so per-item cost, profit and GST stay correct. This is the most important engine rule for us (matches the "documents write ledgers / profit is derived" rules).

Decision: B — stock-aware gift: SKU + token price; out → a pre-decided discount set per offer (confirmed by Anand 18 Jun — in stock → give the gift; out → the pre-set discount); counts only when handed over; leaves inventory. V-flip reuses an EOSS offer then flips to KDPS-owned (no new mechanic). A gift reward differs from a money discount — it's a real stock item, can run out, counts only if handed over. (A) simple: name gift + token price, cashier handles stock / fallback / substitution by hand. (B) stock-aware: offer names gift (a SKU) + token price; if out → fallback discount (the else= we already encode) or substitute gift (trolley→duffel); counts only when scanned/handed over; gift leaves inventory + shows on bill. (C) B + forecast gift demand to stock up (later). Rec: B — exactly today's behaviour. V-flip tie-in (not a new mechanic): the flip "sells" dead stock under an EOSS offer to settle with the brand, then the stock becomes KDPS-owned → KDPS offers + AI clearance apply. Grounded: kdps-offer else= fallback; GWP stock-fallback is a known build item; gift = stock item; handover check from the June-1 meeting.
Q12 · How offers get created (ingest / authoring) Locked — B (18 Jun)
Decision: B — AI-assisted ingest + human confirm (paste brand photo/PDF → AI drafts → person approves → live; reuses kdps-offer); manual entry stays for KDPS offers & one-offs. Brands send offers as messy photos / PDFs / rough sheets (every brand different). (A) manual entry only — reliable but slow, ignores the AI we have. (B) AI-assisted ingest + human confirm — paste the brand's photo / PDF → AI drafts the offer rule(s) (as the kdps-offer skill does) → a person reviews, fixes, approves → live; manual entry stays for one-offs & KDPS offers. (C) B + auto-pull from a structured feed if a brand ever sends one (later). Rec: B — AI reads the messy input, human decides (Rule 8); reuses the kdps-offer skill; manual stays. Grounded: kdps-offer parses promo images→Offer List; Rule 8 AI-at-edges; the authoring sign-off (Q9).
Q13 · Discount base (MRP) Locked — Yes (18 Jun)
Discounts figured on printed MRP, not an already-reduced price (India norm; reports work on MRP). Stacking base: an add-on (store-wide / bank / coupon) on top of a brand offer is computed on the already-discounted price ("extra 10%" = 10% of what's left), protecting margin. Rec: MRP base + add-ons on reduced price. Resolves O1.

Glossary (plain words)

Offer / rule
One discount written as "what the customer must do → what they get", plus its dials (brand, stores, dates, who pays…).
Slab
Steps in an offer — e.g. spend ₹7k→₹1k off, spend ₹13k→₹2k off. Can step by bill value or by number of items.
Cheapest free
In "buy X get Y free", the lowest-priced qualifying item is the one made free.
Brand-decided vs KDPS-decided
Who sets the discount — the brand (on brand-controlled stock) or KDPS (on KDPS-controlled stock). Either way KDPS just earns less margin; nothing is reclaimed from the brand. The tag is kept only for margin attribution in the monthly brand report.
Funder tag
A label on each discounted line (brand or KDPS) — used purely to attribute the margin hit in reporting, not to bill the brand. A discount is never a claim.
No-discount list (NOD / AMM)
Styles the brand forbids discounting (usually new stock); every offer must skip them.
EOSS
End-of-season sale (~Aug and ~Feb): a deep, season-tied clearance — a mode on the normal patterns, not a new one.
Split back to lines
When a discount is on the whole bill, share it across the items so each item's cost/profit/tax stays right.
