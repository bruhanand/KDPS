State machines & lifecycles — every status flow in one place

One reference for every document's life: the states it can be in, what moves it from one to the next, and who acts. The same machines a developer codes, the owner reads, and a reviewer checks. Nothing here invents new behaviour — it consolidates what D1, D3, D4 and D8 already locked, with the 16 Q&A decisions folded in.

Every lifecycle on this page is drawn the same way, so a state flow always means the same thing wherever it appears.

One rule sits under all of them. A state in KDPS is just "which open document is holding this thing, and what those documents add up to" — it is read from the spine, not stored as a loose flag that a human can flip. That is why these machines never drift: the booking's status is a sum of its GRNs, a piece's state is whichever document is holding it, a payable's status is the sum of its allocations.

1 · Booking D1 locked status derived from GRNs

The computation, exactly. Received quantity per line, summed across this booking's GRNs, against booked quantity: none received → Booked · some → Partially received · all → Fully received. Booking lines are style + size only (a booking posts to no ledger); full SKU grain begins at GRN/PT. "Arrived · not received" is no longer a booking status — arrival lives on the Shipment; the booking screen shows it only as live information from inbound.

2 · Booking-less direct receipt Q9 locked

Branded and non-branded goods routinely land at a store with no booking recorded. This is a first-class path, not an error (Q9). Booking is the normal path but never a hard constraint (26 Jun 2026): a receipt is never blocked for a missing booking, and the booking details can be attached / updated later (retro-link). The key insight: the Shipment was always the anchor of inbound, not the booking — so when there is no booking, the booking status machine above is simply not involved.

How it differs from the booking path

  • A Shipment with booking_ref = NULL anchors inbound. A "Receive without order" entry creates a simple-receipt GRN that posts the quantity (goods are sellable day one).
  • No phantom booking is created. A booking-less GRN is in no booking's GRN-set, so the booking machine has nothing to compute — there are no "two states at once", and no new states are needed. (Auto-creating a fake booking would pollute booked-vs-received analytics — forbidden.)
  • The commercial model is snapshotted at receipt/PT time from the effective-dated brand master (default Outright if the brand is known but the model is silent).
  • A Direct GRN can be retro-linked to a later booking. The retro-link re-runs the invoice ↔ GRN ↔ PT reconciliation as a correction (a new dated event), never a silent rewrite.
Two hard gates on this path (Q9 hardenings).
  • An unknown / defaulted model hard-gates D4 payment — the payable sits in an awaiting-invoice / unmatched-payable state until the model is confirmed.
  • A truly unknown brand → "Unconfirmed" model: post stock-in quantity + cost, but withhold the vendor-ledger posting (behave like SOR) until the brand is confirmed.
  • Capture the e-way-bill number on the direct GRN (the recipient must generate one if the vendor ships above the threshold without it).

3 · Shipment → GRN → PT — the two-step posting Q1 locked

Inbound is two postings, not one (Q1, the single rule that unblocks direct receipt and own-POS auto-inward). The GRN posts the quantity; the PT posts the value. This is the cross-ERP standard (SAP MIGO/MIRO, ERPNext Stock-Received-But-Not-Billed) and it is what lets goods sell on day one before they are priced.

"Eligible to pay" is a gate, not a state. A payable only becomes payable after it clears a tiered gate (e.g. tied to an inwarded PT/TIR; SOR/Consignment actually sold; vendor bank details verified). The checks run on the payable and are re-checked at execution — the world can change between approve and pay. A piece that hasn't sold cannot be paid for under SOR/Consignment: that is a hard block, and it is also where the booking-less unknown-model payable waits (Q9).

Vendor ledger (KDPS's party ledger) is itself derived: Opening + Invoices − CD − GR (goods return) − Payments + Interest = Due, at brand + vendor grain.

5 · Payment — the money leaving the bank D4 locked

A separate document that settles one or many open payables, carrying the approval + execution lifecycle. Maker ≠ checker ≠ payer, always — the raiser can't approve, the approver isn't the executor (Rule 10). This is the most-watched machine in the system: a wrong forecast wastes attention, a wrong payment moves a real rupee.

The handshake between the two machines. The link between a Payment and the Payables it clears is the allocation (exactly Tally's Agst Ref). A Payment reaching Confirmed is what drives its allocated Payables to Partly paid / Paid. Goods-return DNs net the payable now; commercial claims (EOSS / V-flip, Correction over-allowance) pend and net only at the agreed amount.

6 · The piece — stock state D3 locked derived from open documents

Before the movement machines (transfer, sale), one foundation: a piece's state is just "which open document is holding it" — so it can never drift the way a hand-flipped flag does. Stock is held per barcode + season + location.

Availablefree to sell
Reserved → In-transitheld by a transfer
Pending seasonal returnheld by a return doc
Quarantineheld by a defect ticket
Gonesold · returned to brand · received elsewhere
Free-to-sell = Physical on-hand − everything locked by an open document. Physical is the honest count; "locked" is whatever a transfer-in-flight, a defect ticket or a pending return is holding. The number pushed to POS is free-to-sell, so POS can never sell a piece that is already promised. Aging, dead-stock, the SOR window and the V-flip all run per cohort (barcode + season) — SS-25 never mixes with SS-26.

The V-flip is a state change in disguise: dead stock the brand won't take back is reported sold under EOSS, the brand settlement is claimed, and ownership flips brand-owned → KDPS-owned (real brand kept, owner=KDPS, status=liquidation, barcode unchanged) so it drops out of brand reports and sells off as ordinary KDPS stock.

The cross-state overlay (Q3). Whether a transfer also moves money and tax depends on the two GSTINs, read off the first two digits (state code) of each:
  • Intra-state move (same GSTIN / same state) → stock-ledger only, no tax event. "Internal move."
  • Cross-state move (Bihar ↔ Jharkhand, distinct persons) → legally a taxable IGST supply. Scope decision (26 Jun 2026): the system records the move and flags it as cross-state needing manual paperwork; the IGST tax invoice and e-way bill are produced manually by KDPS, outside the system (sender out-voucher + IGST tax invoice, receiver matching purchase voucher + ITC, e-way bill on consignment value incl. IGST — ₹50,000 threshold cross-state; ₹1,00,000 intra-state). The stock machine above is unchanged; the taxable-supply fact is kept for awareness/reporting.

8 · Sale / POS sync Q8 locked

The sale itself is simple — scan, bill, post (stock out, cash in, SOR sold-liability). The hard part is getting an offline-first till's sale to the server exactly once, even over a bad network. The KDPS-built POS and a manual bill write the same source-agnostic Sale document; only the sync machine differs. (The counter is ours — the third-party-POS route was dropped on 26 Jul 2026.) One store runs exactly one POS (a hard config invariant), so bill numbers never collide.

Idempotency is non-negotiable (Q8). Exactly-once is the entire game: client-UUID per sale → server returns the same Sale-ID on retry → idempotent upsert. The dedup key is store + bill-no (disjoint because one store = one POS). The adapter ships as a reader first (Ten ingest + daily POS recon green) before any own-POS store goes live. Season is re-attached at the edge from the effective-dated master for that barcode/store/sale-date; if a barcode lives under two seasons, oldest-season-first, else a one-tap "which season?" — never a blocked sale (Q6). The counter bill is a normal B2C GST tax invoice — no IRP/IRN.

9 · Season calendar D8 Q3 locked

One KDPS house season calendar. Each season is a record in a fixed format (FAMILY(Mon-YY), system-derived, never free text) with a lifecycle. A brand-season mapping table translates every incoming spelling ("SS26", "Spring Summer 2020", a raw date, or blank) into the house season. Season is the most load-bearing master — booking, PT and stock identity itself (barcode + season) all hang on it.

10 · Offer applicability window D5 Q5 locked

11 · The two SOR clocks Q4 / F9 — CA to confirm convention

An SOR lot runs two independent countdowns off one removal_date (the date the goods left the vendor / were received). They answer different questions and must never be collapsed into one. Both are alarmed at 30 / 15 / 7 days before they expire (Rule 11). This is greenfield — no ERPNext equivalent — so it is modelled explicitly.

Clock A — commercial return window (60–120 days)

The deal clock: how long KDPS may send unsold SOR / Correction / Consignment stock back to the brand. Per-brand (60–120 days, stretchable). When it runs out, unsold stock must go back or be dealt with (Correction draws its capped allowance; over-cap → V-flip). This is what keeps the booking in "Return-window open" (§1).

Owner: the booking / brand terms. Drives the seasonal-return picking and the booking's close.

Clock B — the ~6-month GST deemed-supply clock

The statutory clock (CGST §31(7)): for goods genuinely sent on sale-or-approval, if not sold or returned within ~6 calendar months of removal, the law deems a supply — a tax event independent of any commercial deadline. Computed as +6 calendar months from removal_date, not a literal +181 days.

Owner: the SOR lot's GST state. Distinct from Clock A — a brand could allow 120 commercial days while the statute deems supply at ~180; the two can fire in either order.

The money trap this guards against (F9). GST must be recognised once, where the tax invoice legally sits — never twice. A gst_recognised = (date, voucher_no) invariant on the SOR lot enforces one convention so the arrival voucher and the settlement journal can't both book it. Which path applies is keyed to the supplier's invoice timing (Q4): consignment-as-agent (Schedule I, invoice at dispatch) → purchase + ITC at arrival; true sale-or-return (Rule 55 challan, §31(7), invoice at sale / 6 months) → no purchase / ITC until sale. The per-brand characterisation is a CA call — this is not testable-away later.
