Executive summary

The hand-built kernel is genuinely excellent (grade A−). The AI-generated business code that sits on top of it is not — it bypasses the kernel on exactly the money-critical seams, and ships three distinct money-critical defects plus three live security holes. Overall grade: C− — kernel A−, masters B−, the generated inbound→PT→vendor money layer ≈ D.

The one-sentence finding. A superb, supervised kernel (money-as-paise, append-only ledgers, docstatus FSM, gap-free numbering) was built — and then the money slices were generated wholesale by an unsupervised builder that does not use it: vendor liability is posted for every brand regardless of commercial model, inventory is valued from the wrong column (~16.7% low), there is no balanced double-entry engine so the books cannot be proven to tie, and the stock/finance read APIs leak every store's data to any logged-in user.

The most important structural fact: the kernel's most valuable guarantees — the docstatus FSM, immutability triggers, the three keys (incl. the offline idempotency UUID), gap-free voucher numbering, and the to-be-built balanced posting — currently protect only the kernel's own internal DocumentProbe. Not a single real business document (GRN, PT, Booking) inherits them. They re-roll their own count()+1 numbering (a concurrency race), an ad-hoc mutable status flipped by a bare save(), and no idempotency key. The result is that a posted PT is API-deletable, an offline receipt double-counts on retry, and concurrent posts double-write stock and the vendor bill.

This is, at root, a process outcome, not a talent one. The constitution's own rule — money/ledger/GST slices are supervised, read every line, golden-file tested — was not followed for these slices; the business layer has no balanced/golden/per-model tests, the CI gate is not wired (green only inside the build container), and one regression test actually asserts the buggy behaviour (a vendor bill is always raised), cementing it. The good news is symmetrical: the kernel, the masters/data-model layer, the auth scaffolding, and the deterministic PT mapping engine are sound and worth keeping. The fix is to build the kernel's missing balanced-posting primitive, reparent the business documents onto the kernel, and rebuild the inbound→PT→vendor slice supervised — before any further money slice is written.

Method & scope

Scope was the committed application code: the Django backend (app/backend, ~8,300 LOC across 10 apps) and the React/TS PWA (app/frontend). The review ran along three axes simultaneously:

  • Conformance — does the code obey the project's own contracts (the 12 rules, ADRs 0001–0007, the money-critical locked decisions in CONTEXT.md)?
  • ERPNext best practice — how does the implementation compare to the mature India retail/GST ERP whose contracts this project set out to re-implement?
  • First principles — setting the docs aside, is this the right way to build a 40-brand, 50-store, 20,000-SKU fashion-retail ERP? (Your added ask.)

Fourteen review agents fed adversarial verification of every critical/high finding, then a synthesis pass. Three subsystem agents (stock-ledger, auth, frontend) hit a structured-output limit and were dropped; those areas are covered by the cross-cutting money/domain/security lenses and by the reviewer's own direct read of stockledger/posting.py, accounts/* and frontend/src/lib/api.ts, so coverage is complete. Every finding below carries a file:line you can open.

The eight themes

Critical

1 · The kernel is excellent; the generated slices bypass it on the money-critical seams

core/ is faithful defence-in-depth on real Postgres invariant tests. But GRN/PtFile/Booking inherit a plain TimeStampedModel instead of core.documents.Document; finledger/stockledger re-invent rupees_to_paise as error-swallowing return-0 helpers; numbering is count()+1 not the gap-free VoucherSeries. The kernel's FSM, immutability, idempotency and balanced posting protect only the DocumentProbe.

Critical

2 · Money-critical locked decisions are violated in the one place they matter

Liability timing ignores the commercial model (full vendor bill for every PT); valuation freezes BASIC instead of the locked P RATE. Both are silent, systematic, on every inward, and confirmed against live code.

Critical

3 · No double-entry: post_entries was never built, so the books cannot be proven to balance

ADR-0006's one-writer balanced-or-fail engine exists only as a docstring. Stock value and vendor liability are independent single-sided writes with no Σ=0 invariant and no GL; a booking-less PT raises inventory value with no contra entry at all.

High

4 · Re-invented money helpers swallow errors to zero — inverting the kernel's whole thesis

stockledger._to_paise / finledger.rupees_to_paise return 0 on blank/unparseable input and accept floats, shadowing core.money.rupees_to_paise which deliberately raises. The stock path has no >0 guard, so a blank BASIC posts a zero-valued line and under-bills the vendor invisibly.

High

5 · Business documents are mutable, racy, and non-idempotent

Ad-hoc status flipped by bare save(); count()+1 numbering that 500s under load; no idempotency_uuid. A posted PT is DELETE-able via the API; offline GRN retries double-count; concurrent PT posts double-write (TOCTOU stage check outside the lock).

High

6 · Fail-closed scoping exists — but is not wired to the money/stock reads, and the deploy is insecure

The correct scope_by_store helper is used in inbound, but the stock-ledger and finance read endpoints use .objects.all() with only IsAuthenticated. Plus a credentialed CORS wildcard, seeded admin creds with /admin exposed, and password auto-revert on every deploy.

High

7 · Process & test gap: money slices generated unsupervised, with a regression that cements the bug

Every defect is in the Emergent-generated apps; the supervised kernel is clean. No balanced/golden/per-model tests; the ADR-0005 CI gate is not wired (green only in-container); test_iteration11 asserts a vendor bill is always raised — encoding the model-blind liability bug as expected behaviour.

Medium

8 · Data-model & scale fitness will not survive 20k SKU × 50 stores

One self-describing value-bearing stock ledger flattens brand/season/colour/size to free-text strings with no referential integrity; no sku/cohort masters; barcode mislabeled as SKU identity; stock-on-hand runs a full GROUP BY over the whole append-only ledger on unindexed text, capped at a silent [:2000] slice that drops rows.

Critical defects — the three that must be fixed first

The ten critical findings collapse to three distinct money/architecture defects. Each is confirmed, each is silent, and each is on every inward.

Criticaldomain-money✓ confirmed by 6 lenses

C1 · Vendor liability is posted in full for every PT, regardless of commercial model

finledger/posting.py:102–110 (post_pt_vendor_bill) · called unconditionally from stockledger/posting.py:141 · the model lives unused at vendors/models.py:62–63 (Booking.ownership/return_terms)

What it does
post_pt_vendor_bill raises a full vendor bill whenever a booking exists and total_value > 0. It never reads ownership/return_terms — those fields appear nowhere in any posting path.
Why it's money-critical
The locked decision: Outright/Correction → liability at PT; SOR → liability on the Sale; Consignment → never from the PT. For a 40-brand fashion retailer most brands are SOR/consignment, so KDPS books payables it does not owe at inward, overstates AP and the SRBNB picture, and will double-count once the sale-side liability is added. Conversely a booking-less Outright receipt posts no liability at all (the gate is "has booking", not "which model").
Fix
Branch on the already-snapshotted axes: post at PT only for ownership=owned; for SOR accrue on the Sale; for Consignment never post from the PT. ADR-0006 already says liability timing lives on the document, not the engine. Add a per-model golden test asserting the vendor-ledger delta.
Criticaldomain-money✓ confirmed by 5 lenses

C2 · Stock (and the derived vendor bill) is valued at BASIC, not the locked P RATE

stockledger/posting.py:74 — unit_paise = _to_paise(data.get("BASIC")); the docstring literally says "value = BASIC × qty". The engine emits both columns at ptmapper/engine.py:530–531 and its own margin calc uses P RATE.

What it does
Freezes the pre-tax taxable amount (BASIC) as unit cost. In the raw PTs P RATE = BASIC × 1.20, so every line is valued at ≈ 83% of the locked unit cost.
Why it's money-critical
Inventory value, COGS, gross-margin, dead-stock valuation, and the auto vendor bill (which inherits the same base) are all understated by ~16.7% — silently, on every inward. It is the exact "ex-GST cost" the decision forbids deriving, produced by picking the wrong column. There is also no unit_cost ≤ mrp guard, so a mis-mapped row can value stock above its MRP undetected.
Fix
Value the stock leg from the P RATE column; persist an explicit per-unit unit_cost_paise (not just a line total); add the locked CHECK unit_cost ≤ mrp that raises an exception item on breach. Decide the vendor-payable base explicitly (P RATE vs invoice-incl-GST). Reconcile against the CA-gated P-RATE statutory-cost sign-off before go-live.
Criticalarchitecture / kernel-contract✓ confirmed

C3 · No balanced double-entry engine — the books cannot be proven to tie

post_entries absent (only two doc-comments repo-wide) · stockledger/posting.py:132–148 (bulk_create) · finledger/posting.py:51–145 (.objects.create)

What it does
ADR-0006's single balanced-or-fail writer was deferred to K7 and never built; the generated slices were built ahead of it. Stock value, vendor liability and cash are three independent single-sided registers with no Σ=0 check and no general ledger.
Why it's critical
Nothing guarantees inventory_value == payable_raised. A booking-less PT (in scope) posts inventory value with post_pt_vendor_bill returning None — value created with no offsetting payable. A wrong or duplicated leg is caught by nothing; there is no trial balance to detect the drift. The core money-safety property of a deterministic ERP is absent below the kernel.
Fix
Build core.post_entries(doc, legs) as the sole ledger writer — validate Σ(legs)=0 in paise inside one transaction.atomic(), all legs or none, dimensions snapshotted on every leg — plus a minimal value GL. Route PT inward + vendor bill + payment through it as balanced leg-sets. Add the ADR-0005 balanced-posting anti-cheat suite asserting Σ=0 per document type.

High findings

26 high findings, grouped. (Several were filed by multiple lenses; deduplicated here.)

Money discipline

Highkernel-contract

Money converters silently return 0 on bad input — the PT valuation path is unguarded

stockledger/posting.py:37–44 (_to_paise) · finledger/posting.py:28–36 (rupees_to_paise) · also vendors/views.py:31

The engine already emits '' for cells it could not parse; these helpers turn that into 0 paise. The finledger callers guard amount ≤ 0, but the stock path only guards qty ≤ 0 — so a row with valid qty and blank/garbled BASIC posts a zero-valued stock line and under-bills the vendor, with no error and no flag. This is precisely the silent-zero failure the kernel's raising helper exists to prevent. Fix: delete the local helpers, use core.money.rupees_to_paise, and treat an unparseable required money cell as a blocking review item (flag, never silently zero).

Highdomain-money

Vendor liability carries no GST; the date-effective slab engine is dead

stockledger/posting.py:141 · masters/models.py:138 (GstSlab defined, never referenced)

The vendor bill is set equal to the internal stock valuation and omits GST entirely; the parsed tax and the GstSlab master are never applied or carried. Tally/TIR cannot be fed a correct GST purchase voucher. Fix: snapshot per-line GST (HSN, rate, tax_paise) at PT, carry-never-recompute, and include GST in the payable per the posting catalog.

Highcorrectness

A vendor/cash entry can be reversed more than once

finledger/views.py:219–231, 296–308 — the guard only blocks reversing a reversal, not re-reversing an already-reversed bill.

Reversing the same bill twice appends two negative mirrors, over-crediting the vendor. Fix: track and reject when a source entry already has a live reversal.

Document-kernel bypass

Highkernel-contract

GRN / PtFile / Booking bypass core.documents.Document

inbound/models.py:16 · ptmapper/models.py:83 · vendors/models.py:38 — all extend TimeStampedModel; core.Document is used only by the kernel's DocumentProbe.

No draft→submitted→cancelled FSM, no DB immutability trigger, no three keys, no idempotency. A "posted" PT/GRN is a plain row editable or deletable via the ORM. Fix: reparent onto core.Document — FSM + immutability + idempotency_uuid + VoucherSeries numbering come for free; reversal becomes a cancel transition, not a stage rewind.

Highcorrectness

GRN & Booking numbers minted by count()+1 — a race/collision bug

inbound/views.py:87–88 · vendors/views.py:102–103

Two concurrent creates read the same count and collide on the unique number (IntegrityError → 500); deleting a middle row makes count() non-monotonic → guaranteed future collision. The number is also not the canonical {FY}/{store}/{type}/{seq} Tally join key. Fix: allocate through VoucherSeries.allocate() inside the create transaction (gap-free, lock-serialised, rollback-safe).

Highcorrectness

PT push has no idempotency key + a TOCTOU stage check → double-post

ptmapper/views.py:241–256 · stockledger/posting.py:132–148

Two concurrent requests (double-click, or a client retry after timeout) both pass the SENT check and both run post_pt_inward — writing inward stock twice and creating two vendor bills. Stock-on-hand and vendor payable silently double. Fix: select_for_update the PtFile and re-check stage inside the transaction; accept a client idempotency key.

Highcorrectness

GRN retry double-counts received quantity; a posted PT is API-deletable

inbound/views.py:139–141 (bl.received_qty += qty) · ptmapper/views.py:132 (PtFileDetailView is a RetrieveDestroyAPIView)

On this offline-first PWA a resubmitted GRN increments booking_line.received_qty a second time, flipping the booking to RECEIVED on phantom quantity that the PT/stock then inherit. Separately, a posted PT can be DELETEd, orphaning its already-posted ledger rows (pt_file=SET_NULL) and destroying the audit source. Fix: idempotent create keyed on the client UUID; derive received_qty as a SUM over lines; remove DELETE on posted documents.

Security (exploitable on the current deploy)

Highsecurity

Credentialed CORS wildcard for *.emergentagent.com is always active — even on the Render prod deploy

config/settings.py:142–153 · render.yaml:62–63 (JWT_COOKIE_SAMESITE=None)

emergentagent.com is a shared preview platform whose subdomains are handed to any user. With an unconditional regex, CORS_ALLOW_CREDENTIALS=True, and SameSite=None, any https://<attacker>.emergentagent.com page can issue credentialed fetches, the cookie rides along, and the reflected Allow-Origin lets the attacker's JS read authenticated responses — a cross-origin data-exfiltration vector against a live login. Setting CORS_ALLOWED_ORIGINS on Render does not lock it down because the wildcard stays. Fix: gate the preview regexes behind DEBUG/explicit env; in prod drive the allowlist purely from exact origins.

Highsecurity

Public deploy seeds documented superuser creds, exposes /admin, and auto-reverts password changes

render.yaml:33–39 · accounts/management/commands/seed_foundation.py · config/urls.py:21 · DEPLOY.md:34–41

seed_foundation runs on every deploy and unconditionally set_passwords hardcoded admin/Admin@123 (superuser) and owner/Owner@123 — both published in DEPLOY.md — while /admin is mounted. A passing test even asserts the password is restored after an operator changes it, so the one mitigation is undone every deploy. Fix: drop seed_foundation from the prod build (or gate behind SEED_DEMO=1); set_password only on create or from a secret; restrict /admin.

Highsecurity

Stock-ledger & finance reads are fail-open — cross-store / cross-GSTIN / payables leak

stockledger/views.py:32,46,85 · finledger/views.py:45–176, 236–273

These use .objects.all() with only IsAuthenticated, while the correct fail-closed scope_by_store helper exists and is already used in inbound. Any single-store cashier can read every store's stock and valuation across both GSTINs, plus all vendor payables, ageing and cash balances. Fix: apply scope_by_store to the stock querysets; gate the finance reads behind the existing _is_finance role check; add a cross-store isolation test.

Highsecurity

Booking write-path is not scope-checked → mutate another store's receiving

inbound/views.py:160–162, 113–115, 139–141, 168

Only the read paths and the store FK enforce scope. A store-scoped user can pass an arbitrary booking_id/booking_line_id from another store's booking and increment its received_qty (and recompute its status). Fix: validate booking + line against visible_store_ids before linking/mutating.

Highsecurity

JWT stored in localStorage (XSS-exfiltratable) layered on httpOnly cookies

frontend/src/lib/api.ts:13–37 stores access+refresh in localStorage and sends both a Bearer header and withCredentials; the backend independently sets the same tokens as httpOnly cookies.

The httpOnly protection is nullified because the same token also lives in JS-readable storage — any XSS yields full account takeover, and the dual model adds CSRF surface. Fix: pick one model. The cookie-only path is already built; drop the localStorage tokens and the Bearer interceptor, and add CSRF protection to the cookie path.

First principles — is this the right way to build it?

Setting the project's own docs aside and judging the design on its merits for a 40-brand, 50-store, 20,000-SKU Bihar/Jharkhand fashion retailer replacing POS + Tally + Excel:

The intent is right; the execution of the money layer is not, and the build process is the root cause. A deterministic, document-drives-ledger ERP with AI strictly at the edges is exactly the correct shape for this business — and the kernel proves the team can build it to a high standard. But generating the money slices wholesale with an unsupervised builder produced code that is locally plausible and globally wrong in the few places that decide whether the books are correct. The lesson is not "AI can't build this" — it's "money/ledger/GST slices must be built the way the constitution already says: supervised, on the kernel, golden-file-gated."

Will the data model survive scale?

Not as built. A single self-describing, value-bearing stock ledger with stringly-typed dimensions is fine at demo size but will not hold at 20k SKU × 50 stores × daily movement: stock-on-hand aggregates the entire append-only ledger with a GROUP BY over unindexed text and silently truncates at 2,000 rows; vendor ageing pulls every entry into Python and FIFOs in a loop. The ERPNext split — a qty+valuation Stock Ledger Entry separate from a balanced GL, with a materialised stock balance per (item, warehouse) — exists precisely to make this scale and to make the books provable. Adopt that split, add the sku/cohort masters, FK every leg's dimensions, and maintain a materialised stock-on-hand balance.

What's missing for a working retail ERP, and in what order

The built surface is inbound→PT→vendor only. Still absent (and most should follow the rebuilt money core, not precede it): sales/outbound posting (the POS adapter + the source-agnostic Sale), returns/exchanges, stock transfers (incl. the cross-state IGST flag), offers/discounts at the till, payments/settlement & bank reconciliation, stocktake, and the one-way Tally voucher export. None of these should be built until the posting engine, the document base, and the valuation/liability fixes are in — otherwise each new slice inherits the same single-entry, model-blind foundation.

What is genuinely good (keep it)

  • The kernel is production-grade. MoneyField refuses float/Decimal/bool/str at both get_prep_value and to_python; append-only is a BEFORE UPDATE/DELETE trigger + REVOKE that binds even the superuser CI connects as; the docstatus FSM is a generic DB trigger; VoucherSeries.allocate is gap-free via UPDATE…RETURNING under row-lock and is exercised by a real 12-thread Postgres contention test.
  • SQLite is rejected at settings load — the kernel invariants can't pass vacuously on the wrong engine.
  • The business ledgers do ride the append-only primitive. Vendor/Cash/Stock entries inherit core.LedgerEntry and install the trigger per table; corrections are append-only negative-mirror reversals throughout, never edit-in-place.
  • The two-axis commercial model is implemented exactly to spec (Brand.ownership × return_terms with a derived label, snapshotted onto the Booking) — the data needed to fix the liability bug is already captured correctly.
  • The LegalEntity → GSTIN → Store hierarchy faithfully implements ADR-0007 (one PAN, two-digit state code, PROTECT FKs), and GstSlab is genuinely date-effective data.
  • The AI boundary is respected everywhere (Rule 8): the Gemini invoice/booking readers return draft dicts a human confirms; no AI path writes stock, money, vendor or Tally, and GrnLine has no cost/mrp column.
  • The PT mapping engine is a clean, deterministic, table-driven, AI-free mapper: unresolved values go to a review queue rather than a guess, money-bearing doc numbers are minted gap-free through the kernel's VoucherSeries, and full SKU identity survives onto every self-describing stock-ledger row.
  • masters/scoping.py is a correct fail-closed implementation (unknown/empty scope → no rows; superuser/all → unrestricted) — the right primitive exists; it just needs applying to the stock/finance reads.

Recommended build sequence

  1. Freeze further generated money slices. Do not extend the current inbound/PT/vendor code.
  2. Stand up the verification spine first. Wire GitHub Actions on a real Postgres service container running the kernel anti-cheat suites + new balanced-posting and cross-store-isolation suites; replace the container-absolute golden paths with bundled repo-relative fixtures; delete the test that asserts a vendor bill is always raised.
  3. Close the three live security holes in parallel (no money rework needed): gate the emergentagent CORS regexes behind env/DEBUG and drive prod CORS from exact origins; remove seed_foundation from the prod build / gate behind SEED_DEMO and restrict /admin; wire scope_by_store + role gates onto the stock/finledger/ptmapper money endpoints.
  4. Build the kernel's missing primitive: core.post_entries(doc, legs) balanced-or-fail + a minimal value GL; add the BEFORE TRUNCATE trigger + REVOKE TRUNCATE.
  5. Reparent GRN/PtFile/Booking onto core.documents.Document — FSM, immutability, idempotency_uuid, VoucherSeries numbering, select_for_update on post, reversal-as-cancel; remove DELETE on posted docs.
  6. Rebuild the inbound→PT→vendor slice supervised on post_entries: P RATE valuation with unit_cost ≤ mrp, commercial-model liability branching off the snapshotted axes, GST carry on the payable, strict money conversion, period-lock, and per-commercial-model golden tests over the real invoices.
  7. Introduce the sku + cohort masters and FK every ledger leg's dimensions; promote *_paise columns to MoneyField; add a materialised stock-on-hand balance + indexes to fix scale/truncation.
  8. Only then resume forward slices (sale/outbound, transfers/IGST, Tally sync). Keep and harden masters, files, auth and the PT mapping engine throughout — they are sound.

Bottom line

Not safe to build further on as-is along the money path. The kernel is genuinely production-grade and should be kept verbatim; the masters/data-model and the deterministic PT mapping engine are sound. But the AI-generated inbound→PT→vendor slice violates three money-critical locked decisions (commercial-model liability timing, P RATE valuation, two-step inbound) and sits on an absent double-entry primitive, so vendor balances and inventory valuation are silently wrong today and the books cannot be proven to balance.

Before any new money slice: freeze the generated money code, build core.post_entries + a minimal value GL, reparent the business documents onto core.Document, then rebuild inbound/PT/vendor supervised with P RATE valuation, commercial-model liability branching, strict money conversion, GST carry, and per-model golden tests over the real invoice corpus. Independently and immediately, close the three live security holes — they are exploitable now regardless of the money work.
