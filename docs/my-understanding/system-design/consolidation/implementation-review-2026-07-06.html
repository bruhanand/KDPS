Design-Conformance Review — the built app vs the design-of-record

The whole implementation on the emergent branch (everything on main + Sprint 1 outbound) reviewed against docs/my-understanding/ (constitution · consolidated design · posting catalog · lifecycles · data model · ADRs · D1–D8 · 16 locked decisions) with the ERPNext study + repo as the proven-pattern benchmark. Successor to the 30-Jun implementation review.

6 July 2026 · 14 review tracks + adversarial verification (139 agents) · every finding cites design clause + code line · findings hand-verified at P0/P1 by the lead reviewer

Overall verdict

The architecture held; the money semantics on top of it did not. The kernel is genuinely conformant — money-as-paise with Decimal half-up, DB-trigger append-only ledgers, the docstatus FSM with race-safe gap-free numbering, and a real balanced value GL written only by post_entries. The 30-Jun remediation was largely real: GRN and PT sit on the kernel FSM, the two-axis commercial model branches liability correctly by timing, and Sprint-1's write-side RBAC and store scoping verified at all 10 write points. Kernel ≈ A−

But the business posting layer deviates materially from the posting catalog: two P0 defects are writing wrong numbers today (the vendor payable is booked ex-GST with no Input-GST leg on every owned PT; every outbound document created through the PWA posts stock and GL at 0 paise), a cluster of locked-decision violations sits beneath them (no GRN quantity step, silent BASIC valuation fallback, uncapped seasonal RTV, ownership never persisted as data, no brand terms card), and a systematic fail-open read-scoping class (outbound reads, PT-file detail/delete, file download, vendor bookings) survives next to the solid write-side scoping. Money semantics & read-scope ≈ C

Sign-off recommendation: do not sign off Sprint 1 as complete. Emergent's automated-surface claims are honest on the write side (see the claim table), but claim #13 — the SUSPENSE WARN being "by design per the posting catalog" — is false, and the review found the Sprint-1 outbound posting layer books zero-value and mis-branched money. Treat the RBAC/scoping work as accepted, the posting layer as needing rework per the fix order below. The alpha must not handle live money until the two P0s and the P1 money items are closed.

Also note: the deployed Render alpha already contains wrong GL rows (every owned PT posted to date carries an ex-GST payable). Remediation must respect the append-only / no-reposting rule — compensating vouchers, never edits — and the ex-GST↔incl-GST correction interacts with the CA-gated Input-GST questions (Q4/Q5), so sequence the fix with the CA conversation.

The SUSPENSE ruling (Emergent's WARN item)

Ruling: not by design — an invention, consciously labelled as interim. Emergent's claim that parking −₹1,400 in SUSPENSE is "by design per the posting catalog" is FAIL.

What the catalog actually documents (verified verbatim by three independent refuters at posting-catalog.html §EOSS & V-flip and outbound.html Q4): the V-flip settlement becomes "a pending claim in D4" that "nets only when the brand agrees, at the agreed amount", with the Tally journal posted "when the claim is agreed." The build invents both the account (generic SUSPENSE rather than an identifiable brand-settlement claim) and the amount (frozen unit cost — which by the design's own definition cannot be "the agreed amount", because no agreement has happened).

Nuances that keep this P2 rather than P1: the voucher is balanced (Σ=0 holds — books still tie), the memo and test self-label it a "Sprint 8 placeholder", and the proper home (D4 Payments) is legitimately unbuilt. But with no clearer in existence, the credit is a real, unlabelled brand liability at an invented value sitting under a green "balanced" badge. Correct treatment until D4 lands: either a recognisable pending-claim account (e.g. BRAND_SETTLEMENT_CLAIM per brand) — or no money leg at all at flip time. Related defect: the flip also posts the SOR-pair reversal with no check that the stock was ever brand-owned (finding #18).

02-inbound/inbound-ingestion-design.html Q37 + posting-catalog.html PT rows :: app/backend/stockledger/posting.py:176-186,332; finledger/posting.py:271-280; core/gl.py:33
What & why it matters

Rationale

The current 8-group nav mixes two incompatible organizing principles: some groups are by business process (Store Ops, Outbound) and one group ('Documents') is by artifact type — a doctype-browse list that cuts across every process and every audience at once. Because backend nav_groups are additive per role (seed_foundation.py grants 'documents' to store_staff, warehouse, accounts and ho_ops alike), that one group becomes the place where duplicate and dead-stub entries accumulate (Transfers, Stock Receive both appear a second time there; Sales/Returns/Payments are stub-only siblings sitting next to fully-live Bookings/PT-mapper) and where audience leakage happens (a cashier's sidebar shows vendor Bookings and PT File Operation, which are HO/warehouse tasks). The fix is to delete 'Documents' as a navigational group entirely — every one of its live children already has, or can move to, a home in a process-scoped group — and to reorganize the remaining seven groups by operational scope + audience (Store Floor / Merchandising & Inventory Control / Finance & Books / Controls & Governance / Intelligence / Admin & Setup), collapsing the Users&Roles vs Users&RBAC duplicate into one canonical admin route, folding 'Outbound' jargon into the inventory-control audience group where store staff don't need most of its items, and requiring every stub to carry a visible 'coming soon' treatment in the sidebar itself (not just after navigating) so live and unbuilt screens are never visually interchangeable. No new features are invented — every item above already exists as a route/page in the current tree; only its group membership, canonical URL (Users&Roles) and stub/live labelling change.

ERPNext precedent

ERPNext groups screens by business process, not by artifact type or "kind of screen": Stock module owns Purchase Receipt/Delivery Note/Stock Entry even though they are money-adjacent (13-doctype-catalog.html; confirmed via erpnext/stock/workspace/), Buying owns Purchase Order, Selling owns Sales Order, Accounts owns Sales Invoice/Payment Entry/Journal Entry — "module = business-process grouping" and there is no cross-cutting "Documents" module that lists every voucher type together. KDPS's current 'Documents' group is the anti-pattern ERPNext explicitly avoids. ERPNext also keeps settings/config ("data not code" tier — Item Tax Template, Pricing Rule, POS Profile) visually separate from transactional screens within a module, which motivates keeping stub/config items visually distinguished from live transactional ones rather than interleaved with no marker, and Role Profiles bundle roles into job-function sets (Inventory/Accounts/Sales/Purchase) that deliberately overlap only where the job genuinely needs it (Sales profile includes Stock User) — the KDPS 'documents' nav_group instead over-shares by defaulting nearly every role into the same doctype-browse bucket regardless of job function.
