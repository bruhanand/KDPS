Posting Catalog

Every business event → which ledgers it writes → the exact debit/credit legs → for each of the four commercial models, plus the Tally voucher it produces. This is the one place that decides whether the books are right. A developer builds the posting engine from this page; an accountant audits it against this page.

How to read every event below. Each event shows three things — what moves in stock, what moves in the vendor account, what moves in cash — and then the four model columns (Outright Correction SOR Consignment). Where a model posts nothing, the cell says so plainly. The last column of each event names the Tally voucher the event produces (or "none — stock-ledger only"). Money is in INR, Lakh/Crore format (₹28,50,000). The four ledgers are colour-tagged:
Stock every piece, every move — quantity and (once priced) value Vendor what we owe each brand Cash what each store collected and where it went Tally the statutory voucher pushed to the book of record

The posting rules this catalog enforces

These are not new — they are the architecture rules (documents write ledgers, append-only, one owner per fact) made concrete for posting. Read these once; every event obeys them.

R1 · Quantity at the GRN, value at the PT Q1 locked

Two-step inbound posting. The GRN at arrival posts the quantity movement only — qty, location, condition, season, owned-flag (provisional), no unit cost. The PT at Patna inward posts the valuation movement — it stamps the frozen landed ex-GST unit cost (= P RATE, see below), finalises the owned-flag, and posts the vendor liability per model. This is what lets goods that arrive direct-to-store sell on day one before they are priced.

R2 · Vendor liability timing depends on the model Q2 locked

The PT does not always post a vendor liability. The PT creates priced stock truth for all four models and carries the settlement basis — but who owes what, and when, splits:

  • Outright Correction — incl-GST payable posts at the PT (Patna inward).
  • SOR — nothing at the PT; the payable accrues on the sale, per piece.
  • Consignment — nothing from the PT, ever; the payable exists only at the sale.

So the Sale document (D3) is the writer of the SOR and Consignment sold-liability. The PT for those two models only flags the stock not owned.

R3 · Corrections are new events, never edits no-reposting

Nothing posted is ever overwritten or deleted. A mistake is fixed by a new, today-dated reversing event with a reason code, linked to the source document. Balances are sums of postings; history always explains the present. (Code-level invariant: REVOKE UPDATE/DELETE on ledger tables + a trigger.)

Period lock / freeze-date (26 Jun 2026): the same engine must also reject a posting whose date falls in an already-closed/reported period, so a back-dated document can't silently restate an already-sent month / brand-report / SOR-settlement.

R4 · GST is recognised exactly once F9 guard · CA to confirm

For SOR and Consignment, the input GST must be recognised at one point — never both at the arrival purchase voucher and again at the settlement journal. The SOR/Consignment lot carries a gst_recognised = (date, voucher_no) stamp; once set, no second voucher may recognise GST on that lot. Which point is the legal one is keyed to the supplier's tax-invoice timing (see the four models and pending CA rulings).

R5 · Every leg balances; every leg carries its dimensions

For every event, the debits equal the credits (or, for the operational ledgers, the movement nets to the documented quantity/value). Every ledger leg carries its store · brand · season · state-GSTIN as declared columns, so the books read per store, per brand, per state without reconstruction.

The two clocks — never collapse them

Every model runs two independent clocks. They look like they should be the same; they are not, and a reader who merges them corrupts either the books or the cash. This table shows both, side by side, so no one ever does.

Model Statutory purchase clock when Tally sees the purchase (D6) Operational payable clock when we may actually pay the brand (D4)
Outright At arrival. Purchase voucher from the invoice, ITC claimed. Locked. At the PT (Patna inward) the payable opens, due per brand terms; pay after the eligibility gate clears.
Correction At arrival. Same as Outright. At the PT; net of the return allowance as returns draw it down.
SOR CA · Q4 Arrival if the brand bills with a tax invoice at delivery (the D6 default); deferred to sale if true Sec 31(7) approval-basis. gst_recognised guard = once only. On the sale — per-piece accrual at the settlement rate. Hard gate: an unsold SOR piece can never be paid.
Consignment CA · Q4 Same question as SOR; pure consignment with no PT value must not book ITC at arrival. On the sale — like SOR. Never payable before the piece sells.
Why two clocks at all. Tally is the statutory book — it must show the purchase when the law says the supply happened (often at delivery). But KDPS must not pay a brand for an SOR/Consignment piece until that piece sells. So the same goods can be a booked purchase in Tally while still being an un-payable, brand-owned item in the operational ledger. The catalog keeps these on separate ledgers (Tally voucher vs vendor ledger) precisely so they never overwrite each other.

Unit cost = P RATE, taken directly Q5 locked

The canonical cost stamped on the PT valuation posting is the per-piece landed ex-GST vendor payable, and it is taken directly from the P RATE column of the PT — not derived by dividing P RATE by (1 + GST slab).

  • Start from P RATE (the per-piece payable to the vendor after line-level discounts). In the real sample PT, P RATE = MRP − margin% (e.g. ₹1,649 × 0.78 = ₹1,286.22).
  • Add this piece's value-weighted share of invoice-level freight / handling; subtract its value-weighted share of invoice-level trade discount. Spread by line value, never by piece count.
  • Do NOT strip a GST rate from P RATE. The raw data showed P RATE = BASIC × 1.20 on every row including 5%-slab brands — the 1.20 is a flat markup convention, not the GST rate, so dividing by (1+slab) yields a wrong cost. If an ex-GST figure is ever needed for a Tally line, compute it from the actual HSN slab, not from BASIC.

The result is frozen on the stock-in posting (Rule 3 snapshot). Profitability reads it (POS revenue − this cost); aging and dead-stock value with it. One definition for all four models — for SOR and Consignment it is simply the settlement rate, taken as P RATE.

CA sign-off (Q5): confirm against a live PT sample that no brand embeds GST inside P RATE, and that Ind AS 2 treatment of recoverable ITC-as-not-cost holds. Inverted-duty rows (INPUT TAX ≠ OUTPUT TAX) exist — handle per actual HSN.

EOSS & the V-flip — markdown, and the brand-owned → KDPS-owned flip

D3 Q12 Stock Vendor Tally

EOSS discount is just a lower selling price — it flows through the ordinary Sale event (Disc%/Disc Amt captured from the POS report). KDPS simply earns less margin; nothing is claimed back from the brand (a normal discount is not a claim). The V-flip is the distinct posting event: dead stock past the return window the brand won't take back is reported "sold under EOSS", the brand settlement is claimed, and ownership flips to KDPS.

LedgerEOSS discount (any model) & the V-flip (brand-owned models)
StockEOSS discount: no stock event beyond the sale itself. V-flip: the piece stays on the shelf — owner flips brand-owned → KDPS-owned, status = liquidation, real brand kept, "V <brand>" display label, barcode unchanged. It then sells as ordinary KDPS stock.
VendorEOSS discount: nothing. V-flip: the brand EOSS settlement is claimed → a pending claim in D4 (no physical return behind it, so it nets only when the brand agrees, at the agreed amount). For Correction over-cap, the over-allowance recovery is the same kind of pending claim.
CashEOSS discount: lower net in the ordinary sale. V-flip: nothing at flip; cash comes when the flipped piece later sells.
TallyEOSS discount: carried in the daily sales voucher (lower revenue). V-flip settlement: Journal when the claim is agreed (the settlement leg), drops the piece out of brand reports.

Money events

Payment — money out to a brand

D4 Q-A…Q-G Vendor Cash Tally

Payments does not decide what is owed — the spine already booked the debt. A Payment is a document that settles one or many open payables (full or partial) by bill-wise allocation (Tally's Agst Ref), through the lifecycle Draft → Pending approval → Approved → Executed → Confirmed → Reconciled. Money leaves the bank manually in v1; the UTR closes the payable. The posting is the same shape for all four models — what differs is which payables are eligible (the gate below).

LedgerAll models
VendorDr Vendor payable (per allocation, partials included) — reduces the open payable. Balance is derived (Opening + Invoices − CD − GR − Payments + Interest = Due), read at both brand and vendor grain.

The eligibility gate decides what may be paid (hard blocks, never overridable): no validated bank details or no GSTIN on the vendor · payable not tied to an inwarded PT/TIR · an SOR/Consignment piece that hasn't actually sold. The last one is the operational-clock guard in code: an unsold brand-owned piece can never be paid.
