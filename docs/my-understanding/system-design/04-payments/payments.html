Payments — System Design

How KDPS pays its vendors (brands) — turning debts the spine already booked into money that actually left the bank, under control and fully traceable — and how the daily store-collection audit proves the money a store reported actually reached the bank. Picks up the payables created at Inbound (PT, Q37) and Outbound (sale payables, brand credits, settlements).

1 · The one idea

Payments does not decide what KDPS owes — the spine already booked every debt (a PT at inbound, a sale for SOR/Consignment, a return, a brand credit). Payments is the controlled lifecycle that turns a booked debt into money that left the bank, plus the daily audit that proves store money arrived. Two halves of one module:

MONEY OUT Vendor payments — settle what we owe each brand: eligibility gate → approval → bank execution → reconciliation against the bank.

MONEY IN Store-collection audit — every day, compare what each store collected (cash / card / UPI) against what actually landed in the bank account.

Three rules the whole module obeys (from the architecture)

  • Documents write the ledger. A payment is a document that posts a vendor-ledger debit; nobody types a balance (Rule 2).
  • Flag, don't block — but money has a real hard gate (Rule 5 + D1 pay-gate): no pay without bank details + GSTIN, and no pay before reconciliation is clean.
  • Every payment has a name on it — requestor, approver(s), executor — as steps inside the document's state machine, not emails on the side (Rule 10).

3 · The payment model Q-A locked

When KDPS pays a brand, the system tracks two layers — the debt and the disbursement — linked by bill-wise allocation (the NetSuite / SAP / Tally standard). The only model that survives SOR's per-sale accruals and Correction's net-of-returns.

Layer 1 — the Payable (the debt)

One debt KDPS owes a brand, born from a spine document (a PT invoice, a per-sale SOR/Consignment accrual, a return debit, a brand credit). Lives on the vendor ledger; status is derived from allocations, never typed:

Layer 2 — the Payment (the disbursement)

A separate document that settles one or many open payables (full or partial), carrying the approval + execution lifecycle. The link is the allocation — exactly Tally's Agst Ref. So one bank transfer can clear a month of SOR accruals; one Outright invoice can be paid on its own.

Which entity, which grain — the ledger reads both ways added by Anand 17 Jun

  • Payables accrue per brand — commercial model, returns, the GR allowance and the SOR sale-accrual are all brand-level (D1: bookings are brand-wise).
  • Payments anchor to the vendor — the bank account + GSTIN live on the vendor / legal entity (D1 §2), so one payment can allocate across payables from several brands under one vendor, or a single brand. The model allows either; whether KDPS sweeps a vendor or pays brand-by-brand stays an operational choice.
  • Every allocation is a posting, partials included — the vendor ledger records each part-payment and reads at both brand and vendor grain ("what we owe / have paid — this brand · this vendor"). Balances derived, never typed (Rule 2).

Bank execution — manual now, the bank is a variable Q-E locked

Once a payment is approved (the Q-D four-eyes), the money leaves like this in v1:

  1. System prepares the payment — beneficiary, amount, the payables it settles.
  2. A person pays manually in the bank's portal (no software-initiated transfer in v1).
  3. The UTR is captured — typed at pay-time and/or auto-matched from the daily read-only statement pull (the §6 narration parser) — which auto-closes the payable (the payment roundtrip).

Everything bank-touching sits behind one bank adapter — swappable on two axes:

  • Which bank — there are three banks; ICICI is the one we have access to now, but no bank name is hardcoded. Treat the bank as Bank X; the adapter is the seam.
  • Which mode — read-only + manual pay now; an H2H file or a payment-initiation API drops in later (v2) with no redesign.

The actual bank API spec is sent to the bank later — design the requirement first, then request the matching API. Read-only statement access is enough to close the loop today.

Settlement & netting — what we actually pay Q-G locked

Brand money flows both ways; the payment is the net. Two-state netting, in KDPS's own vocabulary:

  • Goods-return DN nets now — a Debit Note for returned stock reduces the payable immediately (backed by a physical return); the brand's Credit Note (CN) is tracked as acknowledgment, and the §6 ⑥ reconciler chases the DN-without-CN gap.
  • Commercial claims pend — EOSS/V-flip settlements & Correction over-allowance recovery have no return behind them, so they sit as pending claims and net only when the brand agrees, at the agreed amount. (A normal offer discount is not a claim — it just lowers the selling price; KDPS earns less margin, nothing is reclaimed.)
  • Per model: Outright/Correction payable books at inward (Correction return within allowance nets, over-allowance → pending / V-flip); SOR/Consignment accrues on sale (the Account Sales Report), unsold returns net nothing.

The vendor ledger is KDPS's party ledger, derived: Opening + Invoices − CD (cash discount) − GR (goods return) − Payments + Interest = Due, at brand + vendor grain. Confirmed by the real CN&DN and party-ledger files.

4 · The eligibility gate Q-C locked

The one place the system deliberately holds money back. A payable becomes payable only when it clears a tiered gate — a short hard-block list, everything else flags.

Hard blocks — never overridable (fix the cause)

  • No validated bank details or no GSTIN on the vendor (the D1 pay-gate; India-mandatory anyway).
  • Payable not tied to an inwarded PT / TIR — the debt isn't official yet (Outright/Correction book at Patna inward; SOR/Consignment only on sale).
  • An SOR/Consignment piece that hasn't actually sold.
  • A booking-less direct receipt with an unknown or defaulted commercial model (goods arrived at a store with no booking, model not yet confirmed). The payable sits in an "awaiting invoice / unmatched payable" state and cannot be paid until the model is confirmed. If the brand itself is truly unknown, the receipt records stock-in (quantity + cost) but the vendor-ledger posting is withheld entirely — it behaves like SOR, so there is nothing to pay against until the brand is confirmed. Inbound Q9

Soft holds — flag + a named senior releases with a reason

  • An open invoice ↔ GRN ↔ PT discrepancy (the inbound EOD recon).
  • A Correction return over its allowance.
  • An amount over a per-brand variance tolerance. Trivial ₹/% gaps auto-pass — rounding never freezes a payment.

Checked on the payable, re-checked at execution (the world can change between approve and pay).

Two clocks, never one. A brand's goods can be booked in the statutory book before KDPS owes a paisa operationally — these are two separate timings and the design keeps them apart:
  • Statutory purchase clock — the Tally purchase voucher is booked at goods arrival from the supplier invoice, for every brand including SOR (the SOR difference is money only, not timing). This is the book of record; D6 owns its vouchers.
  • Operational payable clock — the payable D4 will actually pay only becomes eligible on the real event: Outright/Correction at Patna inward, SOR/Consignment on the sale, plus returns and settlements. This is the clock the eligibility gate above runs on — which is why an SOR/Consignment piece that hasn't sold is a hard block even though Tally already shows the purchase.
The two clocks are laid out side by side, per commercial model, in the posting catalog (the consolidation/posting-catalog.html "books-when" table) — read it before reasoning about when money is due.

5 · Roles & access — the people grid here; thresholds at Q-D

Built on the finance-world rule of segregation of duties: the three highest-risk actions — changing a vendor's bank details, approving a payment, and releasing the money — must never sit with one person. Everyone else gets least-privilege, data-scoped access (a store sees only its own money).

This grid is the payments-facing slice of roles. The full role/permission model lives in D8 (master-data & users); this grid and D8's user model merge into one role-permission matrix — each role defined once, read by every module.

A vendor's bank-detail change is its own control point: logged, second-eye verified, and AI-flagged on the next payment (§6). Maker ≠ checker ≠ payer, always. AI is not a role — it's a layer every role sees, tuned to their job.

6 · The AI & Intelligence layer — the "wow" read-only · Rule 8

The market splits AI two ways: autonomous agents that act and copilots that advise. For money, KDPS is deliberately copilot, not autopilot — AI reads everything, summarizes, predicts, drafts and flags, but a human always approves and a document always posts (Rule 8). That one line is what lets us pile on AI safely: the worst an AI can do here is show a wrong suggestion, never move a wrong rupee.

Tags: Now shippable day one · Soon once data accrues · Wow the showpiece.

① Reads the mess Now AI

  • Bank-narration parser — turns INF/NEFT/IN4260… gibberish into "this credit = Allen Solly refund / this store's UPI settlement", using the UTR + narration. The match key for both reconciliations.
  • Invoice & credit-note reader — already in inbound; feeds payables and brand credits here.

② Catches what humans miss Now Soon AI

  • Duplicate-payment guard — catches near-duplicates (same vendor+amount, slightly different invoice no / OCR noise), not just exact matches. Industry cuts duplicate losses 80–95%.
  • Vendor bank-change alert — "⚠️ Madura's account number changed since the last payment — verify before releasing." The single most common AP fraud.
  • Anomaly watch Soon — "this payment is unlike your history" (amount / timing / frequency); split-invoice and approval-policy-violation patterns.

③ Summarizes & explains Now Wow AI

  • One-line approver brief on every payment — "Pay ₹4.2L to Madura · 12 invoices · all 3-way matched · within terms · nothing unusual." The approver decides in seconds, not minutes.
  • Ask-anything box Wow — plain Hindi/English questions over the payables: "Madura ko is season kitna dena hai?", "what's overdue this week?" — instant answer with the breakdown. (Natural-language query over the ledger is a flagship 2026 finance feature.)
  • Owner WhatsApp digest — one morning message: money in vs bank, exceptions, what's due to brands.

④ Looks ahead — only the due-date radar stays here Now AI

  • Payables aging + due-date radar — a live work-queue of what's due / overdue per brand (deadlines are data, Rule 11). Operational to running payments, so it stays.

Forecasting & optimisation are D7 Analytics, not here (Anand, 17 Jun): the cash forecast, the early-payment-discount (dynamic-discounting) optimiser, SOR/Consignment settlement projection, brand payment-behaviour insight. Payments feeds them; it doesn't run them.

⑤ The money-in audit brain Now AI

  • Three-rail auto-match — cash deposit, card settlement (net of MDR), UPI → matched to bank credits by narration/UTR with fuzzy + many-to-many logic; clears the easy 90%, surfaces only exceptions. AI recon cuts matching time ~80%.
  • Today's exception flags — this deposit is short / missing / settled slow / MDR off — raised on the day, for the day's owner to chase.

The cross-time view — ranking stores by leakage, "Store X under-deposited thrice this month", trend lines — is D7 Analytics. The audit here catches each day; analytics finds the pattern.

⑥ Brand-statement reconciler — money-out assurance Soon AI

  • Match a brand's own statement / ledger (what they say KDPS owes) against our vendor ledger (what we say) — line by line, fuzzy + many-to-many — and surface the gaps: a missed credit note, a return they didn't book, an SOR settlement double-counted.
  • Catches a dispute before it festers into a payment fight, and hands the account manager a ready reconciliation to send back.

Added 17 Jun (Anand). An inline assurance control, not analytics — it reconciles two ledgers at a point in time; the money-out mirror of the ⑤ money-in audit.

The boundary with D7 Analytics (Anand, 17 Jun): Payments keeps the inline copilot — AI that acts at the moment of a payment or a day's audit (read the mess · catch the fraud · summarise the payment · match the bank · flag today's gap). All forecasting, optimisation, ranking and profitability analysis is D7 Analytics, reading what Payments records. Clean line: controls live here, intelligence-over-time lives there.

How we apply it safely — the best-practice playbook

Industry consensus, 2026: even frontier "agentic payments" keep a human on money above a threshold, and the real barrier to AI in finance is trust, not technology. So how we ship matters as much as what we ship.

PrincipleWhat it means in Payments
1 · A confidence lineAI auto-fills only above a confidence score; below it routes to a human. The line is set by failure cost, not average accuracy — a payment release gets a far stricter line than coding a stationery bill.
2 · Always show the "why"No black box on money. "Flagged: Madura's account changed since 14 invoices ago." / "Matched on UTR + amount." The human sees the reason, not just the verdict.
3 · AI is in the audit trailEvery suggestion + what the human did with it (accepted / overridden / by whom) is logged. The machine's advice is itself auditable — Rule 10, extended to AI.
4 · Learns from every correctionOverride once → it doesn't repeat the miss (same vendor mapping, same false flag). A feedback loop is mandatory, or it never improves.
5 · Tune the flags (no alert fatigue)Keep false positives low, recalibrate from feedback, sample suppressed items. A flag nobody trusts is worse than no flag — most raw alerts are noise.
6 · Earn trust in stagesShadow → suggest → assist, never big-bang. AI runs silently in parallel first (weeks), we show match rates, then it suggests, then assists. Mirrors the store-split rollout and the build-doc risk tiers.
7 · India-compliant by designBank/payment data stays in India (RBI localisation); document what the AI reads (DPDP consent/provenance). Sectoral RBI rules prevail — build for Indian finance reality from day one.
8 · Degraded modeAI is never the system of record. If it's down, the human queue still runs — the in-app record is the truth; AI only advises.

7 · The store-collection audit Q-F locked

What the real data already tells us

From the live store DSR + bank export: this is not a flat "deposits vs cash" match — there are three rails, each behaving differently:

  • Cash — collected, some spent as petty cash, the rest physically deposited in lumps (not daily) → match against the cash-deposit credit.
  • Card / swipe — settles T+n, net of MDR (bank charge %), keyed by MID / terminal.
  • UPI — settles, keyed to the store.

KDPS's current version of this is a manual Excel sheet (per-MID, "Swip Satt Amt" vs "Swip Amt", "Bank Charge %") that is full of broken formulas — the exact pain the audit replaces.

The match, per rail — 3-way Q-F locked

RailThe 3-wayBehaviour
CardPOS gross ↔ acquirer settlement report (gross − MDR = net, UTR + MID) ↔ bank creditT+2, net of MDR, bulk credit disaggregated by the settlement report
UPIPOS gross ↔ UPI settlement report ↔ bank creditT+1
CashPOS cash ↔ deposit slip ↔ bank cash-creditLumpy (T+1–2), minus petty cash; matched over a window

The lag is carried as "expected / in-transit" until the money lands — so the audit flags only a settlement that's genuinely late, short, or missing, and can always say why a number differs (MDR vs real shortfall). Delivered phased by rail as each settlement feed comes online.

Where the data comes from — and the POS reality dependencies

  • POS collection feed (what each store collected, per rail) — through the swappable POS adapter (same pattern as outbound). The current POS (Ten Software) is limiting and old; whatever it can export we take — the audit doesn't depend on POS internals.
  • Acquirer / aggregator settlement reports (Pine Labs / Razorpay / Paytm…) — the feed that makes the card/UPI legs reconcile; client to confirm which acquirers + getting daily reports.
  • Bank statement — the read-only pull (Bank X; ICICI now).
KDPS will build its own POS (the current one is old, limiting tech) — a separate module discussion, not D4. Two plans on the table: (1) keep the current POS and request the APIs we need; (2) build a KDPS POS that does only point-of-sale while the KDPS system owns the backend. Either way the audit reads the collection feed through the adapter, so D4 doesn't change when the POS does.

8 · The Tally boundary Q-H locked

Tally stays the statutory book of record (Rule 4); KDPS Payments is the operational AP sub-ledger. The boundary is the classic sub-ledger → general-ledger seam: the sub-ledger holds full detail and posts into the GL's control accounts, carried detailed for what's audited and summary for what's just volume, with a tie-out so the two never drift.

Two truths, one direction

  • KDPS = the live truth — "what we owe Madura right now", every part-payment, aging, the party ledger. The operational answer comes from here.
  • Tally = the filed truth — GST returns, final accounts, statutory filings.
  • One-way push, no re-keying — KDPS generates the voucher, Tally receives it; the accountant stops hand-entering payments/bills (Anand, 18 Jun). Nobody types the same payment twice.

What crosses, at what grain

Crosses as detail (bill-wise voucher)Crosses as summary (periodic voucher)
Purchase bill, payment, DN / CN, store receipts — anything GST or the party ledger needs (keeps GSTIN · HSN · party · Agst Ref, so a GST notice traces inside Tally)Per-piece SOR / Consignment accruals — thousands a month; rolled into one settlement journal per brand per period off the Account Sales Report (GST on these crystallises only at settlement)

A daily AP-to-Tally tie-out reconciles the KDPS payables balance against Tally's control account — the safety net against silent drift. The exact voucher catalog (debit/credit legs, ledger names, GST classes) is D6; Q-H fixes only which events cross and at what grain.

Stack reality (18 Jun): the Patna finance team runs Vyapar TaxOne (ex-Suvit) — an accounting-automation bridge that auto-posts documents into Tally. So Tally stays the book and there's already a "stop hand-entering" precedent in their stack. This opens a D6 fork: KDPS pushes to Tally directly (own connector / XML), or KDPS hands the structured export to TaxOne and lets the tool they already trust post it. To confirm with client: Tally is still the book (TaxOne feeding it) — not that accounts have moved into Vyapar itself.

9 · How it connects

  • Offers (D5) → D4: a discount is not a brand claim — it simply lowers the sale's net price, so KDPS earns less margin (profit is derived). The brand decides the discount on brand-controlled stock, KDPS on KDPS-controlled stock; either way nothing is reclaimed. Brand money from outbound stays the existing, real flows only: SOR/Consignment payable on sale, RTV defective brand credit, the EOSS/V-flip settlement of dead stock, and goods-return DN netting.
  • Inbound → D4: the PT posting (Q37) creates the Outright/Correction payables D4 settles; the frozen ex-GST unit cost is the SOR/Consignment settlement basis.
  • Outbound → D4: per-sale SOR/Consignment payables, RTV brand credits, EOSS/V-flip settlements all flow in here.
  • Booking → D4: the Correction return allowance + payment terms set on the booking drive due dates and net-of-returns settlement.
  • D4 → D6 (Tally): every payment + every reconciliation emits a statutory event; the voucher catalog is designed in D6.

Q-A · The payment object grain Locked — Anand approved 17 Jun

The fork: when KDPS pays a brand, what does the system create and track?

Research: NetSuite, SAP and Tally all separate bill/payable (one per invoice, status open→partly-paid→paid, on the AP ledger) from payment (a disbursement allocated across one or many payables — Tally's bill-wise "Agst Ref"; NetSuite's one Bill Payment clearing many Vendor Bills). Lifecycle: approved→scheduled→executed→reconciled.

Option 1 — Payment = one per invoice (1:1). Simplest, but breaks for SOR/Consignment (payable accrues per sale, hundreds of tiny accruals) and doesn't match how KDPS actually pays a brand (one transfer for many invoices).
Option 2 — Two layers: Payable + Payment (allocation). ★ recommended. The payable is the long-lived debt (one per source doc — PT invoice, per-sale SOR accrual, return debit, credit note), carrying open→partly-paid→paid. The Payment is a separate document allocated across one or many open payables for a brand (full or partial), carrying the execution lifecycle. The universal ERP pattern; the only model that handles SOR accruals, Correction net-of-returns, and brand-level reconciliation cleanly. Subsumes Options 1 and 3.
Option 3 — Periodic brand "settlement run" only. Everything is a scheduled per-brand sweep of eligible payables. Great for SOR/Consignment, heavy for one-off Outright invoices — and it's just a special case of Option 2.

Recommendation: Option 2. The payable layer already exists in our vendor ledger; we're naming the payment document that debits it and how it allocates.

✓ Decision: Option 2 locked — two layers (Payable + Payment), bill-wise allocation.

Q-B · The payment lifecycle / state machine Locked — Anand approved 17 Jun

The fork: what states does a Payment move through, and do we adopt PASCS's six verbatim?

Research: real AP systems add unhappy-path states PASCS omits — Failed / Returned (bank bounce), Cancelled / Void, On-hold, plus an in-flight Processing (BILL, Acumatica). And reconciliation is genuinely two steps — matching then clearing (Oracle Cash Management), which is exactly PASCS's Confirmed (we captured the bank ref) vs Reconciled (the statement independently shows it cleared).

Option 1 — PASCS verbatim. Eligible→Requested→Approved→Executed→Confirmed→Reconciled on one object. Honours the client doc, but conflates the payable's "eligible" with the payment, and has no Rejected / Failed / On-hold / Cancelled — which "flag, don't block" and real bank bounces demand.
Option 2 — Two coordinated machines + real branches. ★ recommended. Payable: Open→Partly-paid→Paid (+On-hold, Cancelled), derived from allocations. Payment: Draft→Pending-approval→Approved→Executed→Confirmed→Reconciled, with Rejected / On-hold / Failed-returned / Cancelled branches. "Eligible" sits on the payable (it's the entry gate, Q-C), not the payment. Keeps Confirmed (txn ref captured = matched) distinct from Reconciled (bank statement cleared). Maps PASCS's six onto the payment spine and loses nothing.
Option 3 — Lean 4-state. Approved→Paid→Reconciled (+Failed). Minimal; drops the pre-approval work queue, the partial-payment story and the confirm/reconcile split. Too thin for a 50-store control system.

Recommendation: Option 2 — keep the client's spine, split it across the two layers, add the failure paths, preserve Confirmed≠Reconciled.

✓ Decision: Option 2 locked. Anand's refinement: the vendor ledger is kept at brand + vendor grain — payables accrue per brand, the payment anchors to the vendor (bank account) and may span the brands under it, and every partial payment posts to the ledger.

Q-C · The payment eligibility gate Locked — Anand approved 17 Jun

The fork: what must be true before a payable can be paid, and does a failed check block or flag?

Research: 3-way match (booking ↔ GRN ↔ invoice) is the core AP safeguard; on failure a hold stops payment until released. Mature systems split holds into fix-the-cause (hard) vs override-with-approval (soft), and use tolerances so trivial variances don't freeze payments (Oracle AP, Tipalti, Ramp). India adds a hard prerequisite: PAN + GSTIN + penny-drop-validated bank account before paying.

Option 1 — One hard gate, all-or-nothing. Every check must pass, no override (PASCS literal). Maximum control, but a ₹50 variance or one open line freezes a ₹5-lakh payment — staff route around the system (the Rule 5 failure).
Option 2 — Tiered gate: hard blocks + soft holds with override. ★ recommended. Hard (never overridable, fix-the-cause): no validated bank / no GSTIN (D1 pay-gate, India-mandatory); payable not tied to an inwarded PT / TIR (debt not official yet); SOR/Consignment piece not actually sold. Soft (flag + senior override w/ reason): open invoice↔GRN↔PT discrepancy; Correction over-allowance; amount over a per-brand variance tolerance. Tolerance: trivial ₹/% gaps auto-pass. Checked on the payable, re-checked at execution.
Option 3 — Soft everywhere (flag-only). Always allow, just warn. Too loose — defeats the module's whole purpose.

Recommendation: Option 2 — the only design that's both a real money gate and Rule-5-honest. The hard list is short and non-negotiable; everything else flags and needs a named senior to release.

✓ Decision: Option 2 locked (Anand: "recommendation sounds good"). Hard/soft split as above; tolerances per brand.

Q-E · Bank execution — how the money actually leaves Locked — Anand approved 17 Jun

The fork: once a payment is approved, how does it actually get paid — and does our software push money?

The tension: PASCS wants execution "via bank", but the bank-API brief deliberately scoped the API to read-only statements ("not asking to send money") — faster approval, lower risk. Research: India banks gate payment-initiation (write) APIs behind heavy auth, four/six-eyes and bulk-payout limits; read-only statement access is clean and consent-based. The reconciliation gold standard either way is the UTR flowing back and auto-closing the payable (the "payment roundtrip").

Option 1 — Manual pay + auto-confirm (read-only now). ★ recommended. System prepares the approved payment (beneficiary, amount, allocation); a human pays manually in the bank's net-banking; the UTR is captured (typed at pay-time + auto-matched from the daily read-only statement pull via the §6 narration parser). Loop still closes. Matches the bank-API brief exactly; lowest risk, fastest.
Option 2 — Bank file / H2H (SFTP) upload. System generates a payment file dropped to the bank via SFTP; bank executes the batch, returns status/UTR. Good for many SOR settlements at once; the brief's "reliable fallback." More setup; four-eyes on the file.
Option 3 — Payment-initiation API (full STP write). Approved payment → API pushes the instruction → bank executes → returns UTR. Fully closed loop, no human bank step. But: write access, heavy security/onboarding, bulk-payout limits, and the brief parked it.

Recommendation: Option 1 now, behind a swappable execution adapter (same pattern as the outbound POS adapter) so H2H (2) or the payment API (3) drops in later with no redesign. Confirmation + reconciliation are automated from day one via the read-only statement API, so the loop closes regardless of who presses "pay". Whichever mode, the Q-D four-eyes governs release and UTR capture is the linchpin.

✓ Decision (Anand, 17 Jun): Option 1 locked — manual pay + auto-confirm (read-only) for v1; payment-initiation API wanted later (v2). Behind a swappable bank adapter: the bank is a variable (3 banks; ICICI access now, not HDFC; nothing hardcoded) and the mode is swappable (manual → H2H / API). API requirement to be sent to the bank later — design first.

Q-F · The store-collection audit — matching model Locked — Anand approved 17 Jun

The fork: how do we prove each store's daily collection (cash / card / UPI) reached the bank, when the three rails behave completely differently?

Research (India retail): cash deposits lumpy (T+1–2, minus petty cash); card settles T+2, net of MDR, as a bulk credit you must disaggregate with the acquirer settlement report (Pine Labs / Razorpay / Paytm…); UPI settles T+1. The bank credit alone can't tell an MDR fee from a real shortfall — you need the settlement report (gross − MDR = net, with UTR + MID). Discrepancy types: over-settlement, under-settlement, fee mismatch.

Option 1 — 2-way: POS collection ↔ bank credit. Compare what the POS says each store collected to what hit the bank. One extra feed (bank). But card/UPI arrive net of MDR, lagged and bulked — so the match is permanently "off," and you can't tell a fee from a fraud. Noisy and weak.
Option 2 — 3-way per rail: POS → acquirer settlement report → bank. ★ recommended. Card/UPI: POS gross ↔ settlement report (gross − MDR = net, UTR) ↔ bank credit (matched by UTR). Cash: POS cash ↔ deposit slip ↔ bank cash-credit (windowed, minus petty cash). The lag is handled by carrying amounts as expected / in-transit until they land; flag only a genuinely late or short one. The only model that explains the gap (it's MDR, not theft) and still catches a real missing deposit. Mirrors KDPS's own per-MID sheet, done right.
Option 3 — Option 2, phased by rail. Start with the rails we have data for (bank + POS + UPI), add each card-acquirer settlement feed as it's wired in. Pragmatic if we don't have every settlement report on day one.

Recommendation: Option 2 as the target, delivered Option-3-style (phase by rail as settlement feeds come online). New data dependency: which card/UPI acquirers/aggregators KDPS uses + getting their daily settlement reports — a client item (mirrors the bank-API brief Q6).

Q-G · Settlement maths & credits/netting per commercial model Locked — Anand approved 17 Jun

The fork: brand money flows both ways — we owe payables, brands owe us credits (returns, RTV, EOSS/V-flip settlements, Correction over-allowance). How do these net into what we actually pay? (Note: a normal offer discount is NOT one of these — it just lowers margin, no credit owed.)

Research: best practice — match each credit note to its original invoice and net; never pay before applicable credits are applied; never leave credits floating (they create "phantom liabilities"). For SOR/Consignment, the retailer sends the brand an Account Sales Report (what sold) and pays the settlement on sold units (FreeAgent, QuickBooks; CFI/PwC consignment).

Option 1 — Net everything immediately. Any recorded credit reduces the payable at once. One balance, simple. But an unagreed claim (an EOSS settlement we've only asked for) would wrongly cut what we pay → we short-pay the brand → dispute.
Option 2 — Two-state netting: confirmed nets now, claims pend until agreed. ★ recommended. A confirmed credit (return accepted, credit note in hand, RTV credit acknowledged, Correction return within allowance) is allocated against specific open payables and reduces the payment now. A raised-but-unagreed claim (EOSS/V-flip settlement, Correction over-allowance recovery) sits as a pending claim — visible per brand — and nets only when the brand agrees (then it becomes a confirmed credit). Clean net payable + a visible pending-claims list; nothing floats.
Option 3 — Separate claims sub-ledger. Claims tracked entirely apart, settled manually, never auto-net. Cleanest separation, but heavy and manual — overkill for KDPS.

Per model (all from the spine, just applied consistently): Outright/Correction payable books at inward; a Correction return within allowance nets, over-allowance → pending recovery / V-flip; SOR/Consignment payable accrues on sale (the Account Sales Report) and unsold returns net nothing (never accrued); RTV credits & EOSS/V-flip settlements are claims until agreed. (A normal offer discount is not a claim — it only lowers margin.)

Recommendation: Option 2 — it protects the brand relationship (no surprise short-pay), keeps a clean net payable + a visible claims list, and matches both credit-note and consignment best practice. It feeds the §6 ⑥ brand-statement reconciler directly.

Real-data evidence (17 Jun — KDPS CN&DN + Invoice/Offer files): KDPS already runs exactly this by hand — it raises a Debit Note (DN) on a goods return and the brand issues a Credit Note (CN) back; the two are matched (sometimes at a slightly different amount). The sheet shows a large DN-without-CN gap (~₹1.74 Cr DN vs ~₹0.45 Cr CN) — pending claims in real rupees. Refinement the data reveals: two kinds of credit — (a) a goods-return DN is backed by a physical return, so it nets the payable now; the CN is tracked as the brand's acknowledgment (feeds §6 ⑥), not a block; (b) a commercial claim (EOSS/V-flip settlement, Correction over-allowance) has no return, so it pends until the brand agrees — and confirms at the agreed amount. Adopt KDPS vocab — DN / CN / GR / CD — and their party-ledger identity: Opening + Invoices − CD − GR − Payments + Interest = Due, which D4 produces derived. Payment terms appear here as Credit Days + CD% per supplier (the D1 §11 data, partially captured already); invoice ageing runs 300–600 days on the worst lines — the exact pain this module fixes.

✓ Decision (Anand, 17 Jun): Option 2 locked, refined by the real CN&DN + party-ledger data. Two-state netting in KDPS vocab — goods-return DN nets now (CN tracked as the brand's ack), commercial claims pend until agreed at the agreed amount. Vendor ledger = the derived party ledger (Opening + Invoices − CD − GR − Payments + Interest = Due), brand + vendor grain.

The link that applies a payment against specific open payables — the ERP-standard way one payment clears many bills.
Store-collection audit
The daily money-in check: what each store collected (cash / card / UPI) vs what landed in the bank account, across three settlement rails.
Bank adapter
The single seam behind which all bank-touching lives — swappable on two axes: which bank (3 banks; ICICI now; the bank is a variable, "Bank X") and which mode (read-only + manual pay now; H2H file or payment-initiation API later).
Debit Note (DN)
The note KDPS raises on a brand/party when it returns goods, reducing what KDPS owes. Backed by a physical return, so it nets the payable now.
Credit Note (CN)
The brand's acknowledgment of a DN (sometimes at a different amount). A DN without a matching CN is the reconciling gap with the brand — surfaced by the §6 ⑥ reconciler. KDPS's real sheet carries a large unmatched DN balance.
GR (Goods Return)
Stock sent back to a brand (defective/RTV or seasonal/Correction); its value reduces the payable — the "GR Value" in KDPS's party ledger.
CD (Cash Discount)
An early-payment discount a supplier gives for paying within the credit days; tracked as Credit Days + CD%. Capturing it is the payment-timing win (the optimiser itself lives in D7).
Party ledger
KDPS's per-party running account: Opening + Invoices − CD − GR − Payments + Interest = Due. D4 produces this as the derived vendor ledger (brand + vendor grain).
MDR / bank charge
The fee an acquirer deducts from card/UPI settlements, so the bank credit is less than the amount swiped — the audit must expect the gap.
UTR
The bank reference on a transaction — the key that matches a payment or a settlement back to its record.
Copilot, not autopilot
KDPS's stance on AI in money work: AI advises (reads, summarizes, predicts, flags) but never acts on the ledger or inside an approval — a human approves, a document posts (Rule 8). The opposite of an autonomous agent that moves money on its own.
Segregation of duties (SoD)
The control that the three highest-risk actions — change a vendor's bank details, approve a payment, release the money — never sit with one person. Enforced by role-based access; AI is a layer, not one of the duty-holders.
Dynamic discounting
Paying a brand early in exchange for a discount, timed by an AI cash forecast so KDPS captures the discount without straining cash. A 2/10 term ≈ 37% annualised return.
Bank-narration parser
The AI that reads cryptic statement text (e.g. INF/NEFT/IN…) + UTR and maps each credit to the right store, brand or payment — the join that powers both reconciliations.
Confidence-threshold routing
The core human-in-the-loop pattern: AI auto-acts only above a confidence score, routes the rest to a human. The threshold is set by the cost of being wrong, so money work gets a stricter line than low-stakes work.
Shadow mode
Running AI silently in parallel with humans first — capturing its suggestions and comparing match rates — before letting it suggest, then assist. How trust is earned in regulated finance (weeks, not a big-bang switch).
Data localisation (RBI/DPDP)
India's rule that payment data is stored within the country; AI use must document consent and training-data provenance. Sector rules (RBI) prevail over the general DPDP Act in conflict.
