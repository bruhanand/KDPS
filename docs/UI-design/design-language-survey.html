Seven ways the KDPS system could look

A design survey · 30 July 2026

We are choosing the design language for the new KDPS system before we build more screens. This page dresses the same working day in seven different directions. Nothing here is final artwork; it is a taste test with real numbers and real documents.

Scroll slowly, and imagine doing your actual work inside each one: billing a customer at eight in the evening, checking a GRN from Ranchi, chasing a transfer stuck between Deoghar and JSL. The right choice is the one that still feels good on a busy Saturday, not the one that is prettiest at first glance.

When you are done: pick a first and a second choice, and note one thing you would steal from any of the others. Send your picks on the WhatsApp group. If a direction feels right for the office but wrong for the shop floor, say that too. That is useful.

What each direction shows

  • The top of a screen where you are, and where you can go
  • Today's numbers the figures a manager glances at
  • The documents GRNs, transfers, bookings, sales
  • The till a bill in progress, and the payment moment
  • The small parts buttons, fields, and status marks

Every direction shows the same five things, but arranges them the way that direction wants to. That is deliberate: a design language is not a coat of paint, it changes what the screen puts first.

DIRECTION 1

The day book

Before Tally, every counter kept a day book: ruled entries, a red margin, totals ruled twice. Here the whole screen is that page. No cards, no boxes; entries sit directly on the rules, a posted document takes a stamp, and the till writes its bill on a memo slip like the one stapled to the bag.

Borrowed fromthe bahi khata KDPS already keeps; the double rule under a settled total

DIRECTION 2

The lookbook

Fashion's own typography. The day's sale is set the way a magazine sets a cover line: one enormous numeral, everything else small and quiet. The bill reads like a page of credits, the documents like an index. For people who want the system to feel like the brand, not the back office.

Borrowed fromlookbooks and campaign pages; the credits column of a fashion magazine

TypeBodoni Moda for numerals and titles · Archivo for working text

DIRECTION 3

Counter glass

The frosted one. Bolts of cloth lean behind a glass counter: you can see their colour but not their weave. Navigation and the day's numbers live on the glass; anything you actually read sits on solid paper-white. Calm, current, quietly premium.

Borrowed fromfrosted panels on today's operating systems; the rule that glass frames content and never carries it

DIRECTION 4

Eight o'clock

The till at closing time. The screen is the bill: a scan feed on the left, a total you can read from two metres, tender keys sized for thumbs. Amber on charcoal so a brightly lit shop doesn't wash it out. Everything that is not the sale waits its turn in a thin strip below.

Borrowed fromcash registers and card machines; the dark dashboards of serious finance tools

DIRECTION 5

The painted board

Indigo board, yellow stroke, two scripts: the lettering of every good bazaar, brought indoors and disciplined. English leads, Hindi rides under it, so a new hire in Deoghar reads the same screen as the accountant in Patna. The day's figure gets a haldi swipe, the way a signboard underlines its best price.

Borrowed fromhand-painted shop boards; Ek Type's Anek, one typeface family drawn for both scripts

DIRECTION 6

Gateway

Everyone in the Patna office already speaks Tally. This direction keeps that muscle memory: a navy screen, panels with their titles set into the border, one yellow selection bar, function keys along the bottom edge. The most familiar direction here, redrawn with care instead of nostalgia.

Borrowed fromthe Tally screens KDPS runs today; the keyboard-first habits of its accountants

DIRECTION 7

The invitation

Indian premium is printed: fine double rules, a Mithila-style band, margins that don't hurry. This is the language of a beautifully printed card from Patna, set to work as software, in typefaces drawn for Indian publishing. The boldest direction here, and the most rooted in where KDPS is from.

Borrowed fromfine wedding stationery; the double-rule borders of Mithila painting, Bihar's own line art

TypeMartel for headings and figures · Martel Sans for text, both designed for India

How to vote

  1. Scroll through all seven once, without judging.
  2. Go back to the two or three that pulled you in, and imagine a full day's work inside them.
  3. Pick a first and a second choice.
  4. Note one thing you would steal from any other direction, even one you rejected.
  5. Send your picks and your one steal on the WhatsApp group.

A note on mixing: directions blend after the vote. The day book's stamp could live inside Counter glass; the painted board's Hindi labels could ride on almost any of these; Gateway's function keys work anywhere. The vote sets the base, not a prison.
