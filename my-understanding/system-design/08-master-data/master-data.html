Master Data & Users — System Design

The nouns of the whole system — vendors, brands & terms, stores, SKUs, barcodes, season & taxonomy, offers, users — were each born quietly inside D1–D7. D8 pulls them into one owned place and answers, for every one: who owns it · who may change it · what is logged · how documents snapshot it so today's edit never rewrites yesterday's bill. This is the last consolidation before the build queue.

D8 is designed — all 11 questions locked (21 Jun 2026). The master inventory (§2), the real-file grounding (§2b), the governance model (§3), the full edit-rights grid (§4), every per-master design (§5) and the common five-rule standard (§6) are complete. In short: hybrid stewardship (one HO data owner, delegated editing, a second eye on the dangerous four) · brand terms captured fresh per brand (model/margin/return-window, named steward, brand stays draft until validated), stored as a two-axis registry (ownership × return-terms + rolling-top-up) with the friendly label derived · season a governed house calendar (Open → EOSS → Closed) · identity = the SKU cohort (barcode is a non-unique scan-alias into it) · product attributes come from the PT Master Sheet (KDPS's existing product master, promoted into governed tables) · store master = firm geography + flexible store-groups · vendor = HO-edited with bank+GSTIN second-eye · taxonomy promoted + GST as date-effective HSN+price-band data · offers placed (best-for-customer precedence) · users = configurable RBAC, shape only. What remains is purely client facts (§7, CD1–CD9) — collected after the design is shown, never guessed. POS integration is deliberately out of scope (scope note below).
Scope note — POS. D8 designs the pure, POS-agnostic system: KDPS owns the truth and is built so a better in-house, season-aware POS can take over later. Integrating the current external POS (Ten Software) is out of scope here — it's ~2 months away and unlikely (≈80%) to expose a usable API. So every master must stand on its own; any quirk of the current till is parked to the integration/rollout work, never designed into the masters. The POS requirements we sent the vendor confirm the direction: barcode is the shared key and KDPS may send its own barcodes, and the item master carries season.

1 · The one idea

D8 invents almost nothing new. Every master already exists — inbound needed an SKU, vendor management needed brand terms, payments needed users. They were defined just enough to make each module work, and most were tagged "owner = D8". This discussion does three things and stops:

  • Names one owned home for each master — so a brand's terms, a season, an SKU live in exactly one screen, not scattered across modules (Rule 3: master data lives in one place).
  • Writes the edit-rights grid — for every master: who may create it, who may change it, which changes need a second person, what gets logged (Rule 10: every action has an actor).
  • Closes the four master-level open items left hanging by earlier designs — who may edit brand terms · the season open/close ritual · barcode reuse across seasons · how the under-defined store master is shaped.

It does not re-open how SKUs are minted (D2), how offers match (D5), or how roles approve payments (D4). Those are locked. D8 only settles ownership, edit rights, audit, and snapshot — the governance skin over masters that already work.

Two rules from the architecture carry this whole discussion. Rule 3 — master data lives in one place; documents snapshot it: the master is the live reference; the moment a document is created it copies what it uses, so changing a brand's margin tomorrow never rewrites yesterday's booking. Rule 10 — every action has an actor: who created, who changed, who approved, when — on every master row, not just on documents.

3 · The governance model Q1 locked — hybrid stewardship

The house rule for who may change a master, applied to every row of the grid below. Decided 21 Jun 2026.

Hybrid stewardship — the four parts

  1. One accountable owner. A single data owner role at head office (Patna) is answerable for the quality of every master — the one throat to choke. (Which person fills it is a client fact → CD5.)
  2. Editing is delegated to the nearest role. Day-to-day create/edit sits with whoever lives closest to the list — warehouse picks season & makes SKUs, the buyer sets brand terms, the admin manages users. No bottleneck at HO for routine work.
  3. The dangerous masters need a second pair of eyes. A change only takes effect after a different person approves it (maker ≠ checker). The high-risk set: brand commercial terms GST / tax slabs vendor bank details user roles & rights.
  4. Operational, store-scoped actions stay local — a store confirming its own day's collection needs no HO gate. And every master edit is logged with who + why + when, high-risk or not (Rule 10).

This is the federated/hybrid model the industry research lands on, and it formalises what KDPS already chose by instinct: bank changes already need a second eye (D4), and the warehouse — not Patna — already picks the season (D2). Each later question (Q2–Q10) just applies this rule to one master and confirms its delegated editor + whether it's in the high-risk set.

5 · Per-master decisions complete

All locked — each master's settled design in plain words.

Brand & commercial terms Q2 locked

Shape: one terms card per brand per season, holding the full-price & discount margins, the return window, the Correction return-allowance % + basis, and the commercial model stored as two axes — ownership (KDPS-owns / brand-owns) × return-terms (none / capped / uncapped) + a rolling-top-up flag. The familiar label (Outright / Correction / SOR / Consignment) is derived from those axes, so screens still read the friendly name while the data stays robust to how the deal is actually written (a distinct deal vs Outright-with-a-return-clause land on the same record). The GST trigger keys off the ownership axis.

Captured fresh, not migrated: the raw supplier database has no model / margin / return-window — so these are entered fresh per brand by a named steward. Each brand stays in draft (not usable for booking/posting) until the steward validates it, because the commercial model drives every posting and an unconfirmed model would corrupt the books. Guards on entry: cost ≤ MRP and a canonical (non-date) season are hard blocks.

How it changes: opening a season for a brand copies last season's card forward as the starting point. The buyer adjusts it; a second person approves before it goes live (high-risk, Q1); within the season it's fixed. The commercial model rides forward unchanged unless deliberately changed.

Why it's safe & scalable: every booking snapshots the live season card at creation (Rule 3), so past deals never move. The season is the version — margin-by-season reporting is natural and history is automatic. Finer per-date control could be layered on later without changing the model.

Owner = HO data owner · editor = buyer · approver = second person · every edit logged (who + why). Plugs into the season master (Q3): a season's terms card is created when that season is opened for the brand.

Season master & the open/close ritual Q3 locked

Shape: one KDPS house season calendar. Each season is a record in a fixed format (FAMILY + Mon-YY, system-derived, never free text) with a lifecycle state: Open → EOSS / markdown → Closed. A brand-season mapping table translates each brand's incoming label ("SS26", "Spring Summer 2020", a date, or blank) into the house season.

The ritual: the HO data-owner role opens a season (it becomes pickable on bookings/PTs and sellable) and closes it (no new stock-in; it flips to reporting-only). The warehouse only picks among Open seasons at PT — it cannot open or invent one. Closing first shows a "what this affects" summary (how many pieces become EOSS / return / dead-stock candidates) and lets the buyer set a carryover flag on core (never-out-of-stock) items so they roll into the next season instead of ageing out.

What closing fires: EOSS / markdown eligibility, the return-window & V-flip checks, and dead-stock surfacing — all per cohort (barcode + season).

Corrections: a wrong season on a PT/piece is a controlled, logged, reversing correction by the HO role (not a silent edit), because identity = barcode + season. Opening/closing/correcting are all logged; closing needs no second person (reversible, reporting-only afterward).

Owner = HO data-owner role · season pick at PT = warehouse · open/close/carryover = HO role · which person holds the role = client fact (parked). Feeds Q2 (a brand's per-season terms card is born when its season opens) and Q4 (cross-season barcode reuse).

Barcode is a non-unique scan-alias; identity is the SKU cohort Q4 locked

Shape: the barcode is not the identity — it is a non-unique scan-alias that points to a SKU cohort (brand + design + colour-or-P/M/E-tier + size + season). One cohort = one product = N pieces (a count). The same barcode value can recur across seasons and within a season: by design, several physical colours of one item collapse to a single P/M/E tier and share one barcode (everything under it has the same price/brand/design/size/tier, so it is genuinely interchangeable and a sale just reduces the count). A barcode the brand reuses in a later season is simply a different cohort — no clash in our books.

At the till — never the place to resolve a true collision: a scan resolves to the cohort actually present at that store. If two season cohorts are live in one store under the same barcode, the system takes the oldest season first (matches ageing/markdown); if it genuinely can't tell, it asks the salesperson "which season?" as a one-tap tiebreak. The till never blocks a sale to disambiguate.

A true collision is caught at inbound, not at the till. The accidental case — two different-price / different-SKU items sharing one vendor barcode (which can hit branded goods too) — is detected and resolved at goods-in by the existing barcode generator: flag it and assign an internal KDPS barcode (≈68% of pieces carry no vendor barcode anyway, so the generator already runs). This is targeted, not a blanket re-barcode — the season-aware cohort identity dissolves every other case. Re-labelling a colliding arrival to suit some future external POS that can't read the cohort is a separate, parked fallback.

Barcode is frozen at stock-in (branded = vendor's, non-branded = generated, per D2); creation and any collision-fix logged; owner = HO data owner.

SKU registry governance Q5 locked

Shape: identity = brand + style(DESIGN) + colour + size = the barcode. Attributes are picked from the PT Master Sheet (controlled lists), never free-typed. Branded SKUs are kept as the vendor sends them; non-branded ones de-duplicate automatically (same vendor + style + size → reuse the SKU, add to count).

Missing colour/size: already solved in the master lists — colour falls back to a P/M/E tier, size to "Free Size". We record the coarsest honest identity and let the count carry the pieces; nothing is fabricated, nothing is blocked.

Merge / correct: creating SKUs and the auto-dedup happen at the warehouse during the PT. Merging two SKUs that turn out to be one, or correcting a wrong attribute, is a logged HO data-owner stewardship action (keep the surviving identity, re-point its stock — survivorship). Never a floor edit, because identity is load-bearing.

Owner = HO data owner · create/dedup = warehouse at PT · merge/correct = HO steward, logged · frozen onto the stock-in posting (Rule 3, D2).

Store & warehouse master Q6 locked

Shape: one master for both stores and warehouses, separated by a type field. Firm columns: store code (short mnemonic) · name · type (store/warehouse) · format (EBO / MBO / own) + associated brand if EBO · city · state → state GSTIN · address · active flag.

Grouping: geography stays firm (state/city — it drives GST and roll-ups), while clusters, tiers, offer scope and pilot sets are store-groups/tags a store can belong to many of. Offers (D5) and reports (D7) target any group; vouchers (D6) route by the firm state→GSTIN.

Owner = HO data owner · edited at HO, never store-editable · all edits logged · a store's state/GSTIN change is tax-sensitive, handled carefully · every document snapshots the store + its GSTIN at the time. Starts with the 16 software stores; the full 50+ list is owed (CD1).

Vendor & agent edit rights Q7 locked

Shape: branded vendor record (D1 fields — name, contact, GSTIN(s) per state, PAN + state derived, address, bank, payment terms). Statutory + bank identity snapshots onto each document.

Edit rights: HO/buyer creates & edits; never store-editable; every edit logged. Bank and GSTIN changes need a second eye (both are high-risk — money & tax identity); no payment without a GSTIN and validated bank on file (D4 pay-gate).

Merge: de-dup is a logged HO stewardship action with survivorship (keep the surviving party, re-point its ledger) — promotes D7's clean supplier→brand→sub-brand crosswalk into the official party master.

Scope: branded vendor record built now; agent record + full non-branded vendor record deferred to the data-input discussion (v2) — the non-branded inbound flow still runs with a minimal on-the-fly vendor (name + city). Vendor GSTIN/bank completeness is a data-quality job → CD9.

Taxonomy + GST/HSN slab master Q8 locked

Taxonomy: the PT Master Sheet lists (sub-category / type / item / fit / gender) become governed system tables, owned by the HO data owner; adding a value is a light, logged act (not high-risk). A small vendor→canonical mapping master (from the "SUGGESTED" columns) folds each vendor's own category word into KDPS's canonical category/type — AI-drafted, human-confirmed.

GST/HSN: a date-effective slab master keyed by HSN + price-band (e.g. apparel ₹2,500/piece → 5% / above → 18%, from 22 Sep 2025), held as data, never code. KDPS computes the tax per piece at PT/sale (input & output can differ); the voucher carries the breakup; Tally/TaxOne never recompute (D6). HO-owned; a rate/threshold change is high-risk → second-eye.

Collection: not a separate master in v1 — season + category already cover it; add later only if a brand needs it. HSN rides on each SKU/PT line; the slab master maps HSN + price + date → rate.

Offer rulebook placement & precedence Q9 locked

Shape (D5): offer record = brand · trigger · reward · store scope · start/optional-end dates · item scope · funder tag · "can stack?" switch · approval limit · gift handover. Brand offers AI-ingested + human-confirmed; KDPS offers authored directly; an offer beyond a set depth needs a named sign-off before going live.

Precedence (locked here): when two offers could apply to one item, best for the customer wins (lowest final price); stackable typed add-ons may sit on top; two brand offers never stack on one item.

Owner = HO data owner · editor = offer author (brand-ingest + KDPS) · store scope via Q6 store-groups, no auto-enrol · per-line snapshot onto each bill + daily reconcile + month-end pack (D5).

Users & roles lifecycle Q10 locked · shape only

Shape: configurable RBAC — the system stores a role → screen/action → allow matrix as data, so new roles or permission tweaks are configuration, not code. User record: name · phone/email · role(s) · data-scope (store / store-group from Q6 / region) · active flag · created & last-login.

Scoping: a store user sees only their own store; HO sees all — reuses the Q6 store-groups, no new grouping concept.

Lifecycle: create by Admin; a move is an atomic role swap (remove old + add new together); a leaver is deactivated and fully revoked, never deleted (keep the audit), with delegation to a backup — guards against role creep.

Role changes need a second eye + are logged (role-change history + login trail); separation of duties (maker ≠ checker ≠ payer) carries from D4. Owner = Admin role · the actual user types, hierarchy and people are the client's to define after the design is shown (CD5).

6 · The common standard — one rule for every master Q11

The five rules every master obeys, so the per-master sections above don't each re-state them. This is the consolidation's backbone.

  1. Audit on everything. Every create / edit / merge / deactivate records who + why + when. Nothing is ever hard-deleted — a master row is deactivated and kept, so history always explains the present (Rule 10).
  2. Second eye on the dangerous four. A change to brand commercial terms GST / tax slabs vendor bank + GSTIN user roles & rights takes effect only after a different person approves it (maker ≠ checker). Everything else is edit-then-logged.
  3. Documents snapshot the master (Rule 3). The moment a document is created it copies the master values it uses (terms, price, season, GSTIN, offer rule). Editing a master tomorrow never rewrites yesterday's document.
  4. Version only at the grain that matters. We don't make every master fully time-versioned. Brand terms version by season; GST versions by effective date; everywhere else the current value + the audit log + document snapshots are enough. Simple, not over-built — and finer dating can be added later without rework.
  5. Controlled vocabularies, one owner. Product attributes come from controlled lists (the promoted PT Master Sheet), never free text. One HO data owner is accountable for every master; merges and corrections are stewardship actions with survivorship (keep the surviving record, re-point what pointed at the loser), always logged.

Q1 · Who is allowed to change the master lists? LOCKED 21 Jun — Option C, hybrid stewardship

Decision: one accountable data owner at HO; day-to-day editing delegated to the role nearest each master; the four dangerous masters (brand terms · GST slabs · vendor bank · user roles) require a second person to approve; store-scoped operational actions stay local; every edit logged. Full model in §3.

Every master needs one answer to "who can change this, and what stops a mistake or a fraud." We can set one house rule now and apply it to each master, instead of arguing it nine times. The choice is how tight to make it.

Option A — Everything central. One office team (Patna HO) creates and edits every master. Stores and warehouse staff only use the lists, never change them. Safest and most consistent — one source of truth. But slow: opening a new season, fixing a wrong SKU, adding a store all wait on HO, which is exactly the bottleneck big-MDM writeups warn about.
Option B — Each list owned by whoever is closest. Warehouse owns SKU/barcode/season, the buyer owns brand terms, the admin owns users, and so on — each edits their own freely. Fast and natural. But with 50+ stores and one developer, "everyone edits their own" is how lists quietly drift apart and two versions of the truth appear.
Option C — Hybrid stewardship (recommended). One named data owner at HO is accountable for every master (the single throat to choke), but day-to-day editing is delegated to the role nearest each list (warehouse picks the season, buyer sets brand terms, admin manages users). The few high-risk masters — brand commercial terms, GST slabs, vendor bank details, user roles — need a second person to approve the change before it takes effect. Everything store-facing and operational stays local. This is the federated/hybrid model the research calls the winner, and it matches what KDPS has already chosen by instinct: bank-change is second-eye (D4), the warehouse — not Patna — picks the season (D2). Q1 just makes it the house rule and lists which masters are "high-risk."
Option D — Central, but stores can "request a change". Like A, but stores raise a request and HO approves it. A middle ground, but it adds a request queue for everything and still bottlenecks at HO — heavier than C for no extra safety.

Research folded in: centralized MDM = one source of truth but "what took hours now takes weeks"; decentralized = agile but fragmentation; hybrid/federated (central governance + local stewardship, second-eye on sensitive domains) is the repeatedly-recommended balance. Sources: First San Francisco Partners — choosing a model, Domo — why hybrid wins, Item master data best practices (retail), MDM in ERP — governance & stewardship.

Q2 · When a brand's terms change, how do we handle it? LOCKED 21 Jun — Option C, terms per brand per season

Decision: brand terms live as one card per brand per season; opening a season copies last season's card forward as defaults; the buyer adjusts and a second person approves; within the season terms are fixed; the commercial model carries forward unless deliberately changed. Chosen for scalability/flexibility — the season is the version, history is automatic, finer date-control can be added later without rework. Detail in §5.

A brand's terms = its commercial model (Outright / Correction / SOR / Consignment), its two margins (full-price & discount), its return window and its Correction return-allowance. From Q1 we already know who: the buyer changes them, a second person approves (high-risk). The open part is how the master remembers terms over time. Note one thing is already handled: every booking copies its terms the moment it's created (Rule 3), so past bookings never change no matter what we pick here — this is only about the master's own memory and how staff set terms.

Option A — One current card per brand; overwrite + log. Each brand has one live terms card. Changing it records who/why/when and needs a second person. Old bookings already kept their own copy, so nothing breaks. Simplest. But the master itself can't tell you "what were Madura's terms last March" — you'd have to dig through old bookings. Reporting margin-by-season gets awkward.
Option B — Dated versions (valid-from date). Changing terms adds a new version stamped with a start date instead of overwriting; the system always knows which terms were live on any date. Second person approves each version. This is the textbook ERP way to keep price/margin history. Most flexible — but heavier, and it lets terms change on any random date, which isn't really how brands work.
Option C — One card per brand per season; carry last season forward (recommended). Terms live as one card per brand per season. Opening a new season for a brand copies last season's terms as a starting point; the buyer tweaks the margins / return-allowance, a second person approves, and within the season they're fixed. The commercial model just rides along unchanged unless you deliberately change it. Matches how KDPS already works — the return-allowance is already tracked per brand per season, margins live in seasonal PT files, offers are per season. History comes for free (the season is the version), margin-by-season reporting is natural, and it plugs straight into the season master (Q3). Simpler than B because terms only change at season boundaries, not on arbitrary dates.

Research folded in: effective-dated history (SCD Type 2 — valid-from/valid-to) is the standard for keeping price/margin history so past analysis stays valid; maker-checker / four-eyes on vendor & price-master changes is standard ERP/SOX practice (the person entering a sensitive change shouldn't be the one approving it) — which Q1 already adopted. Option C applies the history idea at the grain the business actually uses (the season) instead of arbitrary dates. Sources: SCD Type 2 — effective-dated history, history tables / versioning explained, vendor master governance & maker-checker, maker-checker (four-eyes).

Q3 · Who owns the season, and what does opening / closing one actually do? LOCKED 21 Jun — Option B, governed house calendar

Decision: one KDPS house season calendar owned by the HO data-owner role, with a lifecycle Open → EOSS/markdown → Closed. Warehouse only picks among Open seasons at PT (can't open/invent one). Opening & closing are single HO-role acts, logged; closing shows a "what this affects" summary and lets the buyer mark carryover for core items. Brand season labels are mapped into the house season. A wrong season is fixed by a controlled, logged, reversing correction (never a silent edit). Closing needs no second person (reversible, reporting-only afterward). Which person holds the HO role = client fact (parked). Detail in §5.

Season is the most load-bearing master in the system: the booking, the PT and stock identity itself (the unit key is barcode + season) all hang on it, and EOSS, aging, the return window, dead-stock and carryover all run per season. Today there's only a stop-gap rule (the warehouse picks a system-made season code when it makes the PT). D8 has to name the real owner and design the open/close ritual. The real files add urgency: every brand spells season differently (SS26 / "SPRING SUMMER(May-26)" / "Spring Summer 2020" / a raw date / nothing at all) — so the master must also normalise.

Option A — A plain controlled list, no lifecycle. Season is just a tidy dropdown in a fixed format; HO adds the next one; "closing" is informal (people stop picking it). Simple, but it throws away the whole point: nothing fires at season-end, so EOSS, returns, dead-stock and carryover have no trigger. The critical-path value is lost.
Option B — Season as a calendar with a lifecycle (recommended). One KDPS house season calendar, owned at HO. Each season moves through clear states: Open (pickable on bookings/PTs, sellable) → EOSS / markdown (clearance pricing, return-window & V-flip checks fire, aging surfaces) → Closed (no new stock-in under it; reporting only). The warehouse only picks among Open seasons at PT — it can't invent or open one. Opening & closing are HO acts, logged; closing shows a "this is what it affects" summary first (how many pieces become EOSS / return candidates) and lets the buyer set carryover for core items into the next season. Each brand's incoming season label is mapped into the house season. Fixing a wrong season is a controlled, logged correction (a reversing move, since identity = barcode + season — never a silent edit). This is exactly how fashion systems run the season — a governed calendar that gives every downstream rule a clean trigger, plus carryover for never-out-of-stock cores. Scalable: any number of seasons, optional years, year-round (NOS) lines, and messy brand labels all fold into one house calendar.
Option C — A separate season calendar per brand. Each brand runs its own seasons (closer to how brands actually name them). More faithful to each brand, but it multiplies calendars and makes cross-brand aging, dead-stock and reporting painful. The industry norm is one house calendar with brand seasons mapped in — which is what B does, without the sprawl.

Research folded in: fashion ERPs keep a multi-season calendar with milestones (receipt → launch → markdown) and an explicit end-of-season lifecycle; carryover flagging re-integrates core/NOS styles into the next season's plan; end-of-season planning is centralised on a single version of truth with clear price/move rules; master data is structured by Season → Collection → Style with a named owner for quality and clear rules for adding new entries; systems support extensive season codes, optional years and year-round styles. Sources: Fashion ERP — multi-season calendar & milestones, seasonal inventory lifecycle & carryover, centralised end-of-season planning, apparel master data by season/collection/style.

Q4 · How does the system handle a non-unique barcode (reused across or within a season)? LOCKED 21 Jun — Option A at the till + true collisions fixed at inbound

Decision: the barcode is a non-unique scan-alias; identity is the SKU cohort (brand + design + colour-or-P/M/E-tier + size + season). The same barcode can recur across and within a season (physical colours collapsing to one P/M/E tier share it, by design). At the till a scan matches the cohort present at that store; if two season cohorts are live under one barcode, take the oldest season first; if truly ambiguous, one-tap "which season?" to the salesperson — never block. A true collision (two different-price / different-SKU items under one vendor barcode, branded included) is caught at inbound: flag it and assign an internal KDPS barcode via the existing generator — targeted, not a blanket re-barcode. Detail in §5.

Our system — and the season-aware POS we'll build — keys on the SKU cohort, so a barcode reused in a different season is internally a non-issue: the two are simply different cohorts. (Whatever the current external till can or can't do is parked — see the scope note.) Two questions remain: when the same barcode number is live in two seasons at the same store at once, how does a scan know which cohort it is decrementing — and what about a genuine collision where one barcode really does cover two different products?

Option A — Resolve by the store's own stock, oldest-season-first (recommended). A scan matches the cohort actually allocated/present at that store. In the rare case both seasons' stock sit in the same store under one barcode, the system takes the oldest season first (clears old stock, exactly how aging & markdown already work). If it genuinely can't tell, it asks the salesperson "which season?" as a one-tap tiebreak. No stickers, no extra work — the system just knows, because it tracks season where a bare-barcode till can't. Reuse becomes a non-event.
Option B — Mint a unique KDPS code for every cohort at goods-in. Give each cohort its own guaranteed-unique code so a barcode can never point at two things. Bulletproof, but means re-stickering huge volumes of vendor pieces for a problem the barcode+season key already solves. Over-engineered for the pure system.
Option C — Assign an internal barcode at inbound, but only for a true collision (adopted alongside A). When goods-in detects that one vendor barcode genuinely covers two different-price / different-SKU items (the accidental case, branded included), the existing barcode generator flags it and mints an internal KDPS code so the two never share an alias. Targeted, not a blanket re-sticker — ≈68% of pieces carry no vendor barcode anyway, so the generator already runs at inbound. This handles the one case the season-aware cohort can't dissolve on its own, and keeps the till clear (no collision is ever resolved at the counter). Re-labelling purely to suit a future bare-barcode external POS stays a separate parked fallback.

Research folded in: GS1 now forbids reusing a GTIN once assigned (pre-2019 apparel allowed reuse after ~30 months), yet manufacturers (and non-GS1 local vendors) do put an old code on a new product — the documented failure is "old stock + new product, same code → inventory confusion." A season-aware identity (barcode + season) dissolves that at the source; only a bare-barcode external system would ever need re-labelling. Sources: GS1 GTIN reuse policy (no reuse), GS1 UK — reusing barcode numbers, same-code collision in real inventories, why reusing barcodes breaks inventory.

Q5 · SKU registry — what to do when colour/size aren't stated, and who fixes a wrong SKU? LOCKED 21 Jun — Option A (already in the PT Master Sheet) + HO stewardship for merge/correct

Decision (confirmed by the PT Master Sheet, not newly invented): when colour isn't stated the PT records a P/M/E tier and when size isn't stated it records "Free Size" — both are already values in the COLOR/SIZE master lists, so no detail is fabricated and goods always move (Option A). Merging two SKUs that are really one, or correcting a mis-keyed one, is a logged HO data-owner stewardship action (keep the surviving identity, re-point its stock); never a floor edit. See §2b key finding and §5.

Most of the SKU registry is already locked: identity = brand + style + colour + size (which is the barcode); branded SKUs are kept as the vendor sends them; non-branded ones are de-duplicated automatically (same vendor + style + size → reuse the existing SKU, add to its count). Two governance gaps remain. (1) The real files show small local vendors often don't state colour or size (Suvidhi has no colour column; Amidhara's size is rough) — so what identity do we record? (2) Who may merge two SKUs that turn out to be the same, or fix a mis-keyed one?

Option A — Record the coarsest honest identity (recommended). When colour isn't written, use a P/M/E tier (premium / medium / economy by price band); when size isn't on the invoice, use "Free Size". We never invent detail we can't see — many pieces may share one identity/barcode and the count carries them. Honest and flag-don't-block: goods always move, and we don't fabricate a colour/size that would just be wrong. This is the interim rule from inbound — Q5 confirms it.
Option B — Always force a real colour + size. The warehouse must enter a genuine colour and size for every piece before it's accepted. Textbook apparel data — every colour×size its own SKU. But for the local supply base it means either guessing (dishonest data) or stopping goods at the door (breaks the rule). Impractical here.
Option C — Honest-coarse by default, force real capture above a threshold. Collapse is fine for cheap basics; for higher-value pieces or key categories, require a real colour/size. A sensible refinement — but it needs a threshold rule the business doesn't have yet. Better added later onto A once a category proves it needs the detail.

Who may merge / correct an SKU — design default (from Q1)

Creating SKUs and the automatic non-branded de-dup happen at the warehouse during the PT. But merging two SKUs that turn out to be one, or correcting a wrong style/colour/size, is a controlled stewardship action by the HO data-owner role — logged, with the surviving identity kept and its stock re-pointed (survivorship). It is never a floor edit, because identity is load-bearing. Confirm or adjust.

Research folded in: MDM practice splits the roles — a data owner defines "good data" (required fields, naming) and a steward gatekeeps changes, reviews anomalies and approves merges; de-dup = match → survivorship → merge, with sensitive changes routed for approval. Apparel guidance wants every colour×size as its own SKU and warns missing size is the #1 fulfilment error — but that assumes the data exists; for KDPS's local vendors the honest-coarse identity avoids fabricating it. Sources: golden record — match/survivorship/merge, retail item-master governance (owner + steward), apparel SKU variation control, data survivorship on merge.

Q10 · Users & roles — the shape only LOCKED 21 Jun — all 4 confirmed

Decision: (1) roles & permissions are configurable data (a role→action→allow matrix), not hardcoded; (2) user record carries role(s) + data-scope via Q6 store-groups; (3) joiner/mover/leaver with atomic role swaps and revoke-not-delete; (4) role changes need a second eye + full audit, SoD from D4. Per Anand's direction, D8 designs only the shape. The actual user types, hierarchy and who-holds-what are the client's to define after they see the whole system (CD5) — so the shape must be flexible enough to hold whatever they hand over, without a rebuild.

1. Roles & permissions are configurable data, not hardcoded. The system stores a role → screen/action → allow matrix as data. New roles or permission tweaks are configuration, not code. Adopt the five-rule standard.
