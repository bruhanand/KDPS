Tally Sync — System Design

The last edge contract. Every other module already emits business events; D6 turns those events into GST-ready vouchers and gets them into Tally — the statutory book of record — safely, once, and reconciled. KDPS stays the live operational truth; Tally stays the filed truth. Picks up the grain decided in Payments Q-H and the TIR purchase export designed in Inbound Q20.

Where D6 stands (updated 25 Jun 2026). The voucher design is done: push route, sales grain, money-in legs, numbering/exactly-once, GST handling, and the daily safety-net are all locked (see grilling log). The "sale or return" purchase timing is now sharpened (§7): GST recognition follows the supplier tax invoice, giving two recognition paths guarded by a recognise-once stamp — only the per-brand path classification waits on the CA. The cross-GSTIN inter-store transfer is locked to taxable IGST (Q5 Part 3) throughout. Implementation is not a priority for ~2 months — the actual go-live / cutover is deliberately left to the rollout discussions (D8/D9). Five small facts are parked for the client (C1–C5). Nothing here blocks the build queue.

1 · The one idea

D6 does not invent any accounting. The spine and payments already booked every event — a purchase at inbound, a sale and its cash at outbound, a payment and a credit note in payments. D6 is the controlled bridge that copies those events into Tally as proper vouchers, exactly once, with a daily tie-out so the two books never silently drift.

Four rules this module obeys (from the architecture)

  • One owner per fact — Tally owns statutory truth; KDPS owns operational truth. We feed Tally, never replace it (Rule 4).
  • Integrations are contracts, reconciled daily — defined push, defined join key (voucher number), retry-safe, daily reconciliation, failure queue (Rule 7).
  • One-way push, no re-keying — KDPS generates the voucher, Tally receives it; the accountant stops hand-entering (locked in D4 Q-H).
  • Flag, never block — a rejected voucher goes to a failure queue, not a phone call; the floor never waits on Tally (Rule 5).

3 · The stack reality — Vyapar TaxOne shapes the push route

KDPS's Patna finance team already runs Vyapar TaxOne (formerly Suvit) — an AI accounting-automation bridge. What its own documentation says it does:

  • It writes vouchers straight into Tally. A desktop Tally Connector (Windows app on the same machine as Tally) points at the company folder with write permission and posts vouchers — "one-click sync to Tally, no exports, no manual typing." Tally stays the book.
  • Its native input is structured Excel / PDF — sale/purchase invoices and bank statements; it converts them to structured entries, the accountant reviews, then pushes to Tally in one click.
  • Also does GST auto-validations and WhatsApp document collection — not relevant to us, but explains why finance trusts it.

Source: taxone.vyapar.com — Data Automation feature, desktop Tally-connector help, third-party-integration explainer (researched 20 Jun 2026).

Why this matters: TaxOne's front door is exactly the clean structured export our system already produces. So instead of building and babysitting our own Tally connector, we can hand TaxOne a clean file and let the tool finance already trusts post it. The wrinkle: TaxOne is built to re-read messy PDFs — we want it doing a deterministic import of already-clean data, not re-interpreting our numbers.

6 · The recon loop & failure queue Q7 locked

A small daily safety net so nothing silently fails to reach Tally:

  • Daily check (tie-out): compare what KDPS sent vs what Tally accepted, matched on the unique voucher number (Q4). Totals match → green; else it lists exactly which vouchers didn't land.
  • "Needs attention" list (failure queue): any refused voucher shows with a plain-words reason ("brand name not found", "already there"); it's fixed and re-sent. The shop floor never waits — back-office cleanup only (Rule 5: flag, never block).
  • Owner: the Patna accountant (already lives in Tally). Cadence: once a day.

Rule: send → check next day → fix the few that bounced. Nothing is ever assumed to have arrived.

7 · SOR & Consignment — when GST is recognised Q6 · locked (path per brand pending CA)

For a sale-or-return / consignment brand, the key question is when the purchase (and the input tax credit on it) hits Tally. The answer is simple and important: GST recognition follows the brand's supplier tax invoice — not the moment the goods physically move. Which means there are two paths, and which one a brand is on is a property of the brand, not of any single shipment.

PathHow the brand billsWhat Tally records, and when
A · Consignment-as-agent
(Schedule I — supply to an agent)		 A proper tax invoice travels with the goods at dispatch. The GST is legally due at dispatch even though KDPS only sells later. Purchase voucher + input GST (ITC) at arrival, off that invoice — exactly like an outright purchase. The later sale/settlement journal carries no GST (it is money settlement only — pay-on-sale, return-on-unsold).
B · True sale-or-return
(Rule 55 delivery challan; invoice under Sec 31(7))		 Goods arrive on a delivery challan, not a tax invoice. The brand raises its tax invoice only when the item sells, or at the 6-month deemed-supply mark, whichever is earlier. No purchase, no ITC at arrival — stock is held quantity-only. The purchase voucher + input GST is created at the sale (settlement), when the brand's invoice legally arrives.
The point both paths share: stock quantity is in KDPS from arrival either way (the count is real); what differs is purely when the value, the payable and the tax credit are booked — and that is decided by where the brand's tax invoice legally sits, never by the stock movement. Carry a per-brand-per-season "GST recognition point" attribute (at-arrival vs at-sale) right next to the commercial-model flag in the brand master; the voucher builder reads it to pick the path.
The recognise-ONCE guard. The real danger is booking the same GST twice — once on an arrival purchase voucher and again on a settlement journal. To make that impossible, every SOR/consignment lot carries a stamp gst_recognised = (date, voucher_no). It starts empty; the first voucher that books the GST writes it; any later voucher that would book GST on the same lot checks the stamp first and refuses if it is already set. So GST lands on exactly one voucher per lot, by construction — Path A writes it at arrival, Path B writes it at sale, neither can write it twice.
Pending the CA (per-brand path): which path a given brand is on — agent-under-Schedule I (Path A) vs true sale-or-return-under-31(7) (Path B) — is a tax characterisation we do not guess. Verified research points to KDPS's dominant regime being principal-agent consignment under Schedule I (Path A), but the per-brand classification is a CA call (parked as C5). The two-path machinery and the recognise-once guard are built either way; only the per-brand GST recognition point value waits on the ruling.

The deferred-billing direct-receipt case (any brand)

A related case from the booking-less direct-to-store receipt (D2 / inbound Q9): goods can arrive and sell before the supplier invoice exists. Here Tally records the stock-in as a "received but not billed" accrual (SRBNB) at receipt — quantity and a held value, but no vendor payable and no ITC yet. The purchase voucher with input GST is created on invoice-match, when the vendor's tax invoice arrives and is reconciled against the GRN/PT (ITC then gated on it appearing in GSTR-2B). Same principle as the two SOR paths: the tax credit follows the tax invoice, never the goods movement.

Grilling log

Q1 · The push route Locked — Hybrid (C), 20 Jun 2026

Settled by our own rules (not debated): one-way push, no re-keying; KDPS = operational truth, Tally = statutory truth; a structured export is the contract whichever route wins.

Research: TaxOne posts to Tally via a Windows desktop connector and natively ingests structured Excel/PDF. Separately, Tally itself documents a native voucher import — XML over HTTP to the Tally gateway (default port 9000), or a one-click vouchers.xml import — so a self-built direct connector is a bounded, documented integration, not a research project.

A — Direct. Own Tally XML/TDL connector. Full control, no dependency; but we build and run a Windows bridge and own every Tally version quirk.
B — Via TaxOne. Clean export → TaxOne → Tally. Reuses what finance already trusts; but couples our format to their importer and to a third-party tool's roadmap.
C — Hybrid (export-as-contract). ★ LOCKED. The deterministic structured export is the real deliverable and the contract. TaxOne is the default importer now; direct-XML is the fallback AND the future self-built replacement path. Because the export is identical either way, replacing TaxOne later is not a new project — it is promoting the fallback to default.

Anand's decisions (20 Jun):

  • Scope stays open to replace TaxOne with our own software. Initially use TaxOne; in future build our own Tally connector to replace it. The hybrid keeps that door open at near-zero extra cost.
  • "Rebuild all of TaxOne" is not the task — most of TaxOne is messy-PDF AI reading + GST OCR + WhatsApp collection, which we never need because our system is already the clean structured source. The only piece we'd build is the Tally voucher push (= Route A), a bounded medium effort.
  • Deterministic Excel-template import into TaxOne is confirmed (Anand, 20 Jun) — it reads the template as-is, no AI re-interpretation. The earlier "TaxOne might re-read our numbers" concern is retired.
Q2 · Sales voucher grain Locked — Hybrid by customer type, 20 Jun 2026

The rule (same as the payables grain): detail where GST/party needs it, summary where it's just volume. GST law agrees — B2C sales are reported only as consolidated per-rate totals in GSTR-1; B2B (customer GSTIN) must be invoice-level so the buyer gets input credit.

A — Per-bill vouchers. One Tally sales voucher per POS bill. Thousands/store/month → Tally bloat; GSTR-1 discards the detail for B2C anyway. Per-item detail already lives in KDPS.
B — Daily consolidated per store. One sales voucher per store per day, split by GST slab + HSN summary, under that store's state GSTIN. Lean, matches GSTR-1's B2C consolidation.
C — Hybrid by customer type. ★ LOCKED. Default = B (daily consolidated per store per GST rate). Carve-out: any sale with a customer GSTIN gets its own per-bill detail voucher so the buyer's input credit + invoice-level GSTR-1 work. Future-proof if B2B sales grow.

Open factual input: how often KDPS issues a GST invoice to a registered (B2B) customer — if essentially never, B2B stays a latent capability; if regular, build the carve-out fully now. This is only the revenue+GST leg; the money-in (receipt) leg is Q3.

Q3 · Money-in / receipt voucher Locked — combined daily voucher, 20 Jun 2026

Decision: one combined daily sales voucher per store carries both the earned side (revenue + GST) and the collected side (the day's tender split as the money-in legs). Card & UPI land in clearing ledgers that the later bank settlement clears — absorbing the settlement lag & fees, which the KDPS money-collection audit already watches.

Tender legs (Anand, 20 Jun): Cash UPI Debit/Credit card Store credit Exchange adjustment. Each maps to its own Tally ledger (cash-in-hand, UPI-clearing, card-clearing, store-credit liability, exchange nets within the sale).

Q4 · Voucher numbering & exactly-once Locked — KDPS-owned number, 20 Jun 2026

Research: Tally has a native "prevent duplicate voucher number" guard; best-practice integrations carry an idempotency key so retries don't double-post.

Decision (Option B): KDPS generates a deterministic, human-readable voucher number (e.g. DEO-SAL-20260620-001 = store · type · date · sequence) and pushes it as the Tally voucher number with prevent-duplicates ON. That single number is both the idempotency guard (a retry bounces off Tally → exactly-once) and the join key for the daily tie-out. Tally auto-numbering rejected — it loses the join key and duplicates on retry.

First-live-test check (not blocking): confirm our number passes through TaxOne unchanged rather than being reassigned. Deterministic template import is confirmed, so it should.

Q5 · GST on each voucher + two GST numbers Part 1 & 3 locked · Part 2 → client (C3), 20 Jun 2026

Note: apparel slabs changed 22 Sep 2025 (now 5% up to ₹2,500/piece, 18% above) — proves GST must be dated data, never hardcoded.

Part 1 — locked: KDPS works out the tax once, at the sale, using the right slab for that date. The voucher carries that tax breakup as-is. Tally and TaxOne never recompute GST — one calculator, everyone else records.

Part 2 — parked for client (C3): whether Bihar & Jharkhand are one Tally company with two GST numbers, or two separate companies. Decides how we address each store's vouchers. Both are easy for us.

Part 3 — locked: stock moving between the two states is a taxable supply — needs an IGST tax invoice (an out-voucher at the sender, a matching purchase voucher at the receiver who claims the tax back). A move inside one state is just a stock move — no Tally voucher, only the stock ledger.

Q6 · When Tally sees the purchase & the GST ("sale or return" brands) Locked — two recognition paths + recognise-once guard, 25 Jun 2026

Anand's 20-Jun premise: the real KDPS process is — every goods arrival comes with an invoice → invoice becomes a PT file → loaded into the POS back office (stock-in) → POS sales report feeds Tally on the selling side. Brand ledgers are built from invoices in + payments out. The first read (Option A) was: book the purchase from the invoice at arrival for every brand, the SOR difference being "money only, not timing".

Why that needed sharpening (25 Jun, F9): booking an input-GST purchase voucher at arrival for every brand and running an SOR/consignment settlement journal risks recognising the same GST twice. The fix is to anchor recognition to the one place GST legally lives — the supplier's tax invoice — not to the goods movement.

Refined decision (Option 2 — two paths, keyed to a per-brand-per-season "GST recognition point"):

Path A — Consignment-as-agent (Schedule I). A tax invoice travels with the goods at dispatch → purchase + input GST (ITC) booked at arrival, like an outright purchase; the later settlement journal carries no GST (money settlement only).
Path B — True sale-or-return (Rule 55 challan, Sec 31(7)). Goods arrive on a delivery challan, no tax invoice; the brand invoices at sale, or at 6 months, whichever first → no purchase / no ITC at arrival (quantity-only); the purchase + input GST is booked at the sale (settlement).

The recognise-ONCE guard (F9): every SOR/consignment lot carries gst_recognised = (date, voucher_no). The first voucher to book the GST writes the stamp; any later voucher checks it and refuses if already set. GST therefore lands on exactly one voucher per lot — Path A at arrival, Path B at sale — never both.

Still true (unchanged from 20 Jun): stock quantity is in KDPS from arrival on both paths; the brand is paid only when the item sells, and unsold stock returns with a credit note — that payment/return timing is handled on the payments side.

Pending CA (path per brand, parked as C5): which brand sits on Path A vs Path B is a tax characterisation — verified research leans to principal-agent consignment under Schedule I (Path A) as KDPS's dominant regime, but the per-brand call is the CA's. The machinery and the once-guard are built either way; only the GST recognition point value per brand waits on the ruling.

Deferred-billing direct receipt (Q9): for goods that arrive and sell before any supplier invoice exists (booking-less direct-to-store receipt), Tally records a "received but not billed" accrual (SRBNB) at receipt — quantity + held value, no payable, no ITC — and the purchase voucher with input GST is created on invoice-match (ITC gated on GSTR-2B). Same rule: the tax credit follows the tax invoice, not the goods.
