3 · What's genuinely strong

Keep these — they are the product's edge. Research confirmed every one as mainstream best practice.

  • The ledger spine is real engineering. Documents write append-only ledgers; corrections are reversing entries; balances are sums, never typed. The 5 layers form a clean one-way chain — the only shape a one-developer build can hold together.
  • AI is kept at the edges, on purpose. LLMs explicitly excluded from forecasting and from every rupee of math; the chat layer answers through a governed metrics layer, never raw SQL. This is the single most important safety call in an AI-heavy ERP — and it's drawn correctly.
  • Four commercial models as a first-class dimension (Outright / Correction / SOR / Consignment), set on the brand and snapshotted onto each booking. Indian incumbents (Ginesys, GoFrugal) bolt SOR on as a flag — none model it first-class. That's a defensible product edge.
  • D3's "one machine, eight settings" collapses sale / exchange / transfer / return / RTV / EOSS / stocktake into one document→ledger→money→audit pattern. Conceptual leverage that makes the build navigable.
  • D5 is a genuinely data-driven offer engine (trigger + reward + dials; new patterns are config, not code) attaching to items, not the bill — the correct, non-obvious call for a multi-brand store.
  • Hosting & AI-cost docs are senior-grade — sourced legal grounding, honest arithmetic (~₹25k/mo, AI < ₹2k, the DB dominates), and they argue against their own pick. Self-correcting throughout (the killed claim/billback model, the SOR rethink — all dated).

B · Missing pieces (needed before / during build)

  • The buildable artifacts don't exist yet — unified data model, double-entry posting catalog ("the heart of correctness", per your own docs), state machines, golden files, feature inventory. This is the #1 gap across everything.
  • D9 migration & rollout — entirely undesigned; migration + training are the top-2 ERP failure causes (38% / 42%).
  • POS fallback architecture — if Ten Software has no usable API, the "build our own minimal POS" fork is a huge latent scope jump treated casually in the meeting. Make it a conscious go/no-go with an August deadline.
  • GST mechanics under-specified — discount-split-back-to-lines across mixed HSN slabs, GST on exchanges and the V-flip, SOR tax-invoice timing. Needs a dedicated CA-assisted pass.
  • E-invoicing (IRN) + e-way bills — statutory (you're > ₹5cr PAN-wise) but deferred to "later" and owned by no module. IRN must be independent of the Tally path.
  • Reversal/void into Tally — exactly-once is designed only for the happy path; a corrected bill after the daily voucher pushed has no defined reversal.

C · Challenge before you build it

  • The "target hold-back" (D7, graded C). Re-dating / re-numbering / month-shifting bills to hit brand targets shades into GST period-shifting and misrepresentation, with no reconciliation spec. Decide whether this should exist at all before building tooling for it. (Note: your live monthly-report duty is already partly blocked here — the AI refuses the manipulation step on ethical grounds.)
  • The 21-Jun AI ambition (an MCP bot reading any data via chat/WhatsApp, per-user analytics pages, AI voice-calls, a native app) is in no requirements doc, isn't cost-bounded, and a read-everything bot edges toward "AI in the core" — against your own Rule 8. Make a conscious scope + rule decision, don't drift into it.

D · Client must-haves not yet covered

  • Attendance & Payroll (WAPCS) — a named PRD must-have with no design folder and a hardware contradiction (store Android + face vs browser + GPS). Decide in/out explicitly; if in, it needs its own discussion.
  • POS / billing / loyalty — the PRD assumed you'd build it; your later docs correctly rule it out of scope (Ten Software owns it). That's the right call — but flag the reversal to the client, don't drop it silently.
  • The CA-led facts (number of GSTINs/entities, one Tally company or two, e-invoicing applicability, SOR invoice vs challan) gate D6 vouchers, D4 P&L and SOR booking at once — one conversation unblocks several modules.

One research correction worth knowing. The generic 2026 consensus for a solo build is Supabase + PowerSync (offline-first sync). The advice here is to keep your own GCP Postgres for the money core — auditable server-side single-writer isolation beats offline-write convenience for ledgers, and the Feb-2026 India Supabase DNS block (~8 days) is an extra reason to own the database. Borrow the pattern (local cache + idempotency keys for offline resilience); the PWA never does optimistic offline money writes.

8 · The research behind this

Six deep web-research streams (2024–2026 practitioner sources). Verdict: your design's shape is the mainstream best practice — no source recommended a different structure.

Do (confirmed best practice)

  • Modular monolith, not microservices — benefits only appear past ~10–15 engineers; 42% of firms are consolidating back.
  • Walking skeleton, then one vertical slice — surfaces integration risk on day one.
  • Append-only ledger, balance = derived view — double-entry gives a free sum-to-zero checksum and the cleanest bridge to Tally.
  • Offline-first store capture — IndexedDB + outbox + idempotency keys; the network is an optimization, never a dependency.
  • Aggregate to a daily Tally voucher — Tally degrades past ~500–5,000 vouchers/month; never one voucher per bill.
  • AI cheapest-first — dead-stock/markdown rules → size curves → forecasting; lead with leakage detection (zero ML).
  • Chat via a semantic layer — expose 10–20 governed metrics as tools, never raw text-to-SQL.
  • Migration = its own 30–40% workstream; ≥3 mock cutovers; pilot store then waves.

Don't (how ERP builds fail)

  • Vibe-coding a production money system — passes unit tests, breaks contracts in prod. Specs + CI guard every change.
  • Letting the LLM do the math — stock counts, GST, balances will hallucinate. Reserve the model for reasoning; execute math in code.
  • Mutable ledger rows — UPDATE/DELETE destroys the audit trail; always append, correct with new entries.
  • Big-bang go-live + one-time training months early — the dominant adoption failure; shadow spreadsheets are the symptom.
  • Treating migration as the final week — ~38% of failures; expect 30–60% dirty records on first load.
  • Leading with demand forecasting — needs ~750–1,500 obs/series you won't have; do markdown/leakage first.
  • Cross-state transfer as "internal" — it's a taxable inter-GSTIN supply needing tax + e-way bill.
  • Building size curves on censored sales — ignoring stockouts systematically under-buys popular sizes.

The sobering frame: Gartner ~70% / Panorama ~68% of ERP implementations miss objectives — almost always for human reasons: change management (42%), data migration (38%), inexperienced teams (35%), weak sponsorship (31%), poor training (29%). 85% of retail-AI failures are data-quality failures. The build method here is explicitly aimed at those failure modes, not at the code.

Sources span: Fowler / Thoughtworks (monolith-first), Modern Treasury & Mettle (immutable ledger), NeurIPS 2024 + M5 (LLMs lose to LightGBM for forecasting), dbt/Cube (semantic-layer MCP), TallyPrime XML connector + CBIC circulars (GST/Tally), TechCrunch (Feb-2026 India Supabase block), Panorama/Gartner 2025 (ERP failure data), and India POS/offline-first practitioner write-ups.
