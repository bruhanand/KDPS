Payments Module

How KDPS pays its brands — under control and fully traceable — and how each store's daily money is checked against the bank.

Please confirm. Below is exactly how the system will handle paying brands and checking store money. Read each point and tell us anything that does not match how you actually work, so we can correct it before building.

1.What this module does

  • Two halves: money out — paying your brands; and money in — checking that each store's daily collection actually reached the bank.
  • It does not decide what you owe — that already comes from the goods received and the sales recorded. This module is the controlled way to pay it, and to prove the money arrived.
  • Every payment carries a name — who raised it, who approved it, who released it.

2.How a payment works

  • Two parts: the bill (what's owed — from a purchase invoice, sold SOR/Consignment stock, or a return) and the payment (the money that goes out).
  • One bank transfer can clear many bills at once — for one brand, or for several brands under the same supplier. Part-payments are tracked too.
  • At any moment the system shows what's owed and what's paid, per brand and per supplier.

3.Before anything is paid — the safety gate

  • Never paid without verified bank details and GSTIN, without the goods being officially inwarded, or for SOR stock that hasn't sold yet.
  • Smaller issues — a small amount gap, an invoice/goods mismatch, a return over the agreed limit — are flagged for a senior to release with a reason, not silently blocked.
  • Tiny rounding differences pass on their own, so nothing freezes over a few rupees.

4.Who approves

  • Approval is tiered by amount — bigger payments need more senior sign-off (you set the rupee bands and the people).
  • The person who raises a payment cannot approve it, and the approver is not the one who releases the money.
  • If an approver is away or slow, it auto-escalates — payments never get stuck.

5.How the money leaves

  • For now, the system prepares the payment (who, how much, which bills) and a person pays in the bank's portal; the bank reference is captured and matched automatically from the daily bank statement, which closes the bill.
  • It's built so it can later pay straight from the system, with no redesign — and works with any of your banks (ICICI to start).

6.Returns & credits

  • When you return goods, the debit note reduces what you owe straight away; the brand's credit note is tracked as their acknowledgement.
  • Bigger claims (end-of-season settlements, over-limit returns) wait until the brand agrees, then net at the agreed amount — so you never short-pay a brand by surprise.
  • A normal discount is not a claim — it just lowers the selling price; nothing is recovered from the brand for it.

7.The daily store-money check

  • For every store, every day, the system checks three kinds of money against what actually hit the bank:
  • Cash — deposited in lumps, minus petty cash, matched over a few days.
  • Card — settles in a couple of days, minus the bank's fee, matched by the card provider's settlement report.
  • UPI — settles next day, matched to the store.
  • It flags only what's genuinely late, short, or missing — and can tell a bank fee apart from a real shortfall.

8.The smart help (advice only)

  • Reads messy bank-statement text and matches each amount to the right store, brand or payment.
  • Warns if a brand's bank account changed since the last payment (the most common fraud), and flags duplicate payments.
  • Gives a one-line summary on each payment for the approver, answers plain questions like "how much do we owe Madura?", and sends the owner a morning WhatsApp of the money picture.
  • It only advises — a person always approves, and it never moves money on its own.

9.Tally

  • Tally stays your official book. The system sends entries into it (one way), so your accountant stops typing payments and bills twice.
  • A daily check keeps the system and Tally in agreement.
